# Consumer drivers in oral care: purchase criteria, needs, pain points and loyalty (worldwide)

Method note for the report writer: Every page fetch (WebFetch and curl) failed in this session because of DNS and proxy errors. All figures below come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from full-text reads. Paywalled syndicated data (Mintel, Kantar, NielsenIQ, Circana, Euromonitor, Statista) appeared only as public press releases or report-store blurbs, so syndicated ranked-driver tables were not obtainable. Most quantitative "driver" data comes from small, convenience-sample academic surveys in South Asia, Africa, the Middle East and Eastern Europe. Sample sizes and countries are given wherever they were visible. Weak sources are flagged inline.

## 1. Ranked purchase drivers for toothpaste

### Takeaway
No single global, representative ranking of toothpaste purchase drivers was publicly available. Across country studies, the most-cited drivers are a functional "protection" need (cavity prevention/fluoride, disease prevention), habit or previous experience, price, brand, and taste/freshness. Dentist recommendation varies widely: it is high among dental-clinic samples (for example 55% in Nigeria) and very low in general-population samples (for example 6% in one survey of about 1,000 people). Natural or herbal positioning is a major driver in India. Sustainability appears as a stated preference, not a demonstrated top driver.

### Cited Findings
- **US (Mintel, 2023):** 64% of US consumers say cavity prevention is an important factor when choosing toothpaste. Whitening and enamel strengthening are also "important", but no percentage was visible. Mintel's 2024 and 2026 US Oral Care reports describe whitening and natural toothpaste as resilient segments and "clean" ingredients as a priority. Sample size was not visible, though Mintel US typically uses about 2,000 internet users aged 18+. — [Mintel US Oral Care Market Report 2024 (store page)](https://store.mintel.com/report/us-oral-health-market-report-2024); [Mintel US Oral Care 2026](https://store.mintel.com/report/us-oral-care-market-report)
- **Pakistan (Lahore, n=410 outpatients):** One version of the paper reports cost as the biggest influence on oral hygiene product choice (82%) and advertising as influencing 55%. Reasons for use were disease prevention (58.7%) and fresh breath (31%). WEAK SOURCE: the abstract also says price was "not significant", so the paper is internally inconsistent. — [Awais et al., Makara J Health Res](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol23/iss3/3/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338242180_Factors_influencing_consumers'_choices_of_oral_hygiene_products_A_cross-sectional_study)
- **General-population survey (n=1,022; country not visible in the extract):** Only 6% use a dentist-recommended toothpaste, while 57.7% choose low-cost toothpastes. — cited in search extract alongside [Awais et al.](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol23/iss3/3/) (exact primary source unverified)
- **Nigeria (n=202 dental patients):** Toothpaste drivers were previous experience (69.3%), fluoride content (62.4%) and dentist advice (55.0%). Toothbrush drivers were texture (89.6%), previous experience (64.4%) and brand (62.9%). The clinic sample inflates dentist influence. — search extract of [cross-sectional studies listing](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol23/iss3/3/) (primary paper not opened)
- **Nigeria (Lagos, hospital dental clinic, older study):** 54% said product availability influenced brand choice and 32.5% said cost did. — cited in [Goldman et al., "Global affordability of fluoride toothpaste", Globalization and Health 2008](https://globalizationandhealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1744-8603-4-7)
- **Ghana (Kintampo North):** 39.3% chose toothpaste on cost, followed by taste (34%). WEAK SOURCE: an academia.edu paper with an unclear date. — [Kintampo study](https://www.academia.edu/98902283/THE_MAJOR_FACTORS_INFLUENCING_CONSUMER_BUYING_BEHAVIOUR_OF_TOOTHPASTE_IN_THE_KINTAMPO_NORTH_MUNICIPALITY)
- **Morocco (Settat, students):** The main factors were brand (55%), family choice (48%) and media advertisements (40%). Only 44% checked production and expiry dates. — [Makara J Health Res vol26 iss2](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol26/iss2/8/)
- **Poland (Masovian Voivodeship adults, 2025):** Price and advertising were the most common purchase determinants, cited by more than a third of respondents. A garbled table extract suggests about 32.8% for price and 28.3% for taste (needs verification). Nearly 20% did not know whether their toothpaste contained fluoride, and about 64.5% reported using fluoride toothpaste. — [Dent Med Probl 2025;62(4):569](https://dmp.umw.edu.pl/en/article/2025/62/4/569/)
- **India (Jhansi):** Consumers prioritise taste, whiteness and "medicinal" features. Doctor recommendations in advertisements did not significantly affect actual brand purchase. Demographics did not change attribute priorities. WEAK SOURCE: older academia.edu paper. — [Jhansi study](https://www.academia.edu/9145874/STUDY_OF_CONSUMER_BEHAVIOUR_WITH_PATICULAR_FOCUS_ON_FACTORS_THAT_INFLUENCE_THE_DECISION_MAKING_OF_CONSUMERS_FOR_PURCHASE_OF_TOOTHPASTE_IN_JHANSI_CITY)
- **India (2024, J Primary Care Dent Oral Health):** This is a "reason for preference of toothpaste among buyers" cross-sectional study, but its percentages were not retrievable. — [LWW](https://journals.lww.com/jpoh/fulltext/2024/05020/reason_for_preference_of_toothpaste_among_buyers__.4.aspx)
- **India herbal (Karnal, Haryana, n=482, 2022):** The pilot found about 40% had changed toothpaste in the past five years. The authors credit Patanjali's launch for the herbal shift, which Colgate and HUL followed with herbal variants. — [J Oral Health Comm Dent](https://www.johcd.net/abstractArticleContentBrowse/JOHCD/27654/JPJ/fullText)
- **India herbal (rural Ludhiana, Punjab, n=300):** 68% used herbal toothpaste. The authors linked this to TV advertising and a belief that herbal products are "chemical-free". 80% of the sample were lower-SES and most had below-secondary education. — [PMC13018336, "Herbal toothpaste craze"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13018336/)
- **India (Mintel, 2024):** 34% of "advanced users" agree natural or herbal toothpaste gives better gum health than regular toothpaste. — [Mintel search extract / Mintel India Oral Care 2024](https://store.mintel.com/report/oral-care-indian-consumer-2024)
- **China (Mintel 2026):** Nearly 60% of Chinese consumers report teeth discolouration, which drives the whitening and stain-removal segment. A 2023 CBNData survey cited by Statista found 71% of mainland respondents use oral care to prevent oral issues. Douyin's 2022 report lists whitening, fresh breath, calculus prevention and ulcers as top priorities. — [Mintel China Oral Care 2026](https://store.mintel.com/report/china-oral-care-market-report); [Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/posts/what-s-fueling-china-s-usd21b-oral-care-market)
- **Students (n=307, health-care students):** Both professional indication and brand/price perception were important factors. — search extract via [ResearchGate review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343130647_Exploring_the_Factors_Influencing_the_Choice_of_Oral_Care_Products_A_Review_on_Personalized_Approach)
- **Review:** Previous experience is "a very strong factor" in choosing both toothbrush and toothpaste. Brand, advertising, family choice, cost, design and packaging recur across countries. — [Int J Oral Dent Health review](https://www.clinmedjournals.org/articles/ijodh/international-journal-of-oral-and-dental-health-ijodh-6-109.php?jid=ijodh)
- **Dentist recommendation as a marketing claim:** Sensodyne's "#1 dentist recommended brand for sensitive teeth" is Haleon's own claim, footnoted to a proprietary survey (in Israel, a "Geocartography" survey). No published purchase-influence percentage was found. — [Haleon Health Partner](https://www.haleonhealthpartner.com/en-us/oral-health/brands/sensodyne/overview/)
- **Sustainability (stated preference):** In a GlobalData survey (date unclear), 44% of consumers prefer recyclable packaging in beauty and grooming. This was cited in coverage of Colgate's recyclable tube. GSK cited research (source unverified) that 57% are willing to change purchasing habits to reduce environmental impact. — [Waste Recycling Mag](https://wasterecyclingmag.ca/circular-economy/recyclable-toothpaste-tube-will-appeal-to-consumers); [KamCity](https://www.kamcity.com/?p=192639)

### Inferences
- In developed markets, the "protection" need (cavities, enamel, sensitivity) appears to be the baseline driver, and whitening, natural and sensitivity act as segmenting benefits on top of it. In lower-income markets, price, availability and taste rank higher in the available surveys.
- How important dentist recommendation looks depends heavily on the sample. Clinic samples (Nigeria at 55%) overstate it relative to general-population samples (6%). Dentist endorsement may still work indirectly through on-pack and advertising claims, which the Jhansi study suggests do not translate directly into purchase.
- "Habit" or "previous experience" is consistently a top-two factor where it is measured. This fits with high brand inertia (see section 3).

### Gaps
- No publicly available, representative multi-country ranked list (for example "% citing each driver") from Mintel, Kantar, NielsenIQ, Ipsos or YouGov was retrievable. They are paywalled.
- No adult toothpaste-choice percentages were found for Brazil or Saudi Arabia. The Saudi study found covers children's preferences only ([PMC13485750](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485750/)).
- Packaging and sustainability have not been measured as a ranked purchase driver against price or efficacy in any credible survey found.

## 2. Toothbrush and power brush purchase drivers

### Takeaway
For manual brushes, bristle texture/softness, previous experience and brand dominate where measured. Power brush adoption is high in the UK (roughly 59–67% of adults) and lower in the US (about 41% used one in the past six months). Smart or app features generate stated interest, but available evidence suggests usage drops off after the first months. Replacement behaviour is imperfect: about two-thirds of US adults replace their brush every three months.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigeria (n=202 dental patients):** Toothbrush choice was driven by texture (89.6%), previous experience (64.4%) and brand (62.9%). — search extract of cross-sectional literature ([see section 1](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol23/iss3/3/))
- **UK (Oral Health Foundation):** 67% of adults, an estimated 34 million people, use an electric toothbrush, and electric users have overtaken manual users. — [Oral Health Foundation, "12 million Brits move to electric toothbrushes"](https://www.dentalhealth.org/news/12-million-brits-move-to-electric-toothbrushes)
- **England (Adult Oral Health Survey 2021, official statistics):** 59% of adults who brushed used an electric toothbrush. — [GOV.UK AOHS 2021](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/adult-oral-health-survey-2021/adult-oral-health-survey-2021-report-summary)
- **US (Mintel 2022, via Consumer Reports):** 41% of adults used an electric toothbrush in the past six months, versus 58% who used a manual one. — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/health/toothbrushes/electric-toothbrush-or-manual-a3193343159/)
- **Smart toothbrushes (cross-sectional survey, n=200 public plus 200 dentists plus 200 hygienists; country per PubMed record):** 87% of the public were willing to use smart toothbrushes, but only 39% were willing to pay for professional feedback. Younger participants favoured interactive functions and older adults favoured educational content. Awareness and familiarity were low across all groups. — [PubMed 41050618](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41050618/); [PMC12495205](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12495205/)
- **Smart feature abandonment:** A review site's survey of its YouTube audience found 57% own a smart toothbrush but 69% stop using the features after three months. WEAK SOURCE: self-selected enthusiast audience. Reviewers also report they stopped checking the app. — [Electric Teeth / search extract](https://www.electricteeth.com/best-electric-toothbrush/)
- **Oral-B app data:** A global in-market evaluation used data from users of an interactive electric brush, covering timers, pressure sensors and position detection. It is an analysis of app data from engaged users, not a consumer survey. — [PMC11640869](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11640869/)
- **Colgate hum:** Colgate states hum was voted "#1 electric toothbrush" (2021) in a Kantar survey of 40,000 consumers. This is a company-sourced claim. — [Colgate-Palmolive](https://www.colgatepalmolive.com/en-us/news/smartest-toothbrush)
- **Unverified aggregator claim:** "Smart connectivity features accounted for 37% of consumer purchase preferences in 2025." No underlying survey is named, so do not rely on it. — [Tricity Local News stats page](https://tricitylocalnews.com/stats/electric-toothbrush-statistics/)
- **Replacement (US, Delta Dental):** 64% of adults replaced their toothbrush at least every three months (2023 report, up 10% vs 2022). The figure was 65% in the 2020 report. — [Delta Dental 2023](https://www.deltadental.com/about-us/press-center/2023-press-releases/delta-dental-report-finds-adults-practice-good-oral-health-care-to-avoid-serious-dental-issues-and-major-expenses/); [Delta Dental 2021](https://www.deltadental.com/about-us/press-center/2021-press-releases/at-home-oral-health-practices-remain-strong/)
- **Ireland:** 69% of shoppers replace their toothbrush every 2–3 months. — [Checkout.ie](https://www.checkout.ie/retail-intelligence/69-of-irish-shoppers-replace-their-toothbrush-every-2-or-three-months-11480)
- **Sustainable brushes (Ireland, n=326 online):** Respondents valued a bamboo handle at more than €5 above a regular one and would pay more than €2 extra for recyclable packaging. — [CDA Essentials 2021](https://cda-adc.ca/en/services/essentials/2021/issue5/25/)
- **Brazil (n=120 patients):** None used bamboo brushes and about 80% did not know they existed. 67.5% discard brushes immediately, with 98.3% going to regular trash. — [RSD Journal](https://rsdjournal.org/rsd/article/view/47777)
- **UK paste volume:** Electric brush use (which needs less paste) and multi-buy promotions were cited as factors in UK toothpaste value decline. This is dated trade press. — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/capping-the-decline-in-the-toothpaste-market/)

### Inferences
- In markets with high power-brush adoption such as the UK, the handle is a durable purchase and the refill-head cost becomes the recurring spend. No survey found measures head-cost sensitivity directly.
- Stated willingness to use smart features is high, but measured or anecdotal sustained use is low. Smart features may work more as a purchase-time differentiator than as an ongoing engagement driver. The evidence is weak.

### Gaps
- No credible survey ranks power brush purchase drivers (price, battery life, dentist advice, design, head cost). Battery and design drivers were not quantified.
- No independent data on the share of smart-brush owners actively using apps after 3, 6 or 12 months.

## 3. Loyalty vs switching: brand loyalty, promotion sensitivity, private label

### Takeaway
Toothpaste is a high-penetration, habit-driven category dominated by Colgate. Kantar found Colgate bought by about 60% of global households in its 2019 Brand Footprint. The category is also heavily promoted: historically about 37–40%+ of US and UK toothpaste was sold on deal. Private label is strong across European FMCG overall, but reliable toothpaste-specific private label shares were not publicly found.

### Cited Findings
- **Kantar Worldpanel Brand Footprint (2019):** Colgate was the only brand present in more than half of global households (60.5%) and ranked as the most chosen personal care brand. About 65% was reported in 2013. — [Kantar Worldpanel](https://kantarworldpanel.com/global/News/Colgate-bought-by-over-half-of-the-worlds-households)
- **Brand Footprint 2025 methodology:** Covers the 52 weeks to end-October 2024. Colgate's 2025 figures were not visible. — [Kantar Brand Footprint 2025 GB](https://kantar.turtl.co/story/brand-footprint-2025-gb/page/4)
- **UK promotions (dated, about 2012–2014):** Over 40% of toothpaste volume was sold on in-store promotion. Price cuts and 2-for-1/3-for-2 offers removed about £20m in value over four years. — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/capping-the-decline-in-the-toothpaste-market/); [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/freshening-a-stagnant-sector/)
- **US promotions (2008):** Promotional sales averaged 37.2% of toothpaste retail sales. Colgate alone was "very heavy" at 47.9%. — [The U.S. Toothpaste Market: A Competitive Profile](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339538350_The_US_Toothpaste_Market_A_Competitive_Profile)
- **Europe private label (FMCG-wide, March 2025):** Private label holds about 40% of FMCG value and about 62% of volume across Europe's six largest markets. Spain's penetration is 48%. Younger consumers in Italy and Spain show less brand loyalty. This is a grocery proxy, not oral care. — [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/03/25/is-this-the-end-of-brand-loyalty/)
- **Germany oral care private label:** Over 40% of German oral care volume is private label (Aldi, Lidl). WEAK SOURCE: unsourced market-report claim. — [Market Data Forecast, Europe Toothpaste](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/europe-toothpaste-market)
- **Switching in India:** About 40% changed toothpaste within five years (Karnal pilot). One market-report-cited survey says 49.79% moved from non-herbal to herbal (WEAK SOURCE). — [JOHCD](https://www.johcd.net/abstractArticleContentBrowse/JOHCD/27654/JPJ/fullText); [Mordor Intelligence Ayurvedic toothpaste](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/ayurvedic-toothpaste-market)
- **Specialist brand trust:** Market reports describe sensitivity and whitening specialist brands (for example Sensodyne) as winning on trust. Marvis reportedly grew more than 30% in 2024. WEAK SOURCE: market-report commentary. — [Market Data Forecast](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/europe-toothpaste-market)

### Inferences
- High penetration together with high promotional share points to a "habitual but deal-responsive" pattern. Shoppers tend to stay within a repertoire of familiar brands and switch among them on promotion, rather than showing strict single-brand loyalty. This is an inference; no repeat-rate or duplication data was found.
- Benefit-led specialist segments (sensitivity, herbal in India) appear to create stickier loyalty than generic cavity protection, because the benefit is tied to a felt symptom or belief.

### Gaps
- No current (2022–2026) promotion share, repeat-purchase rate, or brand-loyalty percentage for toothpaste from NielsenIQ, Circana or Kantar was publicly available.
- No reliable private label value or volume share for toothpaste in Germany, the UK or France. PLMA, NielsenIQ or Circana data would be needed.
- No data on consumer awareness or use of price-per-use or unit pricing in oral care.

## 4. Unmet needs and pain points

### Takeaway
Common felt problems are widespread. Self-reported dentine sensitivity runs at 32–54% depending on method, halitosis at about 32%, and dry mouth at about 22% of the general population. Children's brushing compliance is a major parental pain point, though the data is mostly brand-commissioned. Fluoride avoidance is a minority behaviour (about 5–15%), but fluoride knowledge gaps are large: about 20% do not know whether their paste contains fluoride. Information overload and misinformation, especially among younger adults, add to mistrust.

### Cited Findings
- **Sensitivity (Haleon-funded, Europe, 2023):** Over half of more than 3,500 adults in seven European countries had dentine hypersensitivity. Haleon pages elsewhere cite 41% of adults, peaking at ages 30–40. — [Haleon press release 2023](https://www.haleon.com/news/press-releases/brand/2023/new-study-reveals-now-1-in-2-adults-are-sufferung-the-pain-of-tooth-sensitivity); [Haleon Health Partner](https://www.haleonhealthpartner.com/en-us/oral-health/conditions/sensitivity/overview/)
- **Sensitivity (independent research-on-research, 39 publications, 1998–2022):** Average prevalence was 32%, with very high heterogeneity. Reviews cite a range of 3–98%. — [PubMed 40462051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40462051/)
- **Sensitivity (Saudi Arabia, n=748 online, 2025):** Self-reported prevalence was 54.3%. — [PMC12384756](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12384756/)
- **Halitosis (systematic review and meta-regression, 13 population studies, 2017):** Pooled prevalence was 31.8% (95% CI 24.6–39.0%). A secondary summary gives about 40% in low- and middle-income countries versus 29% in high-income countries. — [PubMed 28676903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28676903/); [National Elf Service](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/dentistry/oral-health-other-health-conditions/bad-breath-how-common-is-it/)
- **Dry mouth (systematic review, 29 population studies, to Feb 2018):** 22.0% (95% CI 17–26%), higher in older adults. — [Braz Dent J, SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/bdj/a/zyjgvkJkWxkVXRs6wvrwGXg/)
- **Dry mouth among tobacco and vape users (meta-analysis, 14 studies, n=6,827):** 26% overall, 33% among e-cigarette users. — [PMC9909684](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9909684/)
- **Gum bleeding by gender (Japan, about 9,100 university freshmen):** Male sex was an independent risk factor for gingival bleeding (OR 1.29). — [PMC7345042](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7345042/)
- **Kids not brushing (Pepsodent/Unilever-commissioned, 5,000 parents and children, 8 countries, 2020):** 30% of children do not brush twice a day and 1 in 4 do not brush every night. 39% of parents sometimes let them go to bed without brushing, and nearly 4 in 10 let children skip brushing as a reward. BRAND-COMMISSIONED. — [Citi Newsroom](https://www.citinewsroom.com/2020/03/new-research-highlights-risks-of-not-brushing-twice-a-day/)
- **Kids brushing stress (Oral-B UK parents, 2019):** 70% say ensuring children brush is the most stressful part of parenting and 70% say it causes tantrums. Parents spend on average 9 minutes asking. BRAND-COMMISSIONED. — [Dental Nursing](https://www.dental-nursing.co.uk/content/news/kids-toothbrushing-big-challenge-for-parents-reveals-survey); [Dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/2019/11/04/children-skip-brushing-teeth-20-occasions/)
- **UK children (bedtime):** A third of kids in Britain do not brush at bedtime. — [DrBicuspid](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-hygiene/article/15375624/third-of-kids-in-britain-dont-brush-at-bedtime)
- **Fluoride avoidance (Latvia and Lithuania, nationally representative households, 2019–2020; published 2025):** 15% of families used non-fluoridated toothpaste and 12% used under 1,000 ppm. — [Maldupa et al., Int J Dent Hyg 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/idh.12827)
- **Fluoride perception (n=1,129):** About one-fifth were unsure whether their toothpaste contained fluoride and 12.3% used non-fluoridated paste. 77% expressed no concern about fluoride. — [PMC11417910](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11417910/)
- **Fluoride-free use among children (n of about 6,069; Japan per search context):** Non-fluoride users were 5.1% of toothpaste users. Their parents cited anti-gingivitis, halitosis or tartar rather than fluoride, price or taste. — [PMC3893514](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3893514/)
- **Reasons for fluoride-free choice (qualitative review):** People consider fluoride harmful or unnecessary, prefer natural or organic products, and avoid additives. — [PubMed 41190576](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41190576/)
- **Mistrust or confusion online (AAE, US, n=1,019, Feb 2025, Atomik Research):** 40% of Gen Z and 36% of millennials often or always struggle to tell dental myths from facts online. 48% of Gen Z fear root canals vs 38% of all adults. — [Oral Health Group](https://www.oralhealthgroup.com/clinical/dental-research/online-dental-misinformation-induces-fear-in-gen-z-and-millennials-u-s-endodontics-survey-finds-1003987184/)
- **Smile anxiety (UK, Oral Health Foundation):** 51% of British adults are often made to feel anxious about how their smile looks. 31% say TV and social media make them feel inadequate about their smile. — [search extract of OHF coverage](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/beauty-and-personal-care/given-the-brush-off-nearly-3-in-10-british-millennials-put-off-visiting-the-dentist-over-the-past-year) (primary OHF page not retrieved)
- **Label and information gaps (Morocco):** Incomplete labelling on toothpaste used by 58% of respondents. — [Makara J Health Res](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol26/iss2/8/)
- **Discolouration (China, Mintel 2026):** Nearly 60% of consumers are affected. — [Mintel China](https://store.mintel.com/report/china-oral-care-market-report)

### Inferences
- Sensitivity, bad breath and dry mouth each affect roughly a fifth to a half of adults. These are large symptom-based needs that support benefit-specific products.
- Parent-child brushing compliance is a persistent pain point. Because nearly all quantification comes from brand-commissioned studies, it may be overstated.
- Fluoride mistrust is a vocal minority (about 5–15% using non-fluoride paste). The larger issue is low fluoride literacy (about 20% unsure), which may make consumers receptive to "free-from" claims.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found on aligner wearers' oral care needs, travel or portability pain points, mess, or tube-waste frustration.
- No credible survey measuring consumer mistrust of oral care marketing claims in general.

## 5. Differences by segment (age, gender, income, emerging vs developed, parents)

### Takeaway
Women consistently show better hygiene behaviour than men (more brushing, less gum bleeding), and Indian men are notably more likely to brush only once a day. Younger adults are the most whitening- and aesthetics-oriented and the most exposed to online misinformation. Emerging markets show lower twice-daily brushing, more price and availability sensitivity, and strong herbal or natural trust in India, especially among rural and lower-SES consumers.

### Cited Findings
- **India by gender and age (Mintel, 2024):** 59% of Indians brush once a day and 34% at least twice. Among men, 62% aged 18–34 and 68% aged 35–44 brush once daily, versus 55% and 52% of women of the same ages. Mintel suggests whitening and stain-removal benefits to engage men. Mintel's India report elsewhere cites about 25% brushing twice daily, a conflicting figure. — [Mintel press release](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/6-in-10-indians-use-toothpaste-once-daily-untapped-oral-care-innovations/); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/mintel-research-6-in-10-indians-brush-their-teeth-just-once-a-day--highlighting-untapped-innovations-in-oral-care-302180146.html); [Mintel India Oral Care report](https://store.mintel.com/report/india-oral-care-market-report)
- **India (Colgate executive, 2023 interview):** "80 percent of urban Indians brush just once a day." Methodology not given; WEAK SOURCE. — [Storyboard18](https://cms.storyboard18.com/?p=16326)
- **Gender (Greek dental students, meta-analysis 1981/2000/2010):** Females brushed significantly more often (OR 1.95). — [PMC4784066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4784066)
- **Gender (Japan, about 9,100 freshmen):** Males were less likely to brush three or more times a day, brushed for shorter durations, and had more gum bleeding (OR 1.29). — [PMC7345042](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7345042/)
- **Gender (Mexican-origin young adults, California):** Women brushed 1.24 times as often weekly. There was no difference in flossing or meeting ADA guidelines. — [PMC11887658](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11887658/)
- **Gender attitude (Delta Dental US, 2016):** Oral health matters more to women than men. Three-quarters of women consider good oral health one of the sexiest qualities in a partner. — [Delta Dental](https://Www.deltadental.com/us/en/about-us/press-center/2016/newsreleaseguysshescheckingoutyouroral201606.html)
- **Parents' gender (Turkey, parents of children aged 4–9, 2023):** Mothers were significantly more likely than fathers to consider "chemical content" when selecting children's toothpaste. — [ACAM](https://acamedicine.org/article/10-4328-acam-22907/)
- **Parents follow child preference (Ukraine, conference abstract):** All parents try to follow children's wishes when choosing toothpaste, and unpleasant taste may shorten brushing. WEAK SOURCE. — [KNMU repository](https://repo.knmu.edu.ua/bitstreams/ac55c7c7-2b7c-4dd0-be8c-d3c9b18bb43e/download)
- **Children (Saudi Arabia, n=500, aged 5–12):** Study of children's preferences for toothbrush and toothpaste colour, flavour and design. Percentages not visible. — [PMC13485750](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485750/)
- **Young adults and whitening (UK, Oral Health Foundation, about 2020):** 42% of under-35s have had tooth whitening and 48% of young adults plan to whiten within the next year. — [Dentistry.co.uk](https://www.dentistry.co.uk/2020/06/09/millennials-spend-national-average-treatment/); [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/3604-millennials-spending-more-than-three-times-the-national-average-on-their-smiles)
- **Gen Z and misinformation (US AAE, n=1,019, 2025):** 40% of Gen Z struggle to separate myth from fact online. — [Dentistry Today](https://www.dentistrytoday.com/gen-z-millennials-most-vulnerable-to-dental-misinformation/)
- **Gen Z whitening study (n=33, mostly women, mean age 23):** Nearly all reported confidence and mood boosts. WEAK SOURCE: tiny sample. — search extract via [drjohnpatterson.com](https://drjohnpatterson.com/teeth-whitening-statistics/)
- **Smart brush features by age:** Younger people favour interactive functions and older adults favour educational content. — [PubMed 41050618](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41050618/)
- **Income and rural markets (India, rural Ludhiana):** 68% herbal toothpaste use in a mostly lower-SES rural sample. — [PMC13018336](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13018336/)
- **Affordability (global):** Fluoride toothpaste is unaffordable for many in low-income countries, and cost and availability shape brand choice in Nigeria. — [Goldman et al. 2008](https://globalizationandhealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1744-8603-4-7)
- **Europe youth loyalty (FMCG-wide):** Younger consumers in Italy and Spain are less brand-loyal. — [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/03/25/is-this-the-end-of-brand-loyalty/)

### Inferences
- Gender differences seem to be more about engagement and frequency (women more engaged) than about distinct product drivers. Mintel's suggestion of aesthetic (whitening) messaging for Indian men is a hypothesis, not a measured preference.
- Age differences centre on aesthetics and digital information sources. Younger cohorts show higher whitening uptake and more exposure to misinformation, while older adults lean towards education and professional advice.

### Gaps
- No robust Gen Z vs boomer purchase-driver comparison (for example % citing whitening by generation) was found from a syndicated source.
- No quantitative data on gender differences in whitening or power-brush purchase.
- Income-band breakdowns of toothpaste drivers in developed markets were not found.

## 6. Behavioural science of brushing (habit, sensory cues, fear vs aesthetics)

### Takeaway
Brushing is a cue-dependent habit. Qualitative work shows morning brushing is triggered by visual or routine cues and night brushing by internal "feelings or urges". Rewards increase automaticity. The Pepsodent "tingle" story, a sensory cue (foam, mint, tingle) becoming the signal of "clean", is a widely repeated popular narrative from Duhigg's *The Power of Habit*, not peer-reviewed research. In messaging, aesthetics and social anxiety (smile insecurity) are prominent, alongside fear-based health framing.

### Cited Findings
- **Pepsodent narrative (Duhigg, *The Power of Habit*, 2012):** Claude Hopkins built a brushing habit via a cue ("tooth film") and reward. Ingredients such as citric acid and mint oil created a tingle that consumers came to crave as proof of cleanliness. POPULAR NARRATIVE, not empirical study. — [Slate excerpt](https://slate.com/culture/2012/02/an-excerpt-from-charles-duhiggs-the-power-of-habit.html); [Next Big Idea Club](https://nextbigideaclub.com/magazine/charles-duhigg-the-minty-fresh-psychology-behind-americas-toothpaste-obsession/1196/)
- **Adult brushing cues (Raison, Corcoran and Harris, 2021; interviews, n=29, UK):** Morning brushing is strongly integrated into cleansing routines and mainly initiated by visual cues. Night-time brushing is more often driven by internal cues (strong feelings or urges). — [Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3117346/)
- **Habit formation experiment (Judah thesis, LSHTM, n=118, 16 weeks):** Reward led to greater automaticity, not mediated by frequency. Attraction or health messages and floss-before-vs-after-brushing placement had no effect. A separate LSHTM pilot (n=50) found flossing after brushing produced stronger habits, a conflicting result. — [LSHTM thesis PDF](https://lshtm-eprints-repo-prod.lshtm-eprints.cdl.cosector.com/2121556/1/2015_ITD_PhD_Judah_G_NEW.pdf); [LSHTM Research Online](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/275190/)
- **Habit definition and interventions:** Habits are cue-activated automatic responses (Lally and Gardner 2013). Aunger (2007) argues brushing should be routinised. Habit-based interventions boost brushing (Pakpour et al. 2016). These are cited secondhand. — [University of Jyväskylä paper](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstream/123456789/83862/2/FINAL_Tooth.pdf)
- **NHS-registered research on brushing automaticity:** A cross-sectional survey of oral health behaviour automaticity is listed. — [HRA summary](https://www.hra.nhs.uk/planning-and-improving-research/application-summaries/research-summaries/cross-sectional-survey-to-investigate-automaticity-of-oh-behaviours/)
- **Taste as a behavioural lever:** Unpleasant taste may shorten children's brushing duration. — [KNMU abstract](https://repo.knmu.edu.ua/bitstreams/ac55c7c7-2b7c-4dd0-be8c-d3c9b18bb43e/download)
- **Aesthetic and social anxiety:** 51% of UK adults feel anxious about their smile and 31% blame TV and social media. — see section 4 (OHF, secondary extract)
- **Fear:** Gen Z (48%) and millennials (46%) report root-canal fear vs 38% of all adults, linked to online misinformation (AAE 2025). — [Oral Health Group](https://www.oralhealthgroup.com/clinical/dental-research/online-dental-misinformation-induces-fear-in-gen-z-and-millennials-u-s-endodontics-survey-finds-1003987184/)
- **Prevention motive (US, Delta Dental 2023):** Adults practise at-home care to avoid serious dental issues and major expenses. — [Delta Dental 2023](https://www.deltadental.com/about-us/press-center/2023-press-releases/delta-dental-report-finds-adults-practice-good-oral-health-care-to-avoid-serious-dental-issues-and-major-expenses/)

### Inferences
- Morning brushing is anchored to routine, but night brushing depends on felt urges. Night brushing is therefore the more fragile habit, which fits with the children's bedtime-skipping data in section 4.
- Sensory signals (mint, foam, tingle) plausibly act as perceived-efficacy cues. Empirical support for this in oral care rests mainly on the Duhigg narrative and general habit theory, not on controlled oral care trials found here.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found that tests whether foam, mint or tingle intensity changes perceived cleanliness or brushing frequency.
- No empirical comparison found of fear-based vs aesthetic messaging effectiveness in oral care advertising.

## 7. How consumers discover and choose (shelf, online, reviews, TikTok, dentist, pharmacy)

### Takeaway
Store shelves (supermarkets and hypermarkets) remain the dominant channel in the West. China is the outlier, with e-commerce at an estimated 40–60% of oral care value. Advertising and family influence are major choice drivers in emerging-market surveys. Social media drives whitening interest and misinformation among younger consumers. Dentist influence is strong in clinical samples but weak in general-population samples.

### Cited Findings
- **China online share:** Estimated at 50–60% of oral care retail value, up from about 30% in 2019 (Daxue Consulting). A separate estimate puts online marketplaces (Tmall, Taobao, JD, Pinduoduo) at 40–45% of value in 2025. The estimates conflict because definitions differ. MARKET-RESEARCH ESTIMATES. — [Daxue Consulting](https://daxueconsulting.com/oral-care-market-in-china/); [Mordor Intelligence China](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/china-oral-care-market)
- **China online toothpaste brand shares (Jan–Aug 2023, China Oral Care Industry Association):** Yunnan Baiyao 11.2%, Darlie 7.8%, Crest 6.5%, Colgate 6.0%, Saky 5.3%. — [ChemLinked](https://market.chemlinked.com/report/keeping-pace-with-the-changing-landscape-and-trends-in-chinas-toothpaste-market)
- **Global and US channel (market-research estimates):** Hypermarkets and supermarkets held about 42.4% of global oral care sales (2025) and about 33.4% of US oral care products (2024). Online is the fastest-growing channel (US CAGR about 7.3%, 2025–2030), but no current online share was stated. Mordor claims e-commerce penetration above 25% in North America and Europe (2025). WEAK: methods unclear. — [Mordor Intelligence global](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/global-oral-care-products-market-industry); [Grand View Research US](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-oral-care-products-market-report)
- **Amazon (NielsenIQ, beauty and personal care, 52 weeks to late 2024):** Amazon held about 20% of US beauty and personal care dollar share, with growth of 17.8%. Oral care is not broken out. — [NIQ ECRM presentation, Jan 2025](https://ecrm.marketgate.com/MarketingFiles/Media/20250124/NIQ_Jan_2025_ECRM_Beauty_Presentation.pdf)
- **Advertising and family as influence:** Morocco brand 55%, family 48%, media ads 40%. Pakistan ads 55%. Chennai, India study on media influence on oral hygiene product choice (abstract only). — [Makara J Health Res](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol26/iss2/8/); [Awais et al.](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol23/iss3/3/); [PubMed 26557606](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26557606/)
- **India herbal adoption via TV advertising:** Rural Ludhiana study. — [PMC13018336](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13018336/)
- **Social media:** Douyin's 2022 report frames Chinese oral care priorities. TikTok whitening "hacks" and charcoal trends appear in trend commentary (WEAK: AI-generated trend pages with unsourced stats). — [Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/posts/what-s-fueling-china-s-usd21b-oral-care-market); [Accio TikTok trend page (weak)](https://www.accio.com/business/toothpaste_tiktok_trend)
- **Dentist influence:** 55% (Nigeria clinic sample) vs 6% (general-population sample of 1,022). — see section 1

### Inferences
- Channel structure varies by market. China is online-first and social-commerce heavy, while US and EU shoppers still mostly choose at the shelf, which makes in-store promotion and on-pack claims central (consistent with the promotion data in section 3).

### Gaps
- No reliable 2023–2026 online share of oral care for the US, UK, Germany, India or Brazil from NielsenIQ, Circana or Kantar was publicly found.
- No survey quantifying the share of consumers who discovered or bought an oral care product via TikTok or other social media, or who use reviews, was found.
- No data on pharmacist advice as a purchase driver for toothpaste.
