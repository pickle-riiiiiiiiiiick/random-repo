## Re-check of key figures in the earlier report ("Global toothpaste formats innovation")

### Takeaway
Most of the earlier numbers hold up, but three need correcting or qualifying. Colgate's **40.9%** was a Q1 2025 year-to-date figure; the **full-year 2025 share is 41.3%**. Germany's "almost EUR 2bn" was 2024 total oral care; IKW now reports **EUR 2.113bn for 2025 (+6.5%)**. The tablet range of "USD 67–120m" mixes current estimates with a 2032 forecast; current estimates are about **USD 67–87m**. Haleon's **+7.9% organic** growth is confirmed. The US and UK toothpaste sizes and the fluoride-free market size remain vendor-only and unverified.

### Inferences
- **Triangulating global toothpaste size from Colgate's share.** Colgate's record FY2025 net sales were USD 20.4bn, of which Oral, Personal & Home Care was USD 15.77bn. If oral care is roughly 40%+ of Colgate's sales (historically about 42%; not verified this session) and toothpaste is the bulk of it, toothpaste sales at manufacturer prices would be roughly USD 6–7bn. At a 41.3% retail value share, plus a typical retail margin, that implies a measured-country retail toothpaste market of roughly **USD 18–25bn**. That is closer to SkyQuest's USD 18.5bn than to WiseGuy's USD 44bn. This is an inference resting on unverified assumptions, and it covers only the countries where Colgate buys panel data.
- Colgate is losing toothpaste share in North America and Latin America, its largest regions, while Haleon (Sensodyne/parodontax) grows at about 2.5x the group rate. Together these point to a premium/therapeutic mix shift inside a flat-volume category.

## Global market size and segment split (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Vendor estimates of global oral care in 2025 run from **USD 34.8bn to USD 68.9bn**, so no single "true" size exists. Most vendors put it around **USD 40bn** and agree on the ranking: toothpaste is the largest segment, then toothbrushes (manual larger than power by value in most splits), then mouthwash, with floss/interdental, water flossers and whitening as smaller pools. On growth, the vendors consistently rank power brushes, water flossers, floss and mouthwash (~5.5–7.5% CAGR) above toothpaste and manual brushes (~2.5–3.5%).

## Regional split and per-capita spend

### Takeaway
Hard regional data are thin. North America is consistently the largest region for devices: about 34–37% of power brushes, water flossers and fluoride-free paste. Asia-Pacific is the largest region for volume (China, India), and China is about 42% of Asia-Pacific power-brush value. Germany is the best-documented single market: EUR 2.1bn of oral care in 2025, about EUR 25 per person. China's toothpaste market of about CNY 37bn works out to about CNY 26 (about USD 3.6) per person, a sevenfold gap to Western Europe.

Toothpaste is a near-duopoly-plus-one at the global level: Colgate holds about 41% of value. P&G (Crest/Oral-B) and Haleon (Sensodyne/parodontax) are the next tier, but neither discloses a global toothpaste share. Local champions lead in specific markets: Lion in Japan, Yunnan Baiyao in China, and Dabur/Patanjali for herbal paste in India. Power brushes are a P&G (Oral-B) and Philips (Sonicare) duopoly, with Chinese brands (usmile, Xiaomi) taking the mid-price tier online. The exact power-brush shares come only from low-quality vendors.

## Consumer behaviour and oral disease burden

### Takeaway
Twice-daily brushing is the norm in rich countries (about 70–80% of adults in England, the UK and Japan; about 50–60% in US self-reports) but not in MENA or rural India. Daily flossing is a minority habit in the US (about 1 in 3). Interdental-aid use is higher in Japan (about 59–65% in prefectural surveys). Power-brush use is roughly half of adults in England, the UK and Germany, but far lower in Asia outside Japan and Korea. People replace brushes less often than the 3-month guidance. Against this, WHO counts **3.5bn people (45%) with oral disease**.

### Cited Findings
- **WHO Global Oral Health Status Report (Nov 2022, GBD 2019 data):**
  - About **3.5bn people (45% of the population)** have oral diseases; 3 in 4 of them live in low- and middle-income countries.
  - About **2bn** have caries of permanent teeth and **514m** children have caries of primary teeth.
  - Severe periodontal disease affects about **1bn**.
  - Edentulism (no natural teeth) is about **340–370m** depending on the summary (uncertain).
  - Untreated caries in permanent teeth is cited as about **2.5bn** in some coverage. That differs from the 2bn figure; the 2bn is the WHO permanent-teeth caries figure.
  - The WHO European Region has the highest prevalence.
  - Sources: [ICIR Nigeria](https://www.icirnigeria.org/who-nearly-3-5bn-people-suffer-from-oral-diseases/); [Dental Products Report](https://dentalproductsreport.com/view/world-health-organization-release-first-global-oral-health-assessment); [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/4403-who-report-reveals-2-5-billion-people-have-untreated-caries); [Straumann summary](https://www.straumann.com/content/dam/media-center/foundation/en/documents/Global-Oral-Health-Insights-00-en.pdf); [WHO Europe](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/20-04-2023-who-europe-calls-for-urgent-action-on-oral-disease-as-highest-rates-globally-are-recorded-in-european-region)

    - 90% of children brush daily, but only **71%** brush twice a day.

    - By country income: low-GDP countries 59.8%, middle-income 78.6%, wealthiest 69.9%.
    - In Egypt (19.3%), Yemen (17.8%) and Morocco (15.1%), more than 10% of children rarely or never brush.
    - Source: [Dental and Medical Problems 2024](https://dmp.umw.edu.pl/en/article/2024/61/4/495/); [Wroclaw Medical University](https://wroclaw-medical-university.shorthandstories.com/brush-up-on-brushing/)

### Inferences
- The biggest behavioural headroom (twice-daily brushing, first toothpaste or brush) is in MENA, rural South Asia and low-income countries. In rich markets the headroom is in adjuncts (interdental, power brushes) and in replacement frequency rather than in brushing incidence.
- Power-brush adoption roughly tracks income: about half of adults in England, the UK and Germany, versus low penetration in most of Asia outside Japan and Korea. The vendor penetration figures are too inconsistent to rank countries reliably.

- MHLW Japan 2022 Survey of Dental Diseases national figures could not be retrieved (DNS failure); prefectural data were used instead.

- No Korea or India national brushing surveys were found; the ADA/HPI US flossing and dentist-visit figures beyond NHANES were not found.
- No reliable mouthwash-usage rates were found outside low-quality US secondary sites.
- No credible power-brush penetration figures by country were found from GfK, Euromonitor or national statistics.

## Price tiers (mass vs premium vs therapeutic)

### Takeaway
No panel source quantifies a global split between mass, premium and therapeutic toothpaste. Proxies suggest that therapeutic/sensitivity positioning takes a large share of value in developed and Chinese markets (about 43–45% in UK sensitivity and China "therapeutic", both vendor figures). Incumbent value growth is coming from pricing and premium mix while volumes are flat or falling.

### Cited Findings
- UK: sensitivity products are about **44.8%** of toothpaste value in 2025 (vendor). — [MDF UK](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/united-kingdom-toothpaste-market); [MRFR UK](https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/reports/uk-toothpaste-market-46324)

  - Colgate OPHC +1.0% on pricing with volume declines (2025). — [Colgate 2025 AR](https://investor.colgatepalmolive.com/static-files/4298e292-4aaf-4b10-8953-aad27c1065d7)
