## 1. DTC and subscription models: what worked, unit economics, churn, and the shift back to retail

### Takeaway
No pure-DTC oral-care subscription brand reached scale on DTC alone. The brands that grew (quip, Hello, Spotlight, SURI, Happybrush, Hismile) used DTC or subscription to acquire customers and then moved quickly into mass or beauty retail. Some used a hybrid "starter kit in store, refills online" design to keep the subscription relationship. No source discloses churn or CAC for any oral-care brand. The only reported subscription base is 900.care's (~235k active subscriptions in 2023). The two clean exits in the space (Hello for $351M, TheraBreath for $580M) were retail-velocity brands, not subscription brands.

### Cited Findings
**quip (US)**
- At its 2018 Target launch, quip sold $25 starter kits in all ~1,800 Target stores and on Target.com, but refills were available only on quip's own website. Commentators described this as using Target for acquisition while quip kept the customer relationship. quip called it "one of the most successful direct-to-customer launches" Target had seen **[CR]** — [Modern Retail 2020](https://www.modernretail.co/startups/why-subscription-toothbrush-brand-quip-is-expanding-into-walmart/); [BeautyMatter](https://www.beautymatter.com/articles/target-and-quip-are-shifting-the-retail-pardigm); [Fortune 2018](https://fortune.com/2018/11/27/quip-toothbrush-delivery-target-stores)
- In Oct 2020, quip launched a full oral-care range in ~3,100 Walmart stores and on Walmart.com. It said store sales were not cannibalising subscriptions and that most customers still used automatic refills **[CR]**. Refill heads were "considerably cheaper" through DTC than in store, a deliberate price nudge toward subscription. An Ordergroove executive put it this way: the customer discovers the brand at Walmart, "but the relationship continues via the subscription channel" — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/startups/why-subscription-toothbrush-brand-quip-is-expanding-into-walmart/); [Business Wire 2020](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201020005255/en/quip-Builds-on-Retail-Success-by-Launching-Comprehensive-Oral-Care-Product-Range-Nationwide-at-Worlds-Largest-Retailer-Walmart); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/357193/quip-expands-with-move-to-walmart-more-products.html)

### Inferences
- The "hybrid subscription" design (device in store, consumables via DTC at a lower price) is the most distinctive business-model innovation in brushes. It is documented only for quip. Whether it held up as retail share grew is unknown.
- The pattern "DTC proves demand, retail delivers scale" recurs in every case. Retail entry generally came within 2–4 years of launch: quip launched DTC in 2015 and reached Target in 2018; SURI launched in 2022 and was in Boots by 2023–24; Moon launched DTC and reached Ulta in 2019.
- The decline from quip's $100M Series B (2021) to a ~$15.5M C2 (2024) fits the wider post-2021 DTC funding contraction. Valuation is not disclosed.

### Gaps
- No reported churn, retention, CAC, LTV or contribution margin for any oral-care subscription brand (quip, Bite, 900.care, SURI, Happybrush).
- quip's revenue, subscriber count and current DTC/retail split are not disclosed. No 2025–26 independent reporting on quip was found.
- Hello's revenue at acquisition was not found. The 44,000-door and 15%-online figures come from snippets of pages I could not open.

## 2. Social, influencer and creator commerce (Hismile, Moon, Twice, TikTok Shop, Douyin/Tmall, K-beauty)

### Takeaway
Paid social and creators built the largest challenger by revenue (Hismile, >US$150m). They also turned whitening strips and purple "colour-corrector" products into best-sellers on TikTok Shop in the US, UK and EU in 2025–26. Pulling back paid social cut Hismile's revenue but raised its profit, and in June 2026 it was fined in Australia over staged "random shopper" videos. In China, Douyin-native brands (参半 Banbu mouthwash, >¥2bn in 2024; usmile electric brushes, #1 online) grew to category leadership through KOL/KOC seeding plus paid traffic. Celebrity-led US brands (Moon, Twice) produced launch spikes, but I found no later sales data for them.

## 3. "Skinification" and beauty positioning (Sephora, Ulta, Cult Beauty, Space NK; CO. by Colgate; Marvis)

### Takeaway
Oral care was repositioned as "oral beauty" from about 2019. Moon at Ulta, Marvis at Sephora.com, Neiman Marcus and Revolve, Cocofloss/Cocolab and Spotlight at Ulta and Boots led the move, and Colgate followed with an Ulta-exclusive Gen-Z sub-brand (CO. by Colgate, March 2021). Packaging (iridescent, metal tubes, colour-blocked tubes) and flavour do most of the work. Ulta is expanding oral care inside its 2025 wellness push, partly to capture shoppers displaced by US drugstore closures. No sales sizing for oral care in beauty retail was found.

### Cited Findings
- **CO. by Colgate** launched in March 2021, exclusively at Ulta Beauty, Ulta.com and CO.Colgate.com, "bypassing traditional FDM channels." The 7-SKU range included Oh So Dazzling enzyme whitening toothpaste, the TLC manual brush, The CO. Worker electric brush and "Later, Stains!" foaming rinse, in iridescent packaging. It was positioned as self-care and self-expression for visual-first Gen Z, with a pledge to donate a share of sales. ## 4. Professional and dentist channel (Sensodyne, parodontax, ADA Seal, elmex/meridol, Spotlight, Cocolab, aligners)

## 5. Purpose and cultural positioning (Ayurveda in India, TCM in China, halal/miswak, eco, US fluoride-free politics, holistic dentistry)

### Takeaway
Cultural or "natural" positioning has produced some of the largest share shifts worldwide. In India, Ayurvedic and herbal pastes (Patanjali Dant Kanti, Dabur Red) took share from Colgate in 2015–2020, and Dabur Red became a ₹1,000-crore brand. In China, Yunnan Baiyao's TCM positioning made it the #1 toothpaste. In the US, 2025–26 fluoride politics (RFK Jr., Joe Rogan, state water bans) drove fluoride-free sales up ~16% a month versus +2.4% for fluoride paste (NielsenIQ, through Apr 2025), benefiting nano-hydroxyapatite brands. Eco positioning (tablets, refills) generated press and crowdfunding but stayed small.

## 7. Pricing and pack innovation as marketing (sachets, Rs 10 packs, multipacks, premium ladders)

### Takeaway
In emerging markets the opening price point is the main marketing weapon. Colgate India relies on Rs 5/10/20 packs, and in 2025 its CEO blamed a mis-set Rs 10 price-pack architecture for volume weakness. In China, BOP turned single-dose stick-pack mouthwash into a #1 sub-category. In the West, the price ladder runs from mass paste through premium-mass therapeutic (Optic White Pro, ~$8–11) and prestige beauty paste ($10–15) to subscription and refill economics (quip's cheaper DTC refills; tablets at ~$0.08–0.25 per use).

### Cited Findings
- Colgate India runs a Rs 10 and Rs 20 pack drive for Colgate Dental Cream to reach under-served central India and UP, after an earlier Rs 5 + Rs 10 ladder — [Business Standard 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/stock-market-news/colgate-s-outperformance-depends-on-volume-recovery-premiumisation-124040501044_1.html); [Business Standard 2009](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/opinion/colgate-a-good-performance-109111300049_1.html)
- On the Q2 2025 call, Colgate CEO Noel Wallace said problems with "price pack architecture," particularly at the Rs 10 opening price point, had hurt India volume growth. He said "getting the price pack architecture right on that is critically important" and that the Rs 20 range had been completed. Rs 10/20 packs kept extra grammage during price increases, which stayed in the low single digits. Small packs run alongside premium launches such as the Colgate Total relaunch — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/colgate-ceo-noel-wallace-not-pleased-with-india-sales-slump-eyes-fix-at-entry-price-point-78132.htm)

- Western price points: Optic White Pro ~$8–11; Boka $11.62 list / $9.88 subscription; Twice $6.99–14.99; Marvis "$10 tube"; Denttabs ~$0.08 per tab; CO. tabs $14 a month; 900.care pastilles ~€2.49 — [FashionBeans (Marvis)](https://www.fashionbeans.com/article/marvis-toothpaste-review/); [LivFresh](https://getlivfresh.com/blogs/blog/boka-vs-risewell-toothpaste); [Essence](https://www.essence.com/lifestyle/lenny-kravitz-toothpaste-brand/); prior notes

### Inferences
- Price-pack architecture is both a growth lever and a vulnerability. Colgate's 2025 India comments show that a mis-set entry pack can dent volume for a 50%-share leader.

- Patanjali: share doubled to 7.4% (FY2017) and was ~9.2% by value in 2020 (low-quality source). No later growth data found (§5).
- Other deals 2022–26 (prior note): Kimberly-Clark/Kenvue (~$48.7B, pending H2 2026); Perrigo reviewing its oral-care unit; BioSyent/Oral Science ($25.5M, Feb 2026).

### Inferences
- Acquirers have paid for retail velocity plus a defensible benefit (dry mouth/breath for TheraBreath; "friendly natural" and fluoride-optional for Hello), not for subscription bases or social followings. No social-first oral-care brand (Hismile, Moon) has had a disclosed exit.
- TheraBreath shows the post-acquisition playbook: an incumbent's distribution doubling the brand's reach. This is the "back to retail" endpoint of the DTC story.

### Gaps
- TheraBreath revenue (Church & Dwight does not break it out).
- Hello's post-acquisition sales under Colgate.
- No confirmed failures or shutdowns among oral-care DTC brands were found.
