## 1. Market size and growth: manual vs power, by region; power-brush penetration by country

### Takeaway
Paid market-research firms put the global electric-toothbrush market at about USD 3.5–5.0bn in 2025, growing about 4.7–8% a year. Manual brushes still make up about 80% of the overall toothbrush market. Power-brush penetration varies widely: roughly 76% in the US, ~82% in South Korea, 40–60% in Europe, ~30% in Japan and ~8% in China, according to a single Chinese consultancy estimate whose methodology is undisclosed. In the mature segment, Oral-B (P&G) reports its power-brush business growing faster than a market that is itself growing mid-single digits.

### Cited Findings
- **Global electric toothbrush market estimates (2025) differ widely by definition:**
  - Reportprime: USD 4.64bn (2024) → USD 4.98bn (2025) — [Reportprime](https://www.reportprime.com/electric-toothbrush-r2919)
  - Ken Research: USD 4,650m in 2025, ~7% CAGR — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/global-electric-toothbrush-market)
  - The Business Research Company: USD 3.84bn in 2025, 5.4% CAGR — [GII/TBRC](https://www.gii.co.jp/report/tbrc1675209-electric-toothbrush-global-market-report.html)
  - GII (Fortune Business Insights listing): USD 3.53bn in 2025, 4.73% CAGR to 2034 — [GII](https://www.gii.tw/report/fbs2005936-global-electric-toothbrush-market-size-share.html)
  - Technavio: +USD 1.9bn incremental growth 2024–2029, 8% CAGR — [Technavio](https://technavio.com/report/electric-toothbrush-market-industry-analysis)
- **Manual share of total toothbrush market:** manual toothbrushes are estimated to hold 79.23% of the toothbrush market in 2026. The snippet does not say whether this is value or units. — [GII toothbrush market report](https://www.gii.tw/report/fbs2005972-toothbrush-market-size-share-growth-global.html)
- **Penetration by country (LeadLeo Research Institute estimate, cited in a China state-media-linked article, undated, ~2022):** China ~8%, South Korea nearly 82% ("highest in Asia"), Japan ~30%, US ~76%, Europe ~40–60%. — [Yidaiyilu / China Daily-sourced article on Evowera](https://eng.yidaiyilu.gov.cn/qwyw/rdxw/245719.htm)
- A different source gives US **household** penetration of electric toothbrushes as 48% (single-source, unverified methodology). — [Marketintelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/toothbrush-market). *This conflicts with LeadLeo's 76%; the two likely use different definitions (users vs households).*
- **Germany (regional cohort, not national):** in the Greifswald SHIP cohort (Pomerania, NE Germany), electric-brush use rose from 18% at baseline (2002–2006) to 37% at the 11-year follow-up. — [Greifswald University Medicine press release (PI 39/2019)](https://www.medizin.uni-greifswald.de/fileadmin/user_extern_upload/Presse/2019/PI_39_2019_UMG.pdf); [zm-online](https://www.zm-online.de/news/detail/elektrische-zahnbuersten-beugen-zahnverlust-vor)
- **Germany brand concentration:** one market report says Oral-B holds "60%+" of German electric-toothbrush value share, a "near-monopoly". The methodology is not verified. — [SourceReady Germany Personal Care Devices Market Report 2026](https://www.sourceready.com/report/detail/germany-personal-care-devices-market-report-2026)
- **Power-brush market growth (company-reported):** P&G says Oral-B power brush grew high single digits in fiscal 2025 (year to June 2025), drove mid-single-digit market growth, and gained +1.3 share points. — [P&G FY2025 Annual Report (SEC Form ARS)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80424/000119312525191752/d879413dars.pdf); [P&G Annual Report 2025 web](https://us.pg.com/annualreport2025/introduction-and-fy-results/)
- **Price as a barrier:** higher upfront cost versus manual brushes "continue[s] to inhibit penetration in price-sensitive markets". — [Reportprime](https://www.reportprime.com/electric-toothbrush-r2919)

### Inferences
- Global estimates of the power-brush market agree on the order of magnitude (USD 3.5–5bn) and on mid-to-high single-digit growth. The spread reflects definitional choices (heads included or not, battery brushes included or not, retail vs manufacturer prices).
- On penetration, the US, South Korea and parts of Europe are mature, so growth there is mainly premiumisation and replacement heads. China, India and other emerging markets are the volume headroom: at ~8% Chinese penetration, every point gained is a very large number of users.
- Oral-B's 2025 comment (high-single-digit growth, +1.3 share points, iO2 launched "to encourage upgrades from manual") suggests incumbents see the manual-to-power conversion as their main growth lever. They describe it as entry-level AI/app-capable brushes trading down, not as a new form factor.

### Gaps
- No verified national figures for UK or Germany power-brush penetration (GfK, Euromonitor or Kantar figures were not reachable). [Unverified background: UK and Germany are commonly described as among Europe's highest-penetration markets, at roughly half or more of adults using a power brush.]
- No reliable global manual-toothbrush market value (USD) was retrieved. The manual-to-power split by region (value and units) remains a gap.
- LeadLeo's penetration figures are undated (likely ~2021–2022) and their method (users vs households, urban vs national) is unknown.
- No Statista or Euromonitor summaries could be fetched.

## 2. Incumbents and their 2020–2026 innovations

### Takeaway
Since 2020, the incumbents' innovation has centred on sensing and software layered on proven mechanisms. Oral-B iO (2020) uses magnetic drive, a smart pressure sensor, AI brushing recognition across 16 zones and a display. Philips Sonicare's Prestige 9900 uses "SenseIQ" adaptive sensing, and hum by Colgate (2020) offers an app with rewards. Incumbents then pushed the same features down to cheaper price points (Oral-B iO2 in 2025, Sonicare 6000/6400 at Walmart). Both P&G and Philips report healthy power-brush growth in 2025. Philips Personal Health grew 8% comparable in FY2025, while weakness in China is a recurring theme.

- C&D's exit from Spinbrush (a battery-powered, low-price spinning brush) while keeping Waterpik flossers fits a broader pattern: the cheap battery-brush tier is being squeezed between USD 20–50 rechargeable Chinese sonic brushes and incumbents' entry rechargeables.
- hum by Colgate's deep discounting points to a smart-brush model (app + rewards) without strong pull, although discontinuation is not confirmed.

## 3. Chinese challengers (usmile, Xiaomi/Mijia, Oclean, Laifen, Roaman, Soocas)

### Takeaway
In China, domestic brands have overtaken the Western incumbents online. usmile is reported as #1, with its share rising from 23% to 29% between 2023 and 2024 and overtaking Philips, and Philips itself reports declining China sales in 2025. Abroad, Chinese brands compete in the mid-market (roughly USD/EUR 50–100) through Amazon. Oclean reportedly earns ~90% of revenue overseas, and Laifen's Wave line has won Western awards (ADA Seal, TIME Best Inventions 2024). There is no verified evidence that they have materially displaced Oral-B or Philips in Europe or the US.

### Inferences
- usmile's rise is a China-domestic story: social-commerce marketing, design-led products and Tmall/Douyin channels. Philips' reported China decline is the mirror image.
- The Chinese export playbook is Amazon-first, mid-price (USD 50–100), with features usually reserved for incumbents' premium tiers (apps, wireless charging, displays, hybrid motion), Western credentials (ADA Seal, CES, TIME awards) and heavy discounting.
- Laifen's hybrid oscillating-plus-sonic motion is an attempt to straddle the two incumbent technologies (Oral-B oscillating-rotating vs Sonicare sonic).
- P&G's reported +1.3 share points in FY2025 suggests the challengers' Western share gains are, so far, coming more from the mid/low tier and from other brands than from Oral-B.

### Gaps
- No verified share data for Chinese brands on Amazon US/EU or in European retail. Keepa, Euromonitor or GfK data was not accessible.
- No current share data for Xiaomi/Mijia, Roaman or Soocas in China. Most Chinese-language source pages (36Kr, Sina, Pedaily) could not be fetched.
- usmile's revenue and IPO status: a domestic A-share tutoring agreement with CICC was reported, but no confirmed listing or Hong Kong filing was found.
- Prices for Xiaomi, Oclean flagship (e.g., X Ultra) and usmile models were not retrieved.

## 4. Startups and business models (winners, acquisitions, failures)

### Takeaway
The 2015–2022 DTC wave (quip, Burst, Goby, SURI, Happybrush, Bruush) was built on design-led sonic brushes plus head-replacement subscriptions. Survivors scaled through retail (quip), dental-professional affiliates (Burst) or sustainability positioning (SURI). Some failed publicly: Bruush, Nasdaq-listed then suspended and delisted in 2024–2025. The "automatic"/mouthpiece segment produced the category's most notable failure (Amabrush, 2019 insolvency and fraud probe) and a restructuring (Y-Brush, 2025 receivership, then acquired by Biotech Dental). Kids' gamification exits went to incumbents (Playbrush to Sunstar, 2021).

## 6. Clinical evidence (power vs manual; smart and automatic/U-shaped brushes)

### Takeaway
The 2014 Cochrane review (moderate-quality evidence) found power brushes reduce plaque by 11% (1–3 months) and 21% (3+ months), and gingivitis by 6% and 11%, versus manual brushes, though the clinical importance is unclear. An 11-year German cohort (SHIP, 2019) linked power-brush use to ~20% less tooth loss. Independent trials of mouthpiece "automatic" brushes found them much worse than ordinary manual brushing: Amabrush removed ~11% of plaque vs ~31% for manual. Positive data for U-shaped devices come mainly from manufacturer-funded studies. No independent evidence was found that "AI"/app features improve clinical outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- **Cochrane 2014 (CD002281.pub3):** 56 trials met the criteria and 51 (4,624 participants) were meta-analysed. Five trials were at low risk of bias, five at high and 46 unclear. Short-term (1–3 months): plaque −11%, gingivitis −6%. Long-term (>3 months): plaque −21%, gingivitis −11%. Evidence quality moderate, heterogeneity high (I² 83–86%). "The clinical importance of these findings remains unclear." Cost, reliability and side effects were inconsistently reported. — [Cochrane Library](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD002281.pub3); [Cochrane summary](https://www.cochrane.org/CD002281/ORAL_powered-vs-manual-toothbrushing-for-oral-health); [Univ. of Bristol record](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/powered-versus-manual-toothbrushing-for-oral-health/)
- The earlier 2003 Cochrane version found only rotation-oscillation brushes significantly better (about 7% less plaque and 17% less gingivitis). Do not mix these figures with the 2014 ones. — [PubMed 15190692](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15190692/)
- **Pitchika et al. 2019, J Clin Periodontol (doi:10.1111/jcpe.13126):** 2,819 adults in the SHIP cohort, followed 11 years. Power-brush users lost about one-fifth fewer teeth and had 22% and 21% smaller increases in probing depth and attachment loss. There was no benefit in severe periodontitis and no association with caries. Observational design. — [Greifswald press release](https://www.medizin.uni-greifswald.de/fileadmin/user_extern_upload/Presse/2019/PI_39_2019_UMG.pdf); [zm-online](https://www.zm-online.de/archiv/2019/12/zahnmedizin/weniger-zahnverluste-durch-elektrische-zahnbuersten/)
- **Schnabl et al. 2020, Clin Oral Investig (doi:10.1007/s00784-020-03359-5):** randomised, single-blind crossover pilot of the Amabrush auto-cleaning mouthpiece in 20 healthy volunteers after 3 days without hygiene. Full-mouth plaque reduction was 11.37 ± 3.70% for the device vs 31.39 ± 5.27% for uninstructed manual brushing (significant). Casts showed at most 2 of 4 bristle rows contacting teeth, and some areas had no contact. The authors concluded bristle alignment and density need improvement and multiple sizes are needed. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00784-020-03359-5)
- A 2022 follow-up from the same group (Clin Oral Investig, doi:10.1007/s00784-022-04755-9) reiterated that the auto-cleaning device "was not able to sufficiently remove dental plaque and needs further technical development". — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00784-022-04755-9); [Springer Medicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/dental-plaque-index/cleansing-efficacy-of-an-auto-cleaning-electronic-toothbrushing-/21320734)
- An older crossover trial compared a U-shaped "Superbrush" with a manual and a rotating electric brush in 36 volunteers aged 6–60. Results were not available in the snippet. — [PubMed 10355617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10355617)
- **Vendor-funded counter-evidence:** autobrush cites SALUS Research studies claiming up to 26.6× better plaque removal than manual and better gingival-index reductions at 30 days. These are sponsor-funded and not independently verified. — [autobrush](https://tryautobrush.com/blogs/dental/autobrush-2nd-clinical-study-reveals-groundbreaking-results)
- Oral-B iO "six times more plaque removal" appears in 2020 press coverage without a cited study (unverified marketing claim). — search summary of Oral-B iO launch coverage (no primary study located).

### Inferences
- The evidence base supports the power-over-manual premise in general (modest but consistent, with some long-term tooth-retention signal). It does **not** yet show added clinical benefit from AI, apps or position-tracking, and it actively undercuts first-generation mouthpiece "automatic" brushes.
- This asymmetry helps explain why incumbent mechanisms (oscillating-rotating, sonic) remain the reference point and challengers compete on price and features rather than on mechanism.

### Gaps
- No Cochrane update after 2014 was found.
- No independent RCTs of smart-feature effects (app coaching or position detection) on plaque or gingivitis were retrieved. Some may exist, but the search budget ran out.
- No independent evidence was retrieved on Y-Brush, Unobrush or Willo efficacy.

## 7. Sustainability in brushes

### Takeaway
The widely repeated "~1 billion toothbrushes discarded per year in the US (~50m lb to landfill)" figure appears to be a back-of-envelope replacement-rate estimate (about 300m people × 3–4 brushes a year), not a measured statistic. A global figure of "23 billion a year" also circulates. Sustainability is a core positioning for startups such as SURI (repairable brush) and for bamboo brands, and incumbents have exited or narrowed cheap battery-brush lines (C&D's Spinbrush exit).

### Cited Findings
- An Italian academic literature review cites 1 billion toothbrushes discarded per year in the US and ~50m lb of landfill, without a primary source. — [Sapienza IRIS](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/1480769)
- Good Magazine cites 1bn in the US out of ~4bn globally, without a primary source. — [Good Magazine](https://goodmagazine.co.nz/?p=25636)
- Recover Brands (a recycled-plastic toothbrush seller) cites 1.2bn per year in the US. — [Recover Brands](https://recoverbrands.com/a/blog/its-time-to-clean-up-your-toothbrush)
- A Scribd document derives "over 1 billion" and 44m lb from ~300m people replacing brushes every three months. — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/30318924/null)
- Earth911 cites over 23bn brushes discarded globally per year. — [Earth911](https://earth911.com/health/guest-idea-the-dirty-truth-about-clean-teeth/)
- SURI markets itself as a repairable electric toothbrush and raised funding from Innocent founders' fund JamJar. — [Unsustainable Substack](https://unsustainable.substack.com/p/the-first-repairable-electric-toothbrush); [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2134644)

### Inferences
- The US "1 billion" claim should be presented as an industry/NGO estimate derived from population and replacement guidance. Its consistency across sources reflects repetition, not independent measurement.

### Gaps
- TerraCycle oral-care recycling programmes (e.g., Colgate and Oral-B partnerships), bamboo-brush market size, biodegradable-head and recycled-nylon claims, and e-waste from power brushes were not researched. The search budget ran out.
