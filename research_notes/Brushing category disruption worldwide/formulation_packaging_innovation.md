Research date: 2026-10-15. **Method and limits:** I made about 40 web searches before the shared session search budget ran out (limit hit mid-task). Every page fetch failed: WebFetch returned DNS errors (ENOTFOUND) and curl was refused by the proxy (403). As a result, almost all findings below come from search-result excerpts of the cited pages, not full-text reads, and **no direct product-image URLs could be retrieved or checked**. Where a finding is reused from the earlier notes in `research_notes/Global toothpaste formats innovation/` (global_formats_landscape.md, competitive_landscape.md, startups_followup.md), I re-cite the original source and mark it "(earlier notes)". Company-reported claims are labelled **[company-reported]**. Market-research vendor sizes are low-confidence and often conflict.

Classification key used throughout: **F** = formulation, **P** = packaging/format, **F+P** = both (the format only works because the formula was changed, as with "instant coffee in a tube").

## 1. Format and packaging innovations: what exists, who sells it, where, since when, traction, classification

### Takeaway
Only two packaging innovations have reached mass scale in the 2020s, and neither changes how consumers use the product. The first is the recyclable mono-material HDPE tube: Colgate had converted about 75% of its toothpaste SKUs by end-2024, and tube maker EPL's sustainable tubes reached 43% of its tube sales by H1 FY27. The second is the low-unit-price sachet in emerging markets. Every format that changes how consumers use toothpaste remains niche or regional:
- tablets (about USD 70–120m globally)
- foams (Japan and Korea; foamgle is growing fast from a small base)
- liquid dentifrice (Japan)
- tooth soap (listings discontinued)
- strips (no toothpaste strip found; strips exist only for whitening and breath)
- drops and concentrates
- refill dispensers (SURI, launched July 2026)
- glass jars and aluminium tubes
- pre-pasted single-use brushes (Colgate Wisp, a hotel and travel staple)
- 2-in-1 paste plus mouthwash (revived in Malaysia and Korea)

Most of these need a formulation change (anhydrous, foaming, concentrated or non-aqueous), so they are really **F+P** innovations.

### Inferences
- The only packaging innovation with documented mass scale (recyclable HDPE tubes) is invisible to the consumer. It keeps the paste, the dose and the ritual unchanged, and incumbents pursued it together with their tube suppliers and then licensed it. Colgate's ~75% (2024) and EPL's 5%→43% (FY22→H1 FY27) show it is close to becoming the industry default.
- Almost every format that changes the ritual requires reformulation: a tablet must be anhydrous, foam must be aerosol- or pump-compatible, a liquid must be swishable, a concentrate low-dose, a pre-pasted brush dry or water-activated. This makes them **F+P** innovations of the "instant coffee in a tube" type, and is part of why they are slower and costlier to scale.
- Single-use and portion formats (sachets, Wisp, hotel kits, travel tablets) are the format innovations with the most durable demand. They solve access (price per pack) or context (no sink or water, travel) rather than sustainability.
- The 2-in-1 idea is cyclical: Colgate in the US around 2000, then a revival through foam in Korea (foamgle) and Darlie in Malaysia in the 2020s. Asia currently leads.

- Tooth soap, bars and strips: no sales, distribution or market-size data. No dissolvable toothpaste film product found anywhere.
- Sachet share of units or value in India, the Philippines, Indonesia or Africa was not found. No current sachet retail prices were found; one Philippine store-check document was undated and illegible.

## 2. Formulation innovations: what, who, where, since when, traction

### Takeaway
Formulation innovation scales when it rides on an existing tube and makes a claim regulators accept. Examples:
- stabilised stannous fluoride (Crest Pro-Health, Colgate Total 2019, Colgate Total Active Prevention 2025)
- arginine (Pro-Argin, from 2009)
- bioactive glass (Sensodyne NovaMin, relaunched 2024–26 across about 21 markets)
- enzymes (Lion Clinica, Japan)

### Inferences

- Non-aqueous bases (NovaMin), anhydrous tablets and stannous stabilisation systems all address the same technical problem: some actives are unstable in water. This is the most direct technical link between formulation innovation and format innovation.
- The purple colour-corrector is a clear example of a challenger-to-incumbent transfer: Hismile's influencer-driven V34 serum was followed by Colgate's mass-priced Purple Reveal in a standard fluoride tube within about two years. This is inferred from the dates; I found no exact V34 launch date.

### Takeaway
The closest analogies are: No Normal's coffee concentrate in a tube (Switzerland, 2024); Colgate's own Softsoap hand-soap tablets with a refillable aluminium bottle (US, 2021); 900.care's water-free tablet refills (France); and Listerine's dissolving strips (US, 2007). ## 4. Which innovations scaled vs stayed niche, and why

### Takeaway
What scaled: recyclable tubes (driven by supply-chain and regulatory pressure, at no change to the consumer); clinically backed actives in a tube (stannous, arginine, NovaMin, enzymes); culturally native actives (TCM, bamboo salt, Ayurveda); sachets (price access); and colour-correction (instant visible effect, adopted by an incumbent).

What stayed niche: tablets (worse sensory experience, often no fluoride, high price per use, zero-waste fashion faded); tooth soap (listings discontinued); strips (none exist for toothpaste); refill dispensers (very recent, premium); glass jars and aluminium tubes (small natural brands); drops (niche DTC); pre-pasted brushes (a one-off spike, then a stable travel and hospitality niche).

Foam is the one format with current, measurable fast growth (foamgle), from a very small base.

### Cited Findings
- **Sensory barrier:** in a Colgate tablet RCT, participants rated "texture/foaminess" more negatively — [LLU 2023](https://dentistry.llu.edu/sites/dentistry.llu.edu/files/2023-08/research-aug-2023.pdf). Haleon's tablet patent explicitly targets "sensorial problems" — [EPO EP4577311](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP4577311NWA1/document.html)
- **Fashion fades:** Denttabs demand declined as the zero-packaging trend lost momentum after around 2020 — [Lebensmittel Praxis](https://lebensmittelpraxis.de/drogerieartikel/40864-plastikfreie-mundhygiene-bleiben-zahnputztabletten-ein-nischenprodukt.html)
- **Fluoride and regulatory barrier:** sharks objected to Bite's lack of fluoride on Shark Tank (2020) — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/marcberman1/2020/04/01/move-over-crest-and-colgatebite-toothpaste-bits-featured-on-shark-tank-is-a-new-alternative/). NAD action limited Boka's nHAp claims — [Minvelle](https://www.minvelle.com/blogs/oral-health/boka-toothpaste-review-2026). Chinese regulators limit toothpaste efficacy claims — [ChemLinked](https://market.chemlinked.com/insight/what-is-new-in-chinas-toothpaste-market)
- **Format spike then fade:** Wisp took $9.3m in its first roughly three months, then sales fell. Its CEO claimed "nine market share points" in month one, within its segment **[company-reported]**. Crest tooth wipes had "bombed" — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/?p=106262); [MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/opinions/2009/22520/will-a-wisp-propel-colgate-forward)
- **Dropped format:** Mentadent's dual-chamber pump was discontinued in 2016 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentadent)
- **Tooth soap:** the main Western SKUs (Georganics) are no longer sold at Ecco Verde — [Ecco Verde](https://www.ecco-verde.co.uk/georganics/tooth-soap-stick)
- **Scaled packaging:** Colgate converted about 75% of SKUs to recyclable tubes by end-2024. EPL's sustainable share went from 5% to 43% — [Colgate proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21665/000130817925000223/cl_courtesy-pdf.pdf); [The Machine Maker](https://themachinemaker.com/?p=113216)
- **Scaled actives:** NovaMin in about 21 markets by end-2025 **[company-reported]** — [Dental Tribune](https://www.dental-tribune.com/news/as-oral-disease-becomes-more-prevalent-collaboration-becomes-essential/). Yunnan Baiyao at about 25% of China — [ChemLinked](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/report/keeping-pace-with-the-changing-landscape-and-trends-in-chinas-toothpaste-market). Bamboo salt above 25% of Korea (undated) — [JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/lifestyle/the-whole-tooth-behind-salty-brushing/11418531)
- **Fast-growing foam:** foamgle's Amazon sales are growing about 65% per quarter on average **[company-reported]** — [Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026070708272317538)

### Inferences
- **Consumer:** formats that ask users to change the ritual (chew, dissolve, swish, wet a bar) face a sensory and habit penalty. They succeed only where the context removes the tube (travel, no water, hotel) or the benefit is instant (foam freshness, purple correction).
- **Cost:** tablets cost about $0.05–0.25 per brush against cents for mass paste (earlier notes), and refill systems (SURI at $30) are premium. Sachets scaled because they lower the price per purchase.
- **Regulatory:** fluoride-free formats are blocked from anticaries claims in the US and constrained in China. Drug or quasi-drug status (US OTC monograph, Japanese quasi-drug) favours incumbents with clinical dossiers, which is why foamgle publicised its US OTC registration.
- **Sensory:** foam, mouthfeel and flavour are the decisive variables. They appear in Haleon's patent rationale, the Colgate RCT, and Marvis's flavour-led premiumisation.

### Gaps
- No consumer survey data comparing format preferences was found.
- No sales history for tablets after 2024 at incumbents (Colgate tabs) was found.

- Incumbent R&D concentrates on making known actives (stannous, bioactive glass, fluoride) work better inside existing formats. Haleon's tablet patent is the one clear signal of incumbent interest in a new dentifrice format, and it is framed as a sensory fix.
- Packaging IP was shared or licensed (Colgate, Unilever) rather than defended. This explains how fast recyclable tubes spread across the industry.
