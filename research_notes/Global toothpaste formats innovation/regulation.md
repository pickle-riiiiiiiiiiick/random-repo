# Regulatory pathway for launching a new toothpaste format in the EU, UK, US and Canada (as of 2026-10-15)

Many findings below therefore come from search-result excerpts of primary documents (FDA M021 PDF, eCFR Part 355, Health Canada monograph, EUR-Lex) and from secondary regulatory-consultancy sources. ## EU: classification and core obligations (Reg. 1223/2009), fluoride, nano-HAp, TiO2, microplastics, claims, PPWR

### Takeaway
In the EU every toothpaste format (paste, tablet, powder, foam, liquid) is a cosmetic, whether or not it contains fluoride. Market entry needs no pre-approval. It does need an EU Responsible Person, a safety assessment (CPSR) inside a Product Information File (PIF), and CPNP notification. The legal fluoride cap is 0.15% F (1,500 ppm). Nano-hydroxyapatite is lawful but restricted: up to 10% in toothpaste, rod-shaped and uncoated particles only, and no inhalation-exposure uses. That restriction has been fully in force since 1 Nov 2025. SCCS opined in mid-2025 that up to 29.5% is safe, but no amendment raising the legal cap was found. The upcoming dates that matter are PPWR application on 12 Aug 2026 and the microplastics rinse-off deadline of 17 Oct 2027.

### Cited Findings
**Nano-hydroxyapatite (nano-HAp)**
- Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/858 restricts hydroxyapatite (nano) to a maximum of 10% in toothpaste and 0.465% in mouthwash. It bars uses that could expose the end user's lungs by inhalation — [EUR-Lex 2024/858](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/858/oj); [Coslaw.eu](https://coslaw.eu/new-ban-and-restrictions-for-certain-nanomaterials-for-use-in-cosmetics/)
- Nano-HAp was added to Annex III as entry 372 — [Cinoll (secondary)](https://www.cinoll.com/blog/sccs-approves-higher-concentration-limits-for-nano-hydroxyapatite/)
- Particle conditions: rod-shaped particles (at least 95.8% with an aspect ratio below 3), not coated or surface-modified — [ChemLinked](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/cosmetic-news/eu-to-amend-the-use-requirements-for-13-nanomaterials-in-cosmetics); [CosmeticOBS](https://cosmeticobs.com/en/articles/news-59/regulation-2024858-12-nanos-banned-hydroxyapatite-subject-to-restrictions-7964)
- Dates: products placed on the market had to comply from 1 Feb 2025. Non-compliant products could no longer be made available from 1 Nov 2025 — [Coslaw watch-out database](https://coslaw.eu/watchout/hydroxypatite-nano/); [CIRS Group](https://www.cirs-group.com/en/cosmetics/european-union-revised-list-of-prohibited-and-restricted-substances-in-cosmetics)
- The same regulation moved several nanomaterials to the Annex II ban list: copper, colloidal silver, gold and styrene/acrylates copolymer (nano) — [CosmeticsCare](https://www.cosmeticscare.eu/en/nano-substances-in-the-light-of-new-regulations/). Relevant to "silver" or "gold" oral-care concepts.
- SCCS final opinion SCCS/1677/25 (around 30 June/1 July 2025, after a consultation from 3 Apr to 30 May 2025) concluded nano-HAp is safe up to 29.5% in toothpaste and 10% in mouthwash. This holds only for particles with the specified characteristics — [ChemLinked](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/cosmetic-news/eu-sccs-concludes-hydroxyapatite-nano-is-safe-at-higher-levels-for-oral-use); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ec-scientific-committee-issues-final-opinion-hydroxyapatite-nano-oral-products); [RIVM](https://www.rivm.nl/en/weblog/new-safety-data-confirms-hydroxyapatite-in-nanoform-is-safe-for-oral-care-products)
- Until Annex III is amended, the binding EU limits stay at 10% in toothpaste and 0.465% in mouthwash. No adopted Commission amendment raising the limit to 29.5% was found — [Cinoll](https://www.cinoll.com/blog/sccs-approves-higher-concentration-limits-for-nano-hydroxyapatite/). One site claims 2024/858 already holds the higher limits. That is contradicted by the sequence of events: the 29.5% opinion post-dates 2024/858 — [same search, flagged unreliable].

**Microplastics (REACH Annex XVII restriction, Reg. (EU) 2023/2055)**
- The general restriction on synthetic polymer microparticles (SPM) at ≥0.01% w/w has applied since 17 Oct 2023. Microbeads used to exfoliate, polish or clean were banned immediately — [EU Access2Markets](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/ko-kr/news/restriction-microplastics-eu-17-october-2023); [Coslaw](https://coslaw.eu/new-eu-restrictions-on-microplastics-in-cosmetic-products/)
- Rinse-off cosmetic products under 1223/2009 have until 17 Oct 2027 for SPMs used for other functions, such as film-formers and opacifiers — [Coptis](https://www.coptis.com/resources/reach-restriction-microplastics/); [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2023/10/safeguards-12623-eu-regulates-microplastics-under-reach)

**Packaging (PPWR, Reg. (EU) 2025/40)**
- PPWR entered into force on 11 Feb 2025 and applies generally from 12 Aug 2026, repealing Directive 94/62/EC — [BRC](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/associate-insight/2026/european-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-ppwr/); [EU Circular Economy Platform](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/en/news-and-events/all-news/eu-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-when-will-it-come-effect-and-what-does-it-cover)
- First recyclability criteria apply from 1 Jan 2028, with further acts due by 1 Jan 2030. The aim is for all packaging to be recyclable in an economically viable way by 2030 — [BRC](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/associate-insight/2026/european-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-ppwr/); [Measurlabs](https://measurlabs.com/blog/new-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation)
- The mandatory refill and reuse provisions found apply to takeaway food and beverage (from 12 Feb 2027), not to cosmetics — [BRC](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/associate-insight/2026/european-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-ppwr/)

### Inferences
- **Classification (unverified this session):** Article 2 of 1223/2009 defines a cosmetic to include products applied to "the teeth and the mucous membranes of the oral cavity" to clean, protect or keep them in good condition. Fluoride toothpaste therefore stays a cosmetic in the EU, unlike in the US and Canada. Tablets, powders, foams and strips are all cosmetics provided they are not ingested and make no medicinal claims, such as "treats gum disease". Claims about treating or preventing disease beyond normal oral hygiene can tip a product into the medicinal-product category under Directive 2001/83/EC.
- **Fluoride (unverified this session; Annex III entries for sodium fluoride, sodium MFP, stannous fluoride, amine fluorides and others):** the maximum is 0.15% F (1,500 ppm), with combined fluoride compounds also capped at 0.15% F in total. Labels must state "contains [fluoride compound]". Toothpastes with 0.1–0.15% F that are not already labelled as contraindicated for children must carry: "Children of 6 years and younger: Use a pea sized amount for supervised brushing to minimise swallowing. In case of intake of fluoride from other sources consult a dentist or doctor." Products above 1,500 ppm, such as the 2,800/5,000 ppm prescription pastes, are medicinal products. For a tablet, the "pea-sized amount" wording doesn't fit naturally, because the dose is fixed per tablet. The CPSR and labelling need to show each tablet delivers about 1,000–1,450 ppm in slurry-equivalent terms, plus clear age-appropriate directions.
- **Core obligations (unverified this session):** (1) appoint an EU-established Responsible Person (Art. 4–5); (2) produce a PIF (Art. 11) with a CPSR (Annex I Part A safety information, Part B assessor conclusion) signed by a qualified safety assessor; (3) manufacture under GMP, with ISO 22716 presumed compliant; (4) notify the CPNP before placing on the market (Art. 13); (5) for nanomaterials not in an annex, notify 6 months before marketing (Art. 16). Nano-HAp is now in Annex III, so the 6-month Art. 16 notification no longer applies. The product must still be notified in CPNP, and "(nano)" must appear in the INCI list (Art. 19(1)(g)). (6) report serious undesirable effects (Art. 23).
- **Claims (unverified this session):** Reg. (EU) 655/2013 sets six common criteria for claims: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness and informed decision-making. The 2017 technical document on claims also covers "free from" claims; "fluoride-free" is acceptable as information, but must not denigrate fluoride. Remineralisation and anti-caries claims for nano-HAp need evidence in the PIF.
- **Titanium dioxide (unverified this session):** TiO2 (CI 77891) remains permitted in EU cosmetics. After the EU food-additive ban (E171, 2022), the SCCS reviewed oral/lip product exposure in 2023, and Reg. (EU) 2024/996 (Omnibus VI-type) is reported to have restricted TiO2 in powder form in face products to manage inhalation. It is unconfirmed whether any restriction now applies to oral products. Startups should consider TiO2-free whitening agents to avoid "E171" consumer and retailer concerns. A tooth powder containing nano-HAp or TiO2 raises the inhalation-exposure question the 2024/858 text explicitly bars for nano-HAp. **Loose tooth powders with nano-HAp are therefore legally risky in the EU. Compressed tablets are lower risk but should still get an inhalation/dust argument in the CPSR.**
- **CMR omnibus updates:** Annual "Omnibus" acts ban newly classified CMR substances. Reg. (EU) 2025/877 (Omnibus VII) applied from 1 Sep 2025 and covered TPO, among others. A new formula should be screened against the latest Annex II.
- **PPWR for tubes and refills:** From 12 Aug 2026, packaging must meet minimisation, labelling and substances (PFAS limits, for food contact) requirements. Recyclability grades start from 2028–2030, which favours mono-material HDPE/PP tubes over multilayer laminate (ABL/PBL) tubes. Tablets in glass or aluminium refill pouches are well positioned, but empty-space and minimisation rules affect oversized refill boxes. Cosmetics-specific reuse targets were not found in this session.

### Gaps
- The official EUR-Lex text of the Annex III fluoride entries and the exact child-warning wording could not be fetched. Verify against the consolidated 1223/2009.
- Whether the Commission has drafted or adopted an Annex III amendment raising nano-HAp to 29.5% after SCCS/1677/25 (no evidence found as of Oct 2026).
- The current TiO2 status specifically for oral-care products; the details of Reg. 2024/996 were not verified.
- Detailed PPWR obligations for cosmetic tubes (empty-space ratio, labelling dates, recycled-content targets for plastic packaging) were not confirmed.

## UK: UK Cosmetics Regulation, SCPN, OPSS, divergence

### Takeaway
GB runs an EU-derived regime ("assimilated" 1223/2009) enforced via OPSS. It needs a UK-based Responsible Person, a PIF/CPSR, and SCPN notification before marketing. Non-annexed nanomaterials need notification 6 months in advance. GB has **not** been confirmed to have adopted the EU's 2024/858 nano-HAp restriction. OPSS ran a 2023 call for data on nano-HAp, so nano-HAp in GB is a divergence point that needs specific checking. Northern Ireland follows EU rules.

### Cited Findings
- OPSS issued a call for data on nanomaterials in cosmetics, and its list includes hydroxyapatite (nano). This is an evidence request, not a restriction — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/call-for-data-nanomaterials-in-cosmetics); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/uk-calls-data-nanomaterials-cosmetics)
- Under Article 16 of the UK Cosmetics Regulation, products containing nanomaterials must be notified to the Secretary of State at least six months before being placed on the market. Notification is via the SCPN portal — [EUverify (secondary)](https://euverify.com/resource/uk-cosmetics-banned-restricted-ingredients/); [SCPN service](https://submit.cosmetic-product-notifications.service.gov.uk/)
- A vendor page refers to "SI 2026/23" amending the assimilated UK regulation on an independent timetable. Its contents, including whether it covers nano-HAp, were not confirmed — [EUverify](https://euverify.com/resource/uk-cosmetics-banned-restricted-ingredients/)
- Notification steps for UK and EU are run as separate parallel filings — [Hooley Brown](https://www.hooleybrown.com/blog-post/notify-cosmetic-products-uk-eu)

### Inferences
- In practice, a nano-HAp toothpaste sold in GB falls under the Art. 16 6-month pre-notification route, unless GB has now listed nano-HAp in its own Annex III. The 6-month lead time should be built into the GB plan.
- The GB fluoride limit is inherited from the EU: 0.15% F, with the same child warnings (unverified this session).
- UK packaging rules differ from the EU PPWR. UK Extended Producer Responsibility fees have applied from 2025 and the Plastic Packaging Tax applies below 30% recycled content (not researched this session).

### Gaps
- Whether GB has adopted any nano-HAp restriction, and what SI 2026/23 contains (legislation.gov.uk not reachable this session).
- The current GB status of the EU Omnibus CMR bans and of 2024/858's Annex II nano bans (silver, gold, copper).

## US: FDA OTC Monograph M021, Drug Facts, registration, testing for non-paste formats, OTC reform, MoCRA, hydroxyapatite, ADA Seal, 2025–2026 fluoride actions

### Takeaway
Fluoride toothpaste in the US is an OTC drug. It must conform to OTC Monograph M021, the deemed final order that replaced 21 CFR 355 under the CARES Act. M021 names dentifrices as paste, gel or powder. The FDA biological testing (animal caries reduction plus another test such as enamel fluoride uptake) is the gate for showing a new formulation works. **Tablets and foams are not named dosage forms.** Some tablet brands list on DailyMed as monograph products, but others say no FDA-recognised test protocol exists for tablets. A sponsor who wants certainty would need an OTC Monograph Order Request (OMOR; OMUFA fees) or an NDA. Fluoride-free toothpaste (for example, HAp-only) is a cosmetic under MoCRA. FDA's 2025 anti-fluoride posture targeted ingestible pediatric fluoride (prescription drops and tablets), not toothpaste.

### Cited Findings
**M021 actives, concentrations, packaging**
- M021 is a final administrative order (OTC000034), deemed under FD&C Act §505G and effective 27 Mar 2020 under the CARES Act. The monograph PDF was posted 2 May 2023 — [FDA final order](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/omuf/Order/Final%20Administrative%20Order%20OTC000034_M021-Anticaries%20Drug%20Products%20for%20OTC%20Human%20Use.pdf)
- Sodium fluoride gel or paste dentifrices must have 850–1,150 ppm theoretical total fluorine (0.188–0.254% NaF) and at least 650 ppm available fluoride ion. Powdered dentifrices have a separate threshold — [FDA M021 PDF](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/omuf/monographs/OTC%20Monograph%20M021-Anticaries%20Drug%20Products%20for%20OTC%20Human%20Use%2005.02.2023.pdf)
- Permitted dentifrice actives are sodium fluoride, sodium monofluorophosphate and stannous fluoride — [Zonhack (secondary)](https://zonhack.com/private-label-dental-products/). Typical concentrations are NaF 0.22–0.24%, SMFP 0.76% and SnF2 0.454%. Secondary sources conflict (one gives SnF2 as "0.15% available fluoride", another 0.45%) — [FDA Registration Assistance](https://www.fdaregistrationassistance.com/is-toothpaste-an-otc-drug-fda-compliance-guide/); [Cinoll](https://www.cinoll.com/blog/toothpaste-otc-in-usa-guide-for-new-brand/)
- The stannous fluoride provision covers dentifrices with 850–1,150 ppm theoretical total fluorine "in a gel or paste dosage form". "Dentifrice (toothpastes and tooth powders) packages shall not contain more than 276 milligrams (mg) total fluorine per package" — [eCFR 21 CFR 355](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-355)

**Testing (M021.70)**
- M021.70 sets testing procedures for fluoride dentifrices. Products must meet the biological test requirement for animal caries reduction plus one of the listed tests, such as enamel solubility reduction or fluoride enamel uptake — [FDA M021 PDF](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/omuf/monographs/OTC%20Monograph%20M021-Anticaries%20Drug%20Products%20for%20OTC%20Human%20Use%2005.02.2023.pdf)
- The EFU method uses 3 mm enamel cores and a microdrill biopsy, with results in µg F/cm³ — [USPTO patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12016940); [PubMed 17994149](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17994149/). Example values are EFU of 15.58 for a 1,500 ppm SMFP paste and 9.23 for a 500 ppm SMFP paste — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01657877](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01657877)

**Non-paste formats (tablets, foam)**
- Bite (a tablet brand) states that tablets "are not included in the monograph" and that there are "no FDA-approved testing protocols we could follow for tablets specifically". This is a vendor statement — [Bite blog](https://bitetoothpastebits.com/blogs/blog/the-f-word-3-reasons-why-our-bits-are-fluoride-free)
- Fluoride toothpaste tablets are nonetheless listed on DailyMed as OTC monograph products under Part 355. Examples are a 0.145% NaF tablet and Dr.NOAH "Sodium Fluoride 0.22% (1,000 ppm fluoride ion)", listed as "paste, dentifrice". DailyMed notes most OTC drugs are not reviewed or approved by FDA — [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ad447c9a-6f0f-7fef-e053-2995a90a055f&type=display); [FDA.report](https://fda.report/DailyMed/3fec6cda-62cc-4e13-a100-1411681c5bbc)
- A Harvard (DASH) paper on toothpaste regulation discusses the monograph system's handling of formulation and testing — [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/7312037c-a8bc-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/download)

**Enforcement**
- FDA warning letter of 28 Jul 2025 to Health and Natural Beauty USA Corp.: fluoride and children's toothpastes did not comply with M021 and were "not otherwise GRASE", making them unapproved new drugs — [FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/health-and-natural-beauty-usa-corp-700187-07282025)

**2025–2026 FDA fluoride actions**
- On 31 Oct 2025, FDA announced actions against unapproved *ingestible* fluoride prescription drugs for children. Notices went to four companies, targeting products labelled for under-3s or for low- or moderate-caries-risk children. FDA also sent a letter to healthcare providers and announced a fluoride research agenda and a national oral health strategy — [HHS press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/fda-protect-children-unapproved-fluoride-drug-products.html); [AGD](https://agd.org/constituent/news/2025/11/12/fda-announces-actions-to-restrict-sale-of-fluoride-products-for-children); [Contemporary Pediatrics](https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/fda-restricts-sale-of-unapproved-ingestible-fluoride-drugs-for-children)
- FDA had first announced its intention to remove ingestible pediatric fluoride in May 2025 — [Contemporary Pediatrics May 2025](https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/fda-seeks-to-remove-ingestible-fluoride-products-for-children)
- No 2026 FDA action changing M021 or restricting topical fluoride toothpaste was found in searches — [search result summary; no primary source located]

**MoCRA (for fluoride-free / cosmetic toothpaste)**
- Facility registration (renewed every 2 years) and product listing are in force. As of 6 Jan 2026, FDA held 14,299 active facility registrations and 992,907 active product listings. Registration and listing are not approval — [Foley & Lardner 2026](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/how-mocra-is-reshaping-fda-oversight-of-cosmetics-in-2026/); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/cosmetics-under-the-microscope-fdas-expanding-regulatory-reach-under-mocra)
- The MoCRA GMP rule is still not proposed. The statutory deadline was 29 Dec 2024 for the proposal and 29 Dec 2025 for the final rule. FDA's February 2026 presentation confirmed the rulemaking is ongoing, and ISO 22716 is the de facto benchmark — [Ecomundo](https://ecomundo.eu/en/blog/mocra-deadlines-update-2026); [Luxmetics](https://luxmetics.beauty/mocra-gmp-rule-iso-22716-2026/)
- The fragrance-allergen labelling proposed rule is expected in 2026; sources conflict on the timing (May vs Nov 2026). FDA withdrew the talc-testing proposed rule on 28 Nov 2025 — [Ecomundo](https://ecomundo.eu/en/blog/mocra-update-2026); [Dicentra](https://dicentra.com/blog/adverse-reaction-reporting/mocra-in-2026-what-has-changed-and-what-does-fda-expect-from-the-cosmetics-industry)

### Inferences
- **Drug obligations for fluoride toothpaste (unverified this session):** (1) Drug Establishment Registration and Drug Listing (21 CFR 207) via SPL in FDA's electronic system, plus an NDC labeler code. (2) Full drug cGMP (21 CFR 210/211), including validated assay of fluoride and available fluoride, stability supporting expiry dating (21 CFR 211.137), and batch release testing. (3) Drug Facts panel (21 CFR 201.66) with the active-ingredient line, use ("aids in the prevention of dental cavities"), warning ("Keep out of reach of children under 6 yrs of age. If more than used for brushing is accidentally swallowed, get medical help or contact a Poison Control Center right away"), and directions: adults and children 2 yrs and older brush after meals at least twice daily; children under 6 use a pea-sized amount with supervised brushing; under 2 yrs ask a dentist. (4) OMUFA annual facility fees for OTC monograph facilities (the FY2026 amount was not verified). (5) Serious adverse event reporting under FD&C Act §760. MoCRA's cosmetic provisions do not apply to a product regulated solely as a drug.
- **Format fit:** M021 names paste, gel and powder. A powder must meet its own available-fluoride threshold. A tablet that becomes a paste in the mouth is usually labelled "paste, dentifrice" on DailyMed. Whether FDA would accept that has not been tested publicly. The prudent startup approach: (a) run the full M021.70 biological package on the final tablet or foam formula at an accredited lab (animal caries plus EFU or enamel solubility, plus available-fluoride stability); (b) seek a pre-submission meeting or consider an OMOR to add a "tablet" dosage form if the claim is to be bulletproof. OMORs carry OMUFA order-request fees (tier 1 is around USD 500k+; verify the current FY amount) and FDA review timelines of a year or more.
- **Fluoride-free path:** A HAp, xylitol or arginine toothpaste with no drug claims is a cosmetic. It needs MoCRA registration and listing, safety substantiation records, a US contact on the label for adverse events, and serious-AE reporting within 15 business days. "Anticavity", "remineralizes to prevent cavities" or "strengthens enamel to prevent decay" claims would make it an unapproved drug, because hydroxyapatite is not a monograph active. Cosmetic-safe wording such as "cleans", "whitens" or "smooths enamel" should be cleared by counsel.
- **Hydroxyapatite in the US:** It is not an M021 active and has no FDA restriction as a cosmetic ingredient. Nano status has no specific US rule, but MoCRA safety substantiation applies. An earlier FDA citizen petition for HAp as an anticaries active (from the 2020s) was not verified.
- **ADA Seal of Acceptance:** voluntary. It requires clinical or lab evidence per ADA Acceptance Program requirements, and fluoride toothpastes have historically needed fluoride for caries claims. The ADA Seal now also covers some fluoride-free products for non-caries claims (unverified this session).
- **State-level:** pending state restrictions on PFAS in cosmetics and packaging, and California Prop 65 for TiO2 (airborne, unbound respirable particles), which matters for powders. Not researched in this session.

### Gaps
- The exact M021 text on powdered-dentifrice available-fluoride thresholds and the full dosage-form wording (fetch failed).
- Current OMUFA FY2026 fee amounts (facility fee, OMOR tier 1/tier 2).
- Whether FDA has ever issued an OMOR or a statement on fluoride toothpaste tablets or foams.
- Cost of a full M021.70 biological test package (rough industry estimates exist, but no source was found).
- ADA Seal current requirements and fees.

## Canada: Health Canada classification, monograph, DIN/NPN, licensing, fluoride-free cosmetic, bilingual labelling

### Takeaway
Health Canada authorises oral-care drugs and NHPs under a single **Oral Health Products Monograph**. A new version dated **25 Sept 2026** replaces the 23 Feb 2024 edition. Products meeting the monograph receive either a **DIN** (non-prescription drug) or an **NPN** (natural health product), depending on ingredients and claims. Secondary sources conflict on which applies to fluoride toothpaste. Market practice, with DINs on major-brand Canadian toothpastes, indicates fluoride toothpaste takes the DIN route. Fluoride-free toothpaste with cosmetic-only claims is a cosmetic and needs a Cosmetic Notification Form within 10 days of first sale. The long-planned "self-care framework" reclassification is still unfinalised.

### Cited Findings
- The Health Canada Oral Health Products Monograph, dated 2026-09-25, replaces the Feb 23, 2024 monograph. It "describes the requirements necessary to receive market authorization [i.e. a Drug Identification Number (DIN) or a Natural Product Number (NPN)], for oral health products." Its ingredient table lists sodium fluoride and acidulated phosphate fluoride with concentrations — [Health Canada monograph PDF](https://webprod.hc-sc.gc.ca/nhpid-bdipsn/dbImages/mono_oral-health-products_english.pdf); [NHPID entry](https://webprod.hc-sc.gc.ca/nhpid-bdipsn/atReq?atid=oral.health.sante.bucco.dentaire&lang=eng)
- Conflicting secondary claims: one source says fluoride toothpastes are NHPs. Another says fluoride toothpaste and cavity-prevention claims generally need a DIN, and that "strengthens enamel" or "reduces sensitivity" claims typically need a DIN. Both say the DIN/NPN outcome depends on medicinal ingredients, concentrations and claim wording — [LLRN Care](https://llrncare.com/canada-cosmetic-labeling-requirements-for-oral-care-products/); [Quality Smart Solutions](https://qualitysmartsolutions.com/blog/toothpaste-classification-under-health-canada-otc-drug-nhp-or-cosmetic)
- Fluoride-free toothpastes, cosmetic mouthwashes and whitening products with aesthetic-only claims are cosmetics, and need only a Cosmetic Notification within 10 days of first sale — [LLRN Care](https://llrncare.com/canada-cosmetic-labeling-requirements-for-oral-care-products/)
- Self-care framework: Health Canada's forward regulatory plan proposes a risk-based approach with expedited, class-based licensing for lower-risk non-prescription drugs and NHPs. It remains a plan; no final rule was found — [Canada.ca forward regulatory plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/corporate/about-health-canada/legislation-guidelines/acts-regulations/forward-regulatory-plan/plan/self-care-framework.html)
- An older CBC piece described fluoride toothpaste as an NHP and hydrogen-peroxide whitening toothpaste as a drug. It is undated in the metadata and treated as background only — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/health/health-canada-products-1.4050629)

### Inferences
- **Most likely current pathway for fluoride toothpaste (unverified this session):** a DIN via a monograph-based ("Category IV"-style) application under the Oral Health Products Monograph. A DIN needs a Drug Establishment Licence (DEL) for the importer or distributor, plus GMP evidence for foreign sites (Part C, Division 2 of the Food and Drug Regulations). If an ingredient set qualifies as an NHP instead, an NPN plus an NHP site licence is needed. Health Canada's DIN monograph-application performance standard is about 45–90 days; NPN Class I (monograph) applications target about 60 days. Fees (evaluation fee for a monograph DIN; annual right-to-sell fee; DEL fees) were not verified this session.
- **Dosage form:** A Canadian monograph lists permitted dosage forms. Whether "tablet" or "foam" is an accepted dentifrice dosage form under the 2026 monograph must be checked in the PDF. If not listed, a non-monograph (Class II/III or full DIN) submission with supporting evidence would be needed, adding months and cost.
- **Labelling:** bilingual English/French labelling is mandatory for both drugs/NHPs and cosmetics. Cosmetics need an INCI ingredient list. ## Typical timelines, costs and testing requirements by market

### Takeaway
No source in this session gave authoritative cost figures, so costs below are unverified ranges for the report writer to frame as indicative. The structural picture is clear. The EU, UK and US/Canada fluoride-free routes are notification-based and can be launch-ready in about 3–6 months, gated by stability testing. US and Canadian fluoride products are drug routes that add drug cGMP, registration and listing, and fluoride efficacy and bioavailability testing. A non-monograph format in the US (tablet or foam) adds the most uncertainty: an OMOR or NDA could mean 1–3+ years and seven-figure costs.

### Cited Findings
- The US fluoride dentifrice monograph requires biological testing (animal caries reduction plus a second test) — [FDA M021 PDF](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/omuf/monographs/OTC%20Monograph%20M021-Anticaries%20Drug%20Products%20for%20OTC%20Human%20Use%2005.02.2023.pdf)
- US OTC drug status means FDA can issue warning letters to non-conforming fluoride toothpastes (2025 example) — [FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/health-and-natural-beauty-usa-corp-700187-07282025)
- EU nano-HAp requires particle-characterisation conformity: shape, aspect ratio and no coating — [ChemLinked](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/cosmetic-news/eu-to-amend-the-use-requirements-for-13-nanomaterials-in-cosmetics)
- GB nanomaterial notification requires 6 months' lead time where Art. 16 applies — [EUverify](https://euverify.com/resource/uk-cosmetics-banned-restricted-ingredients/)

### Inferences (unverified this session — indicative only)
- **Testing common to all markets:** stability testing of 3 months accelerated (40°C/75% RH) plus real-time, with packaging compatibility. This matters most for tablets (moisture uptake, friability, hardness) and foams (propellant/pump). Preservative efficacy testing (ISO 11930 / USP <51>) applies to water-containing formats, but anhydrous tablets and powders may justify a reduced micro risk under ISO 29621. Microbial limits come from ISO 17516. RDA abrasivity (ISO 11609 caps RDA at 250; most brands target under 150) can be measured on a slurry made from the tablet or powder. Fluoride assay covers total and available/soluble fluoride over shelf life, which is critical for SMFP or NaF combined with calcium abrasives or HAp, where calcium can bind fluoride. Nano characterisation (TEM or similar particle size distribution, aspect ratio, coating) is needed for nano-HAp in the EU.
- **EU/UK (cosmetic, including fluoride):** RP service around EUR 1–5k/yr; CPSR around EUR 1.5–5k per formula; stability and PET around EUR 3–10k; CPNP and SCPN notification free. Timeline: 3–6 months, driven by stability. GB nano notification adds 6 months.
- **US fluoride (OTC drug):** drug registration, listing and NDC (low direct cost, but needs SPL capability). OMUFA facility fee is tens of thousands of USD per year per facility (verify). M021.70 biological testing (animal caries plus EFU) is roughly USD 50–150k+ per formula (estimate; no source). Full cGMP compliance usually means using an FDA-registered OTC contract manufacturer. Timeline: about 6–12 months. A non-monograph format via OMOR adds 12–24+ months and OMUFA order fees.
- **US fluoride-free (cosmetic):** MoCRA registration and listing (free), safety substantiation dossier, labelling. About 3–4 months.
- **Canada fluoride (DIN):** monograph DIN application plus DEL for the importer; around 3–6 months. Canada fluoride-free (cosmetic): notification within 10 days post-launch plus bilingual labelling, so a few weeks of regulatory work.

### Gaps
- No authoritative published cost data was found for CPSR, M021 biological testing, OMUFA fees, Health Canada DIN fees, or ADA Seal fees. All figures above are unverified estimates.
- No source confirmed accepted test methods for RDA or fluoride availability specifically for tablets and foams in any market.
