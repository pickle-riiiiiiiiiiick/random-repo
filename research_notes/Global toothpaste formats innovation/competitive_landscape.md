# Competitive Landscape: Innovative Toothpaste Formats and Challenger Oral-Care Brands (Western Europe and North America)

Research date: 2026-10-15. Method: web search (about 30 queries and fetch attempts). Many direct page fetches failed (DNS errors on patents.google.com, prnewswire.com, beautymatter.com, dentalroundup.com), so several findings rely on search-result snippets of the cited pages and not on full-text reads. Revenue and valuation figures for private brands come mostly from third-party estimators (Growjo, ZoomInfo, Owler, ecdb, Grips) and conflict with one another. Treat them as rough indications, not facts.

## 1. Incumbents' format innovations (Colgate, P&G, Haleon, Unilever, Kenvue) and how they performed

### Takeaway
Incumbents have mostly put their effort into packaging (recyclable HDPE tubes) and into buying challenger brands. Their own tablet launches are small, niche or "travel" SKUs. Colgate is the only major with a clearly marketed toothpaste tablet in North America ("Colgate Anywhere / CO. by Colgate" tabs), and Haleon has a 2022-priority toothpaste-tablet patent with no confirmed product. I found no evidence of Crest/Oral-B tablets or foam formats in Western markets.

### Cited Findings
**Colgate-Palmolive**
- Colgate sells its own tablet SKU. "CO. by Colgate On-The-Go Toothpaste Tabs" were listed at $14 for a one-month supply in a 2026 review roundup — [Fluoride Action Network repost of a review roundup](https://fluoridealert.org/news/toothpaste-tablets-are-more-eco-friendly-than-traditional-tubes-and-convenient-for-travel-here-are-the-best-ones-weve-tested/); [Dental Roundup 2026](https://www.dentalroundup.com/best/best-toothpaste-tablets/)
- Clinical evidence: a 2023 *American Journal of Dentistry* study (n=40 adults, 2 weeks) compared Colgate Anywhere Travel Toothpaste Tablets with a conventional NaF paste. Both groups improved in plaque control, with no statistically significant difference in gingival or plaque index. Participants rated the tablets' "texture/foaminess" more negatively — [Loma Linda University Dentistry research summary, Aug 2023 (PDF)](https://dentistry.llu.edu/sites/dentistry.llu.edu/files/2023-08/research-aug-2023.pdf)
- Colgate's own consumer page says "Not a lot of research has been conducted to compare the effectiveness of toothpaste tablets to traditional toothpaste" (as quoted in) — [Dental Roundup 2026](https://www.dentalroundup.com/best/best-toothpaste-tablets/)
- A separate registered trial on "Efficacy of Eco-Friendly Toothpaste Tablets Versus Conventional Toothpaste Using PI and GI Index" also exists — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05805865](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05805865)
- A market report claims Colgate launched a "Smile for Good" tablet brand in September 2021 in a recyclable glass jar. This is unverified and probably confuses it with Colgate's "Smile for Good" recyclable-tube toothpaste; treat with caution — [SkyQuest market report](https://www.skyquestt.com/report/toothpaste-tablet-market)
- Colgate showed the tablet-plus-refill model works for it in an adjacent category. Softsoap Foaming Hand Soap Tablets with a refillable aluminium bottle launched in 2021, billed as the first refill in tablet form from a major U.S. brand: starter kit $5.99, 3-tablet refill $4.99, 71% less plastic per the company — [Drug Store News](https://drugstorenews.com/colgate-palmolive-intros-softsoap-foaming-hand-soap-tablets); [Packaging Insights](https://www.packaginginsights.com/news/colgate-palmolive-launches-hand-soap-tablets-and-refillable-aluminum-bottle-for-softsoap-range.html)
- Colgate bought the challenger Hello Products (natural/fluoride-free-optional, charcoal and similar) in 2020. Inc. reports a $351M sale price — [Colgate IR press release](https://investor.colgatepalmolive.com/news-releases/news-release-details/colgate-announces-agreement-acquire-hello-oral-care-brand); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202304/kevin-j-ryan/how-avoiding-an-exit-strategy-led-hello-products-to-a-351-million-sale.html); [Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/news/article_page/Colgate-Palmolive_acquires_subscription_oral_care_brand_Hello_Products/161815)
- The founder (Craig Dubitsky) stayed on at Colgate as chief innovation strategist — [Beauty Independent](https://www.beautyindependent.com/hello-products-craig-dubitsky-colgate-expanding-into-cbd/)

**Unilever (Signal, Pepsodent, Closeup)**
- Unilever committed to recyclable tubes for its oral-care brands by 2025, starting in France (Signal) and India in 2021 after four years of development — [Unilever press release 2021](https://www.unilever.com/news/press-and-media/press-releases/2021/unilever-to-introduce-recyclable-toothpaste-tubes/); [Packaging Gateway](https://www.packaging-gateway.com/news/unilever-toothpaste/)
- Fast Company (2019) reported Unilever would pilot toothpaste in chewable tablet form — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90414141/how-consumer-products-giant-unilever-plans-to-dramatically-slash-its-use-of-plastic). I found no evidence that the pilot scaled.
- Grand View Research claims "In March 2021, Unilever acquired Bite." **This looks wrong.** No press release or business outlet confirms it, and Fast Company (2020) said Bite's founders kept 100% ownership despite offers — [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/toothpaste-tablet-market-report) vs. [Fast Company 2020](https://www.fastcompany.com/90526016/most-creative-people-2020-lindsay-mccormick)

**Haleon (Sensodyne, Parodontax)**
- Haleon UK IP Ltd filed a toothpaste-tablet patent: WO2024041876A1 / EP 4 577 311 A1, "Toothpaste Tablet Composition," GB priority 25 Aug 2022, PCT filed 7 Aug 2023, EP published 2 Jul 2025. It covers an optional fluoride source and a formulation that "overcomes the previous sensorial problems associated with currently commercially available toothpaste tablets" — [EPO publication server](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP4577311NWA1/document.html); [Google Patents WO2024041876A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024041876A1/en)
- Current Sensodyne and Parodontax FDA listings show only conventional paste formats (for example, a Dec 2025 Sensodyne Cavity & Sensitivity listing) — [hellopharmacist NDC listing](https://hellopharmacist.com/ndc/00135-4032-01)

**Kenvue (Listerine) and the Kimberly-Clark deal**
- Kimberly-Clark agreed to acquire Kenvue (Listerine, among other brands) for about $48.7B, announced Nov 2025. Shareholders of both companies approved it on 29 Jan 2026, and closing is expected in H2 2026 subject to regulators. I found no confirmation that it has closed — [Cosmetics Design Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2025/11/04/kimberly-clark-to-acquire-kenvue-in-487b-deal); [Kimberly-Clark 8-K 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/55785/000110465926008005/tm262467d8_ex99-1.htm)
- Listerine discontinued a range of products in the past, including its toothpaste line — [Listerine discontinued products page](https://www.listerine.com/products/discontinued-products)

**P&G (Crest, Oral-B)**
- No search surfaced a Crest or Oral-B toothpaste tablet, foam or refill product in the US or EU (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Incumbents treat tablets as a travel/eco side SKU, not a core platform. The Colgate trial points to the core consumer barrier: tablets clean as well as paste, but the sensory experience (texture, foam) is worse. Haleon's patent explicitly targets that sensory gap, which suggests a major may relaunch tablets once the sensory problem is solved. An entrant's head start on sensory quality may only last 2–4 years.
- The incumbents' playbook is to let challengers prove a format or benefit and then buy (Hello to Colgate; TheraBreath to Church & Dwight).
- Colgate's Softsoap tablet-plus-aluminium-refill model is a ready-made template for an oral-care refill system at mass price points.

### Gaps
- Crest/Oral-B (P&G) format experiments: none found. Unclear whether that is because none exist or because search coverage missed them.
- Sales performance of Colgate's tablets, and whether "CO. by Colgate" is still active: not found.
- Signal/Unilever tablet pilot outcome: not found.
- Whether Haleon's patent maps to a planned Sensodyne or Parodontax SKU: not found.

## 2. Startups by format (tablets, powders, foams, drops, strips, hydroxyapatite, beauty crossover, probiotics)

### Takeaway
The challenger field divides into three groups: (a) eco-format tablet brands, mostly fluoride-free, small, and bootstrapped or crowdfunded (Bite, Lush, Denttabs, Georganics); (b) benefit-led paste brands that won retail distribution with nano-hydroxyapatite or microbiome claims (Boka, RiseWell, Apagard, Evenmouth, Viome); and (c) heavily funded "beauty/lifestyle" oral-care brands built mainly on devices and whitening (quip, Hismile, Spotlight, Moon). Tablets attracted press but little reported capital. Tablets are a small niche; third-party market estimates for the "Bite-type toothpaste bits" segment are only about US$30–35M (low-quality sources).

### Inferences
- Tablets' structural weaknesses show up in every source: worse texture and foam (the Colgate RCT), frequent absence of fluoride (dentist pushback, Shark Tank objection), and plastic bottles in some cases (Lush). Bite's move to nHAp and Denttabs' fluoride positioning are both answers to the "no fluoride" objection. Price per use (~$0.08–0.25 per tab) is several times that of mass paste.
- Benefit-led brands (nHAp, microbiome) are winning mass retail (Boka at Walmart and Target) while format-led brands stay niche. **Benefit beats format** as the purchase driver. Format becomes a differentiator only when it carries a benefit.
- "No foam / low foam" is emerging as a deliberate positive claim (Fygg, Gallinée, Apagard Non-Foam), which reframes the tablet "low foam" weakness as an asset for sensitive and microbiome-minded users.
- Regulatory claim risk (NAD action on Boka's "remineralizes") will constrain benefit marketing in the US. The FDA monograph status of nHAp (not an approved anticaries active in the US) means fluoride remains the only claimable anticavity active.

### Gaps
- No reliable data found for: Georganics' own powders and tabs; Ben & Anna; Unpaste; Huppy; Smyle; Weleda tablets; Davids; Dr. Brite; Biomin; Elementa; Marvis; Cocofloss; Suri; Happybrush; Lebon; Theodent; Kinder. Funding, revenue and launch years for these remain unverified.
- No Western foam-toothpaste, strip/film, single-dose capsule or pre-pasted brush brands surfaced in the searches. Formats in these categories appear rare or absent in Western mass retail, but this is unconfirmed.
- No closures were found for Denttabs, Georganics, Lush, Huppy or Unpaste. Failure data is the largest gap.
- Systematic Amazon review-theme mining was not done (fetches failed).

## 3. Patents and technology; contract manufacturers

### Takeaway
Tablet IP is old and crowded (fluoride effervescent and self-foaming tablets date to the 1980s). Recent novelty centres on fixing tablet sensory quality (Haleon 2022 priority). Moisture sensitivity of effervescent tablets forces hermetic packaging, which can undermine eco claims. I found no EU or US contract manufacturer that publicly advertises fluoride toothpaste tablets.

### Cited Findings
- Haleon's WO2024041876A1 / EP4577311A1: a toothpaste-tablet composition with an optional fluoride source, aimed at fixing "sensorial problems" of current tablets; not to be swallowed; GB priority 25 Aug 2022 — [EPO](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP4577311NWA1/document.html); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024041876A1/en)
- Archtek US20120175273A1 (filed 2011): a toothpaste tablet. It notes effervescent tablets need hermetically sealed packaging because moisture starts the effervescence — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20120175273A1/en)
- Aberg US4753792A: a water-free fluoride tooth-cleaning tablet that self-foams when chewed (1980s prior art) — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4753792A/en)
- Block Drug US4267164A (filed 1980): an effervescent stannous fluoride tablet that dissolves into a mouthrinse — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4267164A/en)
- An academic sensory study of toothpaste foam uses rheology and dynamic foam analysis; foam is a measurable sensory driver — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42613000/)
- Lumina shows how far "format" can go into biologics (a one-time GM bacterial application) and the regulatory grey zone that comes with it — [Undark](https://undark.org/2024/04/17/brushing-with-bacteria-lumina/)
- Contract manufacturing: PharmaEurope advertises private-label effervescent tablet compression in the EU (ISO 22000, GMP, HACCP), but for food supplements, with no oral-care or fluoride claims — [PharmaEurope](https://www.pharmaeurope.eu/effervescent-tablet-manufacturing/)

### Inferences
- Freedom to operate for basic fluoride chewable tablets is probably good, given the 1980s prior art has expired. Differentiating IP would need to come from sensory (mouthfeel, foam, disintegration), packaging (moisture barrier in compostable or refill formats) or active-delivery stability (for example nHAp plus fluoride, or stannous stability in a dry matrix; stannous is unstable in water, which favours anhydrous formats).
- **Opportunity:** anhydrous formats (tablet, powder, water-free concentrate) suit actives that degrade in water (stannous fluoride, probiotics, enzymes). This is a technical rationale for format beyond "eco."

### Gaps
- Patents on foams, dissolvable films, encapsulation, freeze-drying or pastilles were not searched successfully (patents.google.com was unreachable).
- Named oral-care CMOs able to produce fluoride tablets or foams in the EU or US (for example, those used by Denttabs (Germany) or Bite) were not identified. Denttabs appears to manufacture in Germany, which suggests a German-based supply route, but this is unverified.

## 4. M&A activity in oral care, 2022–2026

### Takeaway
Strategic M&A in oral care 2022–2026 has been modest and mostly about established brands or portfolio reshaping: Kimberly-Clark/Kenvue (Listerine, pending), Perrigo reviewing its oral care unit, BioSyent/Oral Science, and quip's service bolt-ons. The major benefit-led challenger exits (Hello in 2020, TheraBreath in 2021) predate the window. No large acquisition of a format-led startup (tablet or other) was found.

## 5. White space: under-served format × benefit × segment combinations

### Takeaway
The evidence points to white space where a non-paste format **delivers a functional benefit paste delivers poorly**, not to "eco tablets" as such. The tablet niche is crowded, small and sensory-handicapped. The most promising combinations are: (1) fluoride (or nHAp + fluoride) tablets or anhydrous formats with paste-equivalent sensory quality; (2) low/no-foam formats for sensitive, microbiome-minded or dry-mouth users; (3) anhydrous formats stabilising sensitive actives (stannous, probiotics); (4) kids' fluoride formats with exact dosing; (5) mass-price refill systems modelled on Colgate's Softsoap tablets.

### Cited Findings (evidence base for white-space reasoning)
- The sensory gap is the main tablet barrier (texture and foam rated worse in a Colgate RCT) — [LLU 2023](https://dentistry.llu.edu/sites/dentistry.llu.edu/files/2023-08/research-aug-2023.pdf). A major (Haleon) is patenting a fix — [EPO](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP4577311NWA1/document.html)
- No human trials of any tablet measure cavities, and no studies compare fluoride release from tablets vs. paste (per a 2026 consumer guide) — [Dental Roundup](https://www.dentalroundup.com/best/best-toothpaste-tablets/)
- Fluoride absence is a recurring objection to tablets (Shark Tank on Bite; dentist critique of Lush) — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/marcberman1/2020/04/01/move-over-crest-and-colgatebite-toothpaste-bits-featured-on-shark-tank-is-a-new-alternative/); [Lab Muffin](https://labmuffin.com/lush-toothy-tabs-powder-review/)
- Kids' fluoride tablets at 500 ppm exist only from Denttabs, a small player — [Georganics](https://georganics.com/products/denttabs-toothpaste-tabs-strawberry)
- No-foam positioning: Fygg, Gallinée and Apagard Non-Foam — [Fygg](https://fygg.com/); [Gallinée](https://www.gallinee.com/us/product/prebiotic-toothpaste/); [Apagard](https://apagard.org/)
- Benefit-led brands are scaling at mass retail (Boka retail +500% YTD 2025, Walmart entry; company-reported) — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/boka-launches-probiotic-nano-hydroxyapatite-toothpaste-for-holistic-wellness-302548959.html)
- Drops format exists only at niche DTC (Evenmouth Dental Drops, $30) — [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2023/11/21/Newly-launched-probiotic-toothpaste-targets-oral-microbiome-health/)
- Mass-price tablet-plus-refill model proven by Colgate in hand soap ($5.99 kit / $4.99 refills) — [Drug Store News](https://drugstorenews.com/colgate-palmolive-intros-softsoap-foaming-hand-soap-tablets)
- Effervescent tablets need hermetic moisture-barrier packaging — [US20120175273A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20120175273A1/en)

### Inferences (candidate white-space matrix; author's synthesis, not sourced claims)
| Format | Benefit | Segment | Why under-served |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fluoride chewable tablet with paste-like foam and mouthfeel | Caries prevention + travel/eco | Mainstream eco-curious adults, EU first (fluoride accepted) | Incumbents have only travel SKUs; startups are mostly fluoride-free; the sensory gap is unresolved commercially |
| Anhydrous tablet or powder with stannous fluoride or probiotics | Gum health / sensitivity / microbiome | Adults 35+ (Parodontax/Sensodyne buyers) | Stannous and live cultures degrade in water; no challenger is exploiting the anhydrous stability rationale |
| Low/no-foam gel or tablet | Gentle, SLS-free, microbiome, dry mouth | Sensitive mouths, orthodontic/aligner wearers, older adults, chemo or dry-mouth patients | Only premium niche brands (Fygg, Gallinée) and Japanese imports (Apagard) |
| Pre-dosed kids' tablet or drop with exact fluoride dose | Correct dosing, no swallowing paste | Parents of children 3–10 | Only Denttabs kids; dosing control is a parental pain point (inference) |
| Tablet refill system at mass price (pharmacy and grocery) | Eco without premium | Price-sensitive eco consumers | Tablets cost several times paste per use; Colgate's Softsoap template not yet applied to toothpaste |
| nHAp + fluoride hybrid (any format) | Remineralisation + sensitivity | Users who want "both"; EU market where nHAp is widely sold | US market is polarised (fluoride vs. fluoride-free); NAD scrutiny limits claims |
| Aligner/orthodontic-specific format (foam or tablet for on-the-go) | Cleaning on the go without a sink | Aligner wearers | Spotlight's Invisalign-provider network shows a dentist-led channel exists |

- **Things to avoid:** fluoride-free "eco tablet" undifferentiated by benefit (crowded, small, dentist pushback); celebrity-led whitening (heavily contested by Hismile, Moon and others); unproven GM probiotics (regulatory risk, per Lumina).

### Gaps
- No consumer survey data on format preference (tablet vs. paste vs. foam) in EU or NA was found.
- No market sizing for foams, films, drops or capsules in the West.
- Asian format inspirations beyond Apagard (Korean foam toothpastes, Japanese liquid dentifrices, Chinese mousse formats) were not documented in sources retrieved, and remain to be verified.
- Price-per-use comparisons for mass paste were not sourced (a typical $3–5 / 100 ml tube is common knowledge but not cited here).
