# France: private-label / contract (façonnier) sun care manufacturers, with a focus on low MOQ, SPF foam and "sun water"

> **Method caveat for the report writer (please read):** In this session, `WebFetch` was blocked by the network egress proxy for every company site I tried: cosmetics-insiders.com, europages.fr, abctexture.com, solacos.com, propos-nature.com, cosmetique-marque-blanche.fr, alpol-cosmetique.com, premiumbeautynews.com, proersa.com and skinlys.com. The shared web-search budget (200 calls per turn) also ran out after about 30 of my queries. **Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine results: snippets and quoted excerpts of the cited pages, not from full-page reads.** The URLs are the pages those snippets came from. Treat MOQs and prices as "published claim, verify in writing". Several named candidates could not be checked at all; they are listed under Gaps. No MOQ or price has been invented. Where none was found, the text says "not published, ask".

## 1. Which French companies offer private-label (marque blanche) or contract manufacturing (façonnage) of sun care? Checking the candidate list

### Takeaway
Only a handful of French manufacturers are publicly documented as making sun care for third parties. The strongest are **ALPOL Cosmétique** (Ain; a sun care division since 2015, with catalogue "turnkey" and white-label SPF formulas), **SofIA Cosmétiques** (Carros, near Nice; private label since 1989, with sun care about 35% of its business) and **Fareva** (group-level sun care and aerosols). Most names on the candidate list could not be verified as sun care contract makers. Several look like brands, directories or misspellings, for example "Groupe Inovia" for Innovi and "Cosmetic Labs" for a directory website.

## 3. Low-MOQ / small-batch ("petites séries") routes and their published MOQs and fees

### Takeaway
French small-batch white-label labs publish MOQs from **20–50 units** up to **500–1,000 units**, and one publishes a **€350 per product** turnkey fee that includes CPNP. These labs are general natural or organic cosmetics makers. **None was confirmed to offer an SPF product.** SPF products also carry heavy testing costs that small labs may not have absorbed, so expect sun SKUs to be excluded or to carry separate MOQs and fees.

### Cited Findings
- **Cosmétique Marque Blanche (south of France).** Turnkey formula with "un minimum de 20 produits par référence" (minimum 20 per reference). Another page on the same site says "sans aucun minimum", which contradicts it. A 30% discount applies from 500 units of one reference. — [cosmetique-marque-blanche.fr](https://cosmetique-marque-blanche.fr/)
- **Oomylab.** "Première commande de 50 pièces, puis achat sans minimum de commande, si la formule reste inchangée" (first order 50 pieces, then no minimum while the formula is unchanged). More than 95% natural content; also makes products by the kilo for small and medium runs. — [Oomylab: marque blanche](https://www.oomylab.com/marque-blanche/)

## 4. Who can fill aerosol, bag-on-valve (BOV) or a foamer for an SPF mousse?

### Takeaway
France has several aerosol contract fillers: **Fareva/Interspray, Laboratoires Chemineau, Aerochem, Ardepharm, Aerolub, MJ Conditionnement and Laboratoire M Line**. **None was found advertising an SPF mousse**, and French SPF mousses on the market (Soleilbiafine, Bergasol, Topicrem, SVR) do not name their manufacturer. Technically, a true aerosol *foam* needs liquefied-gas propellant: compressed-air BOV "cannot foam". Aerochem is the only aerosol filler that explicitly says it handles "small to large" runs.

### Inferences
- **SPF mousse in France is likely a two-party job.** A sun formulator with tested SPF chassis (ALPOL, SofIA, Cosmoya) would be paired with an aerosol filler holding a flammable-aerosol permit, because hydrocarbon foam propellants are flammable: Chemineau, Interspray/Fareva, Aerochem or Ardepharm. An exception is a single group with both capabilities, possibly Fareva, or Anjac via Innovi plus Chemineau.
- Compressed-air BOV will *not* give a foam. A BOV route suits a *spray* or *mist*, not a mousse. Note that some "crème mousse" products, such as Topicrem and SVR Blur, may be whipped textures in jars or tubes rather than aerosols. That route avoids aerosol MOQs entirely. This is inferred from product naming and is not confirmed for these SKUs.
- SPF testing must be done on the final aerosol product as dispensed, so an aerosol mousse means a new SPF/UVA test campaign even if the bulk formula is already tested. This is background knowledge, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No MOQ was found for any French aerosol filler. Aerochem's "petite série" is not quantified.
- Lindal France (Briey) BOV filling and "Aérosol Développement" were **not verified**: the search budget was exhausted.
- No source names who fills Soleilbiafine, Bergasol Blur Mousse or other French SPF foams.
- No French supplier was found offering a *non-aerosol* foamer-pump SPF.

## 6. Testing and regulatory points to put to any French supplier (supporting context)

### Takeaway
Before relying on a stock SPF formula, the client needs the supplier's **SPF in vivo report (ISO 24444:2019)**, **UVA report (ISO 24443 in vitro or ISO 24442 in vivo)** and, for water-resistance claims, an **ISO 18861 / in vivo water-resistance** report, all on the exact formula and format. SPF 50+ results vary widely between labs. Only Labo Heme publicly bundles CPNP and DIP work into its white-label fee.

### Cited Findings
- SPF in vivo testing follows ISO 24444:2019. UVA testing follows ISO 24442 (in vivo) or ISO 24443 (in vitro). One lab notes the in-vitro method is not suited to powders or mineral filters. — [CertifiedCosmetics: SPF testing](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/blog/cosmetics-laboratory-testing/sunscreen-efficacy-and-spf-testing/); [Acorelle blog](https://www.acorelle.fr/blog/comment-mesurer-lefficacite-des-produits-solaires-bio--n21)
- Water and sweat resistance claims are tested under ISO 18861 (in vivo). — [CertifiedCosmetics: sweat resistance test](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/product/spf-testing/sweat-resistance-spf-laboratory-test-in-vivo-sun-protection-factor/)
- ISO 23675 (published 2024) is a newer in-vitro SPF method that SGS markets as faster and cheaper; that is a provider claim. — [SGS, Dec 2025](https://www.sgs.com/fr-fr/actualites/2025/12/tests-sur-les-produits-solaires)
- Inter-lab SPF discrepancies are "particularly striking for sunscreens with an SPF50 or SPF50+". — [PMC7540522](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7540522/)
- The DIP (product information file) is mandatory before placing a product on the market. Labo Heme prepares one for each white-label product and includes the CPNP notification in its €350 fee. — [Labo Heme](https://labo-heme.net/marque-blanche)
- Sprays and aerosols are recognised forms of sun protection, and only the UV filters authorised by the Cosmetics Regulation (about 30) may be used. — [DGCCRF: protection solaire](https://economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques-et-les-faq/protection-solaire-les-conseils-pour-bien-choisir-son)

### Inferences
- For a low-fixed-cost launch, the key negotiating point is whether the supplier will **license or transfer its existing ISO test reports and safety assessment** for a stock SPF formula to the client's brand. That determines whether the client pays for new SPF/UVA testing, which is a major fixed cost.
- A format change (cream to mousse, or to sun water) normally triggers new tests. This is background knowledge, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No French supplier was found to state publicly that its stock SPF formulas include transferable ISO 24444/24443/water-resistance reports, or that it acts as EU Responsible Person for client brands. **Ask each supplier.**
