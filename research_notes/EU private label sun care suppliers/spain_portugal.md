# Spain and Portugal: private-label and contract manufacturers of sun care (SPF), with a focus on low MOQ, SPF mousse and "sun water" sprays

**How these notes were gathered (read first).** The network proxy blocked every direct page fetch in this session, including valquer.com, carasalab.com, labesp.com, proersa.com, romarglobalcare.com, promcosmetics.com, skingreen.pt and valenciaplaza.com. All findings below therefore come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from reading each page in full. The session's web-search budget also ran out before every candidate could be checked; the unchecked names are listed under Gaps in Q1. Claims are attributed either to the company's own site (its own marketing) or to third-party listings, and the two are kept apart. Several "top manufacturers" lists (Xiran, Metro Private Label, Rixin, Ausmetics) are published by competing factories in China or elsewhere. They are used only as leads and are labelled where cited. Unknown MOQs and prices are marked "not published — ask".

## Q1. Which companies in Spain and Portugal offer private-label or contract manufacturing of sunscreens? Verification of the candidate list

### Takeaway
Only a handful of Iberian companies have **confirmed, current, sunscreen-specific** private-label or contract offers. Valquer Laboratorios (Toledo) is the strongest fit for a small D2C brand. CarasaLab (Gipuzkoa) is the next. For aerosol SPF formats there are Proersa Aerosoles (Barcelona) and Romar Global Care (Valencia, owner of the Agrado brand). Laboratorios Magriña (Barcelona) lists sun care in directories. In Portugal, only Fancystage (Trofa) has sun care listed. Several names on the brief turned out to be depilatory, perfume, pharma or captive-retailer makers, and are excluded or unconfirmed.

### Inferences
- The realistic low-MOQ shortlist in Spain is **Valquer** (no mandatory minimum, about 1,000 units per reference), **CarasaLab** (1,000 units per reference) and possibly **LABESP** (from 1 unit for stock items, if it has an SPF base). Proersa and RGC are the only confirmed aerosol routes, and their MOQs are unpublished and likely higher. That is an inference from their scale; RGC's Sagunto plant is rated at over 180M units/yr.
- Portugal's private-label ecosystem is skewed towards skincare and solid cosmetics. Only Fancystage shows sun care, and its own positioning favours established brands, so Portugal looks weaker than Spain for a small SPF launch.
- Maverick, Belloch, Cosmewax and Mixer & Pack are large retailer-own-label suppliers. Even where they can make sun care, they are unlikely to suit a startup's minimum-fixed-cost goal; this is an inference from their client lists and capacity.

## Q2. Company-by-company detail: location, formats, MOQ, stock vs custom, SPF test data, fees, lead times, EU RP/CPNP/CPSR, packaging, fulfilment, URL

### Takeaway
Only Valquer, CarasaLab, LABESP and Prom Cosmetics publish MOQs. Valquer says it has no mandatory minimum and is "particularly attractive from 1,000 units per reference", and it is the only sun-care maker that explicitly says it registers products on CPNP **in its own name as Responsible Person**. That matters for a brand wanting minimum fixed cost. **No Iberian supplier was found publishing whether its stock SPF bases come with existing ISO 24444, ISO 24443 or water-resistance reports.** None publishes development fees or unit prices for sun care.

Agrado Sun Care "duplica su catálogo": to its best-selling SPF 50+ facial mist and SPF 50+ mousse it adds four references — [Valencia Plaza](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/agrado-sun-care-duplica-su-catalogo-de-solares); [Alimarket: Agrado Sun Care](https://www.alimarket.es/nonfood/noticia/423706/-agrado-sun-care--apuesta-por-la-innovacion-y-la-especializacion-por-target)

**Laboratorios Magriña (Spain)**

Has served supermarkets, pharmacy chains, perfumery groups and clothing brands — [FindMyManufacturer](https://findmymanufacturer.com/manufacturer/fancystage/); [ListCompany](https://www.listcompany.org/Fancystage_Unipessoal_Lda_Info.html)
- MOQ "quotation required"; described as a better fit for brands with established distribution than for founders wanting about 100 units. Certification is listed as "not provided" — [FindMyManufacturer](https://findmymanufacturer.com/manufacturer/fancystage/); [eWorldTrade](https://www.eworldtrade.com/c/fancystageunipessoal/)

**Prom Cosmetics (Portugal; sun care not confirmed)**
- Porto de Mós. Private label MOQ is 1,000 units per reference (≥50 ml) and 1,500 (<50 ml); white label is 500 units per reference. **SPF testing context (relevant to stock-formula due diligence)**
- AEMPS (Spain's medicines and cosmetics agency) ran an in-vivo SPF market-control campaign using a lab experienced in both versions of ISO 24444. It asked companies to withdraw products voluntarily where results did not support the label. One SPF 50 batch measured a mean of 47.1 (SD ±7.3) and was later allowed back after new studies — [AEMPS report PDF](https://www.aemps.gob.es/informa/notasInformativas/cosmeticos-cuidado-personal/2021/Informe_AEMPS-an%C3%A1lisis_FPS_de_productos_solares.pdf?x39768=); [AEMPS campaign update](https://www.aemps.gob.es/informa/actualizacion-de-la-informacion-de-la-campana-de-control-de-mercado-de-productos-de-proteccion-solar/)

### Inferences
- For minimum fixed cost, Valquer's model is the closest to "white-label with the factory as Responsible Person". It combines turnkey service, no hard minimum, an existing in-house sun line and CPNP registration in its own name. This could avoid the brand paying separately for an RP, a CPSR and possibly new SPF testing, **if** the client launches an existing Valquer sun formula. Whether the existing SPF/UVA reports transfer to a white-label SKU must be confirmed in writing.
- If a stock SPF base comes with existing ISO 24444 and ISO 24443 reports that the brand can reference, the brand avoids the largest fixed cost of a new SPF launch. No Iberian supplier states this publicly, so it is the first question to ask every supplier.
- Prom Cosmetics' €7,000 minimum investment for a 1,000-unit skincare project is the only published Iberian cost floor found. It is a rough benchmark, not sun care, and excludes SPF testing.

### Gaps
- No Iberian supplier publishes: unit prices, development or sampling fees, SPF/UVA/water-resistance test costs, whether stock SPF bases carry transferable ISO 24444/24443/water-resistance reports, CPSR cost, or fulfilment/dropship services.
- Lead times are published only by Proersa (6–8 weeks) and Prom (about 90 days, not sun-specific).
- Fulfilment or dropship: none of the sun-care makers found advertise D2C order fulfilment.

## Q3. Which can do aerosol, bag-on-valve or foamer packaging for an SPF mousse?

### Takeaway
**Romar Global Care (Agrado)** is the only Iberian company found that already sells an **SPF 50+ mousse**. Its packaging (aerosol vs mechanical foamer) is not confirmed. **Proersa Aerosoles** is the clearest contract filler with confirmed **bag-on-valve with compressed air for sunscreens**, and it also fills foams such as shaving foam. No Iberian private-label lab was found offering a ready-made SPF mousse base at low MOQ.

### Cited Findings
- Agrado's Mousse Facial Solar SPF 50+ (75 ml) is vegan, water-resistant and alcohol-free, with a creamy texture. Primor's spec sheet says "Formato Spray", and one reviewer says it is "algo espeso, como una crema solar" for a mousse — [Primor](https://www.primor.eu/es_ic/agrado-mousse-facial-solar-spf50-55127.html); [Druni](https://www.druni.es/mousse-facial-solar-spf-50-agrado-mousse-facial-resistente-al-agua); [BigBuy](https://bigbuy.eu/en/sun-screen-mousse-agrado-75-ml_533151.html)
- Agrado is a Quimi Romar / Romar Global Care brand — [Druni Agrado brand page](https://www.druni.es/marcas/agrado); [Alimarket](https://www.alimarket.es/nonfood/noticia/423706/-agrado-sun-care--apuesta-por-la-innovacion-y-la-especializacion-por-target)
- RGC fills aerosols for third parties and retailer own-label, with custom formula development — [RGC aerosol manufacturing](https://romarglobalcare.com/en/fabricantes-productos-en-aerosol/); [RGC MDD](https://romarglobalcare.com/en/fabricantes-marcas-para-distribucion/)
- Proersa: "Gracias al sistema de válvula de bolsillo [bag-on-valve], simplemente utilizamos aire comprimido como propulsor. Lo usamos para geles de afeitar, protectores solares, pero también aguas termales." It says large cosmetics multinationals already use BoV for sun-protection aerosols — [Proersa blog: Bag on Valve](https://www.proersa.com/blog/envasado-aerosoles-ecologicos/); [Proersa blog: gases](https://www.proersa.com/blog/envasado-de-aerosoles/)
- Proersa's cosmetics line includes "espuma de afeitar" (shaving foam) and "sun spray" — [Proersa blog](https://www.proersa.com/blog/fabricacion-de-aerosoles-cosmeticos/)
- Context: SPF mousses on Spanish shelves come mostly from non-Iberian brands (Rituals foaming SPF 50 200 ml at about €19; SVR Sun Secure Blur; EVY Technology). Manufacturing origin was not identified — [Rituals](https://www.rituals.com/es-es/the-ritual-of-karma-foaming-sun-protection-spf-50-200ml-1119979.html); [Druni EVY](https://www.druni.es/sunscreen-mousse-spf50-evy-technology-protector-solar-rostro-y-cuerpo)
- Outside scope (France), flagged for other researchers: Trenois publishes a technical sheet for a "mousse écran solaire SPF50" in a 200 ml aluminium aerosol — [Trenois fiche technique](https://media.trenois.com/static/mousse-ecran-solaire-spf50_vei042_fiche-technique.pdf)

### Inferences
- The most direct Iberian route to an SPF mousse would be asking RGC to white-label or adapt the Agrado SPF 50+ mousse formula. Alternatively, Proersa could fill a client-owned or co-developed SPF mousse concentrate in an aerosol or BoV. Both are likely to carry higher MOQs than Valquer or CarasaLab; this is inferred from aerosol-line economics, and no MOQ is published.
- A non-aerosol pump-foamer SPF mousse would need a lab with foamable SPF chassis expertise. None of the low-MOQ labs (Valquer, CarasaLab, LABESP) advertise this.
- Aerosol SPF mousse adds pressurised-container compliance and propellant compatibility, and needs SPF testing on the finished aerosol format. Proersa says sun-spray regulation is the longest of all cosmetics.

### Gaps
- Whether Agrado's mousse is an aerosol, BoV or mechanical foamer is unconfirmed, as is whether RGC offers it as private label.
- MOQs for aerosol or BoV filling at Proersa or RGC are not published — ask.
- No evidence was found of any Portuguese aerosol or BoV filler with sun care.

## Q4. Which can do hydroalcoholic or water-thin "sun water" (transparent SPF spray, Lancaster Sun Protective Water type) sprays?

### Takeaway
No Iberian private-label maker was found publicly offering a transparent, hydroalcoholic "sun water" SPF base. The closest capabilities are Proersa (DME propellant aerosols, "commonly used for sun or hydroalcoholic sprays"), RGC/Agrado (an aerosol SPF 50+ facial mist) and Valquer (own SPF 30/50 trigger sprays, one sold as "Agua Solar").

### Cited Findings
- Proersa on DME propellant: "Se usa comúnmente para solares o productos con una alta concentración de agua, tipo spray hidro alcohólico" — [Proersa blog: aerosol gases](https://www.proersa.com/blog/envasado-de-aerosoles/)
- Proersa fills thermal-water sprays and sun sprays, including BoV with compressed air (non-propellant, suits thin liquids) — [Proersa blog: Bag on Valve](https://www.proersa.com/blog/envasado-aerosoles-ecologicos/); [Proersa blog](https://www.proersa.com/blog/fabricacion-de-aerosoles-cosmeticos/)
- RGC/Agrado: award-winning "Bruma Facial Solar SPF 50+", ultralight and water-resistant, applied over make-up — [Valencia Plaza](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/tierra-de-empresas/agrado-recibe-el-premio-al-aerosol-mas-innovador-del-ano-por-su-bruma-facial-de-la-mano-de-aeda)
- Valquer: own SPF 30 and SPF 50 "spray hidratante" 300 ml in a "pulverizador" (pump spray); one retailer calls the body SPF 50 "Sun Valisol Agua Solar". Search results found **no** Valquer sun "bruma" or biphasic sun product; its biphasic items are hair conditioners — [Solopelos](https://www.solopelos.com/solares/2303-valisol-spray-protector-solar-50-spf-hidratante-300-ml.html); [Marta Masi](https://www.martamasi.com/valquer-protector-solar-corporal-spf50-alta-proteccion-e-hidratacion-300-ml.html); [Valquer solares](https://www.valquer.com/en/collections/solares)
- Mercadona's own-label range includes refreshing body sprays and a water-resistant SPF 30 "bruma solar", so a retailer-own-label mist supply chain exists in Spain. Its manufacturer is not identified — [Merca2](https://www.merca2.es/2026/07/26/protector-solar-mercadona-comparativa-2424277/); [Vozpópuli](https://www.vozpopuli.com/economia/distribucion/mercadona-lo-ha-vuelto-a-hacer-recupera-la-bruma-solar-protectora-y-resistente-al-agua-que-ex-un-exito-de-ventas-cada-verano.html)

### Inferences
- A Lancaster-style transparent sun water needs a non-emulsion, solvent-borne filter system, often ethanol-based (general formulation knowledge, not sourced). Among the Iberian candidates, this fits an aerosol or BoV filler (Proersa, RGC) or a custom-development project at CarasaLab or Valquer better than any published stock base.
- Valquer's "Agua Solar" naming may only be a retailer label; whether that product is a clear water-thin spray or a milky lotion spray is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No source confirms that any Iberian lab has a stock transparent or hydroalcoholic SPF 50 spray with existing ISO 24444/24443 data.
- The manufacturers behind Spanish retailer own-label SPF mists (Mercadona, Lidl, Carrefour) could not be identified; the Alimarket chart data was not accessible.
- Portuguese capability for alcohol-based sprays was not found.
