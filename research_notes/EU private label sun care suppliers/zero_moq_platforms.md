## Q1. Which zero-MOQ or near-zero-MOQ beauty platforms serve Europe, and how do they work (country, MOQ, ordering, Shopify, fees, pricing, shipping origin, EU RP/CPNP)?

### Takeaway
Selfnamed (Latvia; legal entity Cosmetics NORD, SIA) is the only platform found that truly works like Gelato for cosmetics in the EU. It has zero MOQ, prints your label on demand, offers Shopify and WooCommerce dropshipping, ships from the EU, and acts as EU Responsible Person with products already CPNP-notified. Every other "no-MOQ" beauty platform found either ships from North America without EU compliance (Blanka, Supliful, viaGlamour, Jubilee, DR.HC, Rapid Fulfillment) or is a general dropshipper that brands packaging only (CJ Dropshipping, Hertwill, EPROLO). Real EU alternatives start at 12 to 100 units.

### Inferences
- For an EU brand that wants a Gelato-style experience (order one unit, label printed on demand, Shopify sync, EU stock, compliance handled), Selfnamed is effectively the only option found. All the North American no-MOQ platforms either ship from outside the EU with VAT and duty on arrival, or explicitly provide no CPNP/RP. Blanka says so outright, and Supliful does not ship skincare outside the US. Selling those products in the EU would leave the brand without the RP and CPNP it legally needs.
- Under Selfnamed's model the brand rides on Selfnamed's RP and CPNP notification. That is what makes zero MOQ possible, but it also means the brand does not control formula claims or RP identity, and it inherits Selfnamed's claims on the label.
- The realistic EU middle ground is 15 to 100 units (Meine-Eigenmarke, My Private Label, Tojo, Options Ltd, SORELLE). These are web-quote and email workflows, not Shopify-integrated POD.

## Q3. Why do many zero-MOQ platforms exclude sunscreen (testing cost, claims liability, US OTC drug status, per-brand labelling)?

### Takeaway
No platform found states in writing why it excludes SPF. The evidence points to three structural reasons. In the US, any SPF claim makes the product an OTC drug, and most no-MOQ platforms are US-based. Every SPF claim needs efficacy testing tied to a specific formula. And the brand on the label carries liability for the claim. The "~€25k per sample" figure is not a lab fee: it is an average from a research consortium. Commercial ISO 24444 in vivo tests are listed at about $2k per product, though blogs quote $5k to $35k.

### Cited Findings
- US: "All products that claim to provide Broad Spectrum SPF protection are regulated as sunscreen drug products". The rules also apply to "cosmetics and moisturizers labeled with SPF values." — [FDA Q&A on OTC sunscreens](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/understanding-over-counter-medicines/questions-and-answers-fda-announces-new-requirements-over-counter-otc-sunscreen-products-marketed-us) (the FDA page is several years old).
- EU: sunscreens are cosmetics under Regulation (EC) 1223/2009. EU guidance requires UVA-PF of at least 1/3 of the labelled SPF and a critical wavelength of 370 nm, and products below SPF 6 cannot be labelled as sunscreens. — [European Commission document](https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/13250/attachments/1/translations/en/renditions/pdf)
- Anyone placing a cosmetic on the market under their own name or brand becomes the Responsible Person: they must notify CPNP, hold a safety assessment and PIF, and print the RP name and address on the label. — [AGES (Austria)](https://www.ages.at/mensch/kosmetik/kosmetik-informationen-fuer-unternehmen); [BVL (Germany): Notifizierung](https://www.bvl.bund.de/DE/Arbeitsbereiche/03_Verbraucherprodukte/03_AntragstellerUnternehmen/02_Kosmetik/01_Notifizierung/bgs_fuerAntragsteller_kosmetik_Notifizierung_node.html)
- SPF test cost, commercial: Certified Cosmetics lists an in vivo SPF test (ISO 24444:2019) at "$1 944.91 through $2 127.04" and an in vitro SPF test at $529.16. — [Certified Cosmetics: in vivo SPF](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/product/spf-testing/spf-test-sun-protection-factor/); [Certified Cosmetics: in vitro SPF](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/product/efficacy-testing/skin-testing/spf-laboratory-test-in-vitro-sun-protection-factor/)
- SPF test cost, the "€25k" origin: Euro Cosmetics Magazine divides an assumed €800,000 spend by the 32 samples tested in the ALT-SPF consortium, giving "an average of € 25'000 per SPF sample". It is a research-project average, and the article notes "The costs vary from test institute to test institute." — [Euro Cosmetics Magazine: SPF testing methods](https://www.eurocosmetics-mag.com/spf-testing-methods-scientific-and-statistical-basis/)
- SPF test cost, blog estimates: Vizor estimates $5,000 to $15,000 per study. Another consumer-brand blog claims $15,000 to $35,000 per product for in vivo testing, which is low reliability and inconsistent with vendor prices. — [Vizor](https://www.vizorsun.com/blog-in-vitro-vs-in-vivo-spf-testing-methods/); [World of Asaya](https://worldofasaya.com/blogs/sun-protection/in-vivo-vs-in-vitro-spf-testing-complete-guide)
- Changing economics: ISO approved new methods for 2025. An expert expects the in vitro ISO 23675 method to cut costs "from several thousand euros or dollars to just a few hundred". — [Cosmetics & Toiletries](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/testing/sun-protection/news/22929537/2025-will-see-faster-more-ethical-and-accurate-spf-testing-iso-approves-two-new-methods)
- Dropshipping economics: "suppliers would price themselves out of the market if they had to arrange lab testing for products that may, at some point, be ordered". Many dropshipped products are untested, so compliance is not verified. — [ComplianceGate](https://www.compliancegate.com/dropshipping-product-safety-compliance-risks/)
- Platform-side evidence of the same burden: Blanka provides no CPNP documentation, and Supliful blocks skincare shipments outside the US. — [Blanka FAQ: EU](https://faq.blankabrand.com/en/articles/11956170-can-products-be-legally-sold-in-the-european-union-eu); [Supliful Help: shipping](https://help.supliful.com/en/articles/5660488-how-to-set-up-shipping-rates-in-shopify)
- Price signal of SPF burden at a low-MOQ EU maker: Meine-Eigenmarke sets 30 units for SPF30 but 500 units for SPF50. Custom development costs about €1,500 to €5,000 "including all tests and approvals" (this applies to its general development, not SPF specifically). — [Meine-Eigenmarke: Kosmetikprodukte](https://www.meine-eigenmarke.de/fuspflege/kosmetikprodukte/); [Meine-Eigenmarke: costs](https://www.meine-eigenmarke.de/kosten-ablaeufe-eigenmarke/)

### Inferences
- US-based no-MOQ platforms (Blanka, Supliful, viaGlamour, Jubilee, DR.HC) would need FDA OTC drug compliance (drug establishment registration, drug facts labelling and monograph filters) for any SPF SKU, which plausibly explains the absence. That is my inference; none of them says so.
- Selfnamed can sell SPF because, in the EU, sunscreen is a cosmetic and Selfnamed holds RP status and the formula's test data centrally. The brand only swaps the label artwork, so testing is paid once per formula, not per brand.
- The client's "~€25k per sample" assumption should be corrected. The figure is an academic or consortium average. Commercial in vivo SPF quotes are in the low thousands per product, before UVA (ISO 24443), water-resistance and stability testing, which add further costs (amounts not sourced here).

### Gaps
- No platform's written policy on excluding SPF was found.
- I found no EU-lab euro price list for ISO 24444, ISO 24443 or water-resistance testing. Request quotes.

## Q4. Low-MOQ "starter" programmes (about 50 to 500 units) between POD and contract manufacturing, and whether they include SPF

### Takeaway

Cosmacon/Tojo offers SPF30 and SPF50 from 100 units (one older page says 25). Other EU low-MOQ makers (Options Ltd at 12 units, My Private Label at 25, MLNT Lab at 50, SORELLE at 100) were found, but their SPF offer is unconfirmed.
