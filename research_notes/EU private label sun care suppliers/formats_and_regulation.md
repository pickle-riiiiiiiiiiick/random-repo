## 1. How European SPF mousses are made, and who makes or fills them (incl. Evy Technology)

### Takeaway
Every SPF "mousse" or "foam" found on the European market is a **classic pressurised aerosol foam**. That means an emulsion with liquefied hydrocarbon propellant (butane, propane or isobutane) in a metal can. None of them uses bag-on-valve or a non-aerosol foamer pump. Evy Technology (Sweden) dominates the category. Its actual filler/manufacturer **could not be confirmed**. A single retailer listing names "Celsus Sweden AB, Torsby", but no company by that name could be found in Swedish registers. No evidence was found that Evy offers private label or licensing. However, two Swedish pharmacy own-label mousses (Apotek Hjärtat and Apotea) appear to share one formula. That is a lead for who supplies own-label SPF mousse in the Nordics.

### Inferences
- **The working format is a hydrocarbon aerosol.** Evy, sunozon, Hjärtat/Apotea, Biafine and Veidec all use metal aerosol cans with butane, propane or isobutane. This implies a client mousse would also be a classic aerosol emulsion foam.
- **An aerosol format brings costs that a pump bottle does not:**
  - CLP flammability labelling.
  - Dangerous-goods shipping (ADR road and IATA air limits), which matters for D2C parcel post. Lab Muffin's customs confiscations illustrate this.
  - EU Aerosol Dispensers Directive 75/324/EEC compliance and pressure testing. This comes from background knowledge and was not verified in this session.
  - Can, valve and actuator MOQs, typically in the thousands.
- **Bag-on-valve does not naturally make foam.** In BOV the product sits in a bag and the propellant (air or nitrogen) stays outside it. A BOV "foam" needs a **post-foaming gel**, with a volatile foaming agent inside the bag. That is what Wear Valley describes. It would be a *new* formula needing its own SPF test, not a refill of an existing mousse.
- **Non-aerosol foamer pumps are a poor fit (unverified).** These pumps (Rieke/Airspray, Albéa, Aptar and others) generally need a thin, surfactant-rich liquid. High-oil, high-filter sunscreen emulsions are hard to foam that way, and **no European SPF product using a foamer pump was found**. This is formulation background knowledge, not verified in this session.
- **The strongest lead for an existing SPF mousse dossier is in the Nordics.** Two Swedish pharmacy own labels share a 100% INCI match, and Apotea's copy mirrors Evy's "unique technology" language. So either Evy itself, or the factory that fills Evy, may already supply own-label pharmacy mousse. **This is a hypothesis.** Verify it by reading the "Tillverkad för/av" line on the Apotea and Hjärtat cans, or by asking Evy directly.
- Similarly, Rossmann's sunozon foam is made for Rossmann by an unnamed contract filler, probably German or EU (inference). Its identity would appear only on the can or in the CPNP and RP records.

Also open: whether Evy fills in-house, the patent number, the applicant, and whether Evy licenses its technology or offers private label. Espacenet, PRV, Bolagsverket and evy.com could not be reached (blocked or no results). Suggested check: Espacenet applicant search for "EVY" or inventor "Pinto", plus a direct inquiry to Evy.
- The fillers named in the brief were **not researched** because the search budget ran out: ASM Aerosol-Service AG (CH), Lindal (valves), Coster, Swallowfield, Aeropres, Ampack, Aerofill, Arrow. Foamer-pump suppliers (Rieke/Airspray, Albéa, Aptar) were also not researched.

- The filler behind Rossmann sunozon, Hjärtat/Apotea and Biafine's foam is not identified.

## 2. "Sun water": what Lancaster Sun Protective Water is, who makes it, and European equivalents and stock formulas

### Takeaway
Lancaster's Sun Protective Water is a **bi-phase, shake-before-use, water-like spray**, not a true clear solution. Its base is water plus a light emollient (dicaprylyl carbonate) carrying organic UV filters. It is made by Coty in Monaco: no sale of the brand or the plant was found up to 2024, but 2025–26 status was not verified. Comparable "sun waters" and transparent mists exist from Caudalie, Laboratorios Babé, La Roche-Posay, Vichy, Nivea and Rossmann sunozon. **No private-label lab advertising a stock "sun water" with SPF data was found.**

### Inferences
- **"Sun water" is a texture and marketing category, not a defined technology.** Lancaster's is a two-phase lotion: water phase plus dicaprylyl-carbonate oil phase with dissolved organic filters, shaken before spraying. Others, such as LRP and Vichy "invisible" mists, are typically alcohol-based clear sprays (background knowledge, not verified here).
- **A bi-phase product is technically simple, but its SPF depends on the dose delivered after shaking.** It is also sensitive to the spray device, which is why the device should be fixed before SPF testing (see section 3).
- **The newer in vitro method may not fit a bi-phase product.** ISO 23675's scope is reported as "emulsions or alcoholic one-phase formulations" (section 3), so a bi-phase sun water may *not* qualify for the cheaper in vitro double-plate method. Confirm with a test lab.

## 3. EU regulation of SPF claims and testing (2006/647/EC; ISO 24444/24443/16217; ISO 23675/23698 status 2025–26)

### Takeaway
EU sunscreens are cosmetics, but SPF is a **quantified efficacy claim**. It must be proven by standardised testing on the **exact finished formula**, and EU guidance and an industry recommendation set minimum performance and labelling rules. Since late 2024, two non-human ISO SPF methods exist, and in June 2025 Cosmetics Europe began recommending them in preference. In vivo methods still dominate for water resistance and for forms outside the in vitro scope.

### Cited Findings
- **Commission Recommendation 2006/647/EC** was adopted 22 Sep 2006 (OJ L 265, 26.9.2006, pp. 39–43). — [EUR-Lex ELI](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reco/2006/647/oj/eng); [Regjeringen EEA note](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/sub/eos-notatbasen/notatene/2006/des/anbefaling-2006647ef/id2430506/)
- It is non-binding but is "the main document to consider when placing sunscreen products on the EU market". — [Biorius](https://biorius.com/regulatory/sunscreen-products)
- **Minimum efficacy:** SPF ≥ 6, **UVA-PF ≥ 1/3 of labelled SPF**, and **critical wavelength ≥ 370 nm**. — [Recommendation text (Croatian MoH copy)](https://zdravlje.gov.hr/UserDocsImages//2018%20Sanitarna//RECOMMENDATION%20EC%20on%20the%20efficacy%20of%20sunscreen%20products%20and%20the%20claims.pdf)
- **Label categories:** "low", "medium", "high", "very high". In vitro methods are to be preferred "where they deliver equivalent results". — (same source)
- **Banned claims:** no total-protection claims. Recital 11 notes that no sunscreen filters all UV and that melanoma prevention is unproven. Secondary sources list "sunblock", "sunblocker", "total protection" and "all-day prevention" as not allowed. — [Recommendation text](https://zdravlje.gov.hr/UserDocsImages//2018%20Sanitarna//RECOMMENDATION%20EC%20on%20the%20efficacy%20of%20sunscreen%20products%20and%20the%20claims.pdf); [Obelis](https://www.obelis.net/news/stricter-labelling-principles-for-sunscreens-in-europe/). Colipa (now Cosmetics Europe) has discouraged "sunblock" since 2002. — [IKW](https://www.ikw.org/fileadmin/IKW_Dateien/downloads/Schoenheitspflege/recommendation_sunscreens.pdf)
- **Labelled SPF values are stepped.** A measured SPF between 30 and 50 must be labelled 30. — [Beauty of Joseon EU/US explainer](https://be.beautyofjoseon.com/blogs/news/daily-tinted-fluid-sunscreen-us-and-eu-versions-explained)
- **Methods:** ISO 24444 measures SPF in vivo, and ISO 24443 measures UVA-PF and critical wavelength in vitro. — [Coslaw](https://coslaw.eu/sunscreen-focus-regulatory-requirements-in-the-european-union-and-united-states/)
- ISO 24444 is a human-volunteer erythema method. The current edition is ISO 24444:2019, with Amd 1:2022 listed by a BS EN reseller. The standard document alone costs €212.90 ex-VAT on DIN. — [DIN Media](https://dinmedia.de/en/standard/iso-24444/318466088); [technickenormy.cz](https://technickenormy.cz/en/bs-en-iso-24444-2019-a1-2022-cosmetics-sun-protection-test-methods-in-vivo-determination-of-the-sun-protection-factor-spf)
- One industry source characterises in vivo testing as "ethically problematic, time-consuming, technically challenging and costly". — [J.S. Hamilton Poland](https://hamilton.com.pl/en/spf-without-volunteers-the-cosmetics-market-is-taking-a-new-direction/)
- **UV filters** must come from the authorised list and be used within its conditions. — [Biorius](https://biorius.com/regulatory/sunscreen-products). A secondary blog reports **4-MBC banned under Regulation (EU) 2024/996 from 1 May 2025**; verify on EUR-Lex. — [Maven RS](https://www.mavenrs.com/blog/eu-sunscreen-regulations-compliance)
- **New in vitro and hybrid ISO methods:**

### Inferences
- **A typical EU claim set needs several tests on the final formula:**
  - SPF: ISO 24444 in vivo, or now ISO 23675/23698.
  - UVA-PF and critical wavelength: ISO 24443 in vitro.
  - "Water resistant" claims: ISO 16217 in vivo water immersion (background knowledge; ISO 16217 was not directly sourced here). Water resistance cannot use the double-plate method.
- **For the two target formats:**
  - **Aerosol mousse:** the propellant-containing emulsion may or may not fall in ISO 23675's emulsion scope. Labs normally test the product as dispensed, which needs confirming.
  - **Bi-phase sun water:** probably outside the "emulsion or alcoholic one-phase" scope, so in vivo ISO 24444 likely remains necessary. Both points need lab confirmation.
- **A brand starting in 2026 can choose in vitro SPF in the EU.** But it should expect to need ISO 24444 data for some non-EU markets, and in vivo data for any water-resistance claim.

## 4. Can a white-label brand rely on the manufacturer's SPF test? Cost and time of testing, effect of changes, and RP/CPSR/PIF duties

### Takeaway
**Not established from sources in this session.** The regulatory logic (background knowledge, flagged) is that the brand's Responsible Person must hold proof of the SPF claim for **the exact formula and presentation it sells**. A manufacturer's test report can be used if the formula is identical and the manufacturer grants access to the data. Any change to formula (including fragrance) or dispensing system can invalidate it. Real-world failures show why platforms avoid this: in Australia, one base formula used across 31 brands tested well below its labelled SPF.

### Cited Findings
- **Shared-base failure (Australia, 2025):** a zinc sunscreen base formula that failed multiple preliminary tests was reportedly used across **31 different brands**, with SPF "in the low to mid 20s" against claimed 50+. — [RUSSH](https://www.russh.com/zinc-sunscreens-fail-testing-abc-investigation/)
- **Sourcing advice:**
  - Request SPF validation reports, a PET, microbial limits and stability data from private-label suppliers. — [Alibaba sourcing guide](https://www.alibaba.com/price-comparison/spf-private-label)
  - Get a **batch CoA** confirming each production batch "matches the formula that was tested". — (same source)
  - CPNP notification "does not mean the product actually meets all regulatory requirements". — (same source)
- **The EU lab-report convention** cites a method per figure, for example "SPF (ISO 24444, in vivo), UVAPF (ISO 24442, in vivo)". ISO 24442 is the older in vivo UVA method. — [Beauty of Joseon](https://be.beautyofjoseon.com/blogs/news/daily-tinted-fluid-sunscreen-us-and-eu-versions-explained)
- **Same brand, different formulas by region:** the brand sells separate US and EU versions because of filter and regulatory differences. — (same source)
- **Cost and time:** in vivo testing is described only qualitatively as "costly" and "time-consuming". The double-plate method is "reportedly more cost efficient, more reliable and faster". — [J.S. Hamilton Poland](https://hamilton.com.pl/en/spf-without-volunteers-the-cosmetics-market-is-taking-a-new-direction/). **No price list was found.**

### Inferences
These are background regulatory knowledge, **not verified in this session**. Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 is at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2009/1223/oj (not fetched).
- **The brand owner is the legal Responsible Person, not the factory.** If the client's name is on the pack as the RP, the client is the RP. The RP must ensure:
  - a **Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR)** by a qualified safety assessor;
  - a **Product Information File (PIF)** held for 10 years after the last batch, including **proof of the claimed effect** (the SPF, UVA and water-resistance reports);
  - **CPNP notification** before sale;
  - GMP (ISO 22716) manufacture;
  - label compliance (RP name and address, Annex VI filter limits, 2006/647/EC wording and categories).
  
  The relevant articles (Art. 4, 10, 11, 13, 19) should be verified on EUR-Lex.
- **Reusing the manufacturer's test is normal in practice, but only for an identical product.** A white-label brand can usually cite the manufacturer's SPF and UVA reports in its PIF *if*:
  1. the formula is identical, including fragrance, colour and any "minor" active;
  2. the packaging and dispensing system is the same type;
  3. the manufacturer contractually provides the report or a letter of access, and the CPSR assessor accepts it.
- **Changes to formula or dispenser are what break the test.** Changing fragrance or adding an ingredient changes the formula, so assessors and labs usually require at least a retest, or a documented justification. Switching from cream to spray, or from pump to aerosol, can change the applied film and dose, and labs typically retest. An SPF *mousse* is a different product from the same emulsion without propellant. This rests on unverified practice knowledge and should be confirmed with a CPSR assessor or lab (e.g. Eurofins, Dermscan, Complife, BioScreen, Hamilton).
- **Batch-to-batch SPF drift** is a known risk (see the Australia case). That is why serious buyers ask for batch CoAs and periodic re-verification. A one-off historical report may not protect the RP if market surveillance (e.g. JACOP) tests a product that underperforms.

## 5. Why zero-MOQ / dropship platforms rarely offer SPF, and who sells stock SPF with test reports in small runs

### Takeaway
**No EU platform or lab selling "stock SPF with test reports included" in small quantities was found.** The closest were one 200-unit-MOQ EU-made SPF 50 sun milk listing (unverified, no test report mentioned) and Alibaba listings at 1,000+ pcs. The structural reasons platforms exclude SPF follow from the cited facts above.

### Cited Findings
- An EU-made "Sunscreen Milk SPF 50 with Bulgarian Rose" is offered as private label at **MOQ 200 units per type**. No SPF report is mentioned, and the listing is on an aggregator of uncertain reliability. — [china.org.ru listing](https://china.org.ru/product/50036550500)
- Alibaba private-label SPF offers seen carried MOQs of about **1,000 pcs**. — [Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/price-comparison/spf-private-label)
- A reviewer reports that customs repeatedly confiscated aerosol sunscreen shipments, a shipping barrier for dropship. — [Lab Muffin](https://labmuffin.com/top-sunscreens-2024-part-2-european-sunscreens/)
- Regional filter rules mean one stock formula cannot serve both the EU and the US. — [INCIDecoder](https://incidecoder.com/products/niche-beauty-lab-transparent-lab-lightweight-sunscreen-spf50); [Beauty of Joseon](https://be.beautyofjoseon.com/blogs/news/daily-tinted-fluid-sunscreen-us-and-eu-versions-explained)

### Inferences
Reasons zero-MOQ platforms exclude SPF, synthesised from the findings above plus flagged background knowledge:
1. **Per-formula efficacy testing is a fixed cost that cannot be spread over tiny runs.** Each variant needs its own SPF and UVA test (often in vivo), and any customisation (scent, tint, add-in) can trigger a retest.
2. **Liability concentrates on the brand-owner RP.** SPF underperformance is a health risk, and it is now being market-tested (JACOP 2025, the Australian base-formula scandal). A platform that offered one tested base to hundreds of micro-brands would create exactly the Australian 31-brand exposure.
3. **US OTC-drug status** (background knowledge, not sourced here). In the US, sunscreens are OTC drugs under the FDA monograph, which requires an FDA-registered drug facility, Drug Facts labelling and a limited filter list. US-centric platforms (Blanka, Supliful and similar; not verified) therefore usually exclude SPF entirely.
4. **Per-brand labelling obligations:** the RP's name, the CPNP notification, the 2006/647/EC categories and warnings, and the UVA logo. Each white-label SKU therefore needs its own regulatory work, not just a sticker.
5. **Batch-specific risk:** SPF can drift between batches, so suppliers must control and document each lot.
6. **Format logistics:** aerosols (mousses) are dangerous goods, and spray devices affect the delivered dose.

**Implication for the client:** both target formats sit at the hard end of white-label SPF. The mousse is an aerosol, with dangerous goods and can/valve MOQs. The sun water is bi-phase and sprayed, with a device-dependent dose and likely in vivo testing. Expect to work with a filler or lab that owns a tested base and accepts MOQs in the low thousands, rather than a zero-MOQ platform.
