# Low-MOQ Sunscreen Stops Short of Mousse and Water

**This research found no European private-label or white-label manufacturer that sells a ready-made SPF mousse like Evy's, or an SPF 50 "sun water" like Lancaster's, at low MOQ. No zero-MOQ platform offers either format at all.** Only one supplier works like Gelato for sunscreen: Selfnamed in Riga, Latvia. It prints your label on demand, dropships one unit at a time through Shopify and acts as EU Responsible Person. Its sun range, though, is limited to a mineral SPF 30 cream, a tinted SPF 30 and an SPF 50 stick. One step up, about a dozen Italian, German and Austrian labs sell fixed-formula SPF creams, milks and sprays from 3 to 50 units. Laboratori Sabatel, near Rome, sells "transparent" SPF sprays from about two hundred units, the closest stock product to a sun water. Every company shown to hold an SPF foam runs at aerosol scale and does not publish its MOQ: Romar Global Care/Agrado in Spain, Biofarma's San Pietro Viminario plant in Italy and Dermcos in Germany. That is because Evy-style mousses are flammable hydrocarbon aerosols. The sun water is the more reachable of the two formats. Lancaster's product is a bi-phase lotion you shake before use, not an aerosol, so a mid-tier sun lab can build one in a pump spray. It will probably need human-volunteer (in vivo) SPF testing, because the new non-human (in vitro) method covers only emulsions and single-phase alcohol products. The often-quoted "~€25k per SPF test" is an average from a research consortium. A commercial ISO 24444 in vivo SPF test is listed at about $2,000, and an in vitro SPF test at about $530.

> **How these facts were gathered.** Every finding comes from search-engine result extracts of the cited pages. The research environment's network proxy blocked direct access to supplier websites, and the shared search budget ran out before every candidate could be checked. Most MOQs, prices and capabilities are therefore the supplier's own marketing copy as indexed by a search engine, sometimes from pages one to four years old, plus directory listings and a few competitor-written listicles. **Treat every MOQ, price, format and regulatory service below as a lead to confirm in writing before relying on it.** "n/p" in the tables means "not published, ask".

## Nobody sells a ready-made SPF mousse or SPF 50 sun water at low MOQ

The research covered seven areas: DACH and Benelux; France; Italy; Spain and Portugal; the UK, Nordics, Central and Eastern Europe and Turkey; zero-MOQ platforms; and formats and regulation. All seven point to the same negative finding: **no European supplier was found advertising a stock SPF mousse or a stock SPF 50 sun water, with test data, at low MOQ.** The micro-MOQ labs that will put your label on 3 to 50 units sell only creams, milks, oils, sticks and conventional sprays. The companies that already own an SPF foam keep it for their own brand or a retailer, or run aerosol lines with unpublished minimums. A targeted search for private-label sun water found no EU maker advertising a stock transparent or sun-water SPF formula; the only private-label result was a generic how-to blog ([Beautetrade](https://beautetrade.com/blogs/6-simple-steps-to-create-your-private-label-sunscreen-brand)). This finding covers published offers only. Suppliers may hold such formulas without advertising them, which is why the questions at the end of this report matter.

### Evy's mousse is a flammable aerosol with no white-label programme

**Evy Technology's mousse is a pressurised aerosol, not a pump foam.** The Danish H&M listing for Evy SPF 50 carries the warning "Yderst brandfarlig aerosol" (extremely flammable aerosol) ([H&M DK](https://www2.hm.com/da_dk/productpage.1113971001.html)). One reviewer reports that customs confiscated Evy shipments several times "because of the aerosol format" ([Lab Muffin](https://labmuffin.com/top-sunscreens-2024-part-2-european-sunscreens/)). Every other European SPF mousse examined uses the same design: an emulsion with butane, propane or isobutane in a metal can.

Evy's manufacturer could not be confirmed. A single Boozt listing names "Celsus Sweden AB, Torsby" ([Boozt](https://www.boozt.com/is/is/evy-technology/sunscreen-mousse-spf-30_27624580)), but no registered company of that name turned up ([allabolag](https://www.allabolag.se/foretag/celsus-medica-ab/-/-/5594291931)). No evidence was found that Evy offers private label or licensing. Qogita sells finished Evy stock wholesale, which is resale, not white label ([Qogita](https://www.qogita.com/brands/evy/)). The format still owns its Nordic category. Swedish comparison sites ranked Evy's children's SPF 50 mousse first in 2026 and named Apotek Hjärtat's own-label Solmousse SPF 30 as the budget pick ([Testkompassen](https://www.testkompassen.se/kategorier/skonhet/hudvard/solskyddsmousse); [Testra](https://testra.se/test/solskyddsmousse-bast-i-test-2026)).

### Every shortcut around the aerosol fails or adds cost

Bag-on-valve (BoV), the "eco" aerosol that uses compressed air, cannot make a foam. The Spanish filler Proersa states that "les mousses ont besoin du gaz pour avoir un aspect mousseux" (mousses need the gas to look foamy) ([Proersa](https://www.proersa.com/fra/conditionnement-aerosols-ecologiques/)). A BoV foam requires a post-foaming gel, the technology the UK's Wear Valley Aerosols started with and now lists for suncare ([BAMA](https://bama.co.uk/member-companies/view/wv-associates-ltd-trading-as-wear-valley-aerosols)). That means a new formula with its own SPF test.

No European SPF product using a non-aerosol foamer pump was found. Germany's artefactum, however, lists an "Advanced Non-Aerosol Foam Technology" alongside its private-label sun range ([Europages](https://www.europages.de/ARTEFACTUM-GMBH/00000005311420-001.html)). Some products sold as "crème mousse", such as Topicrem's SPF 50 ([Topicrem](https://fr.topicrem.com/products/creme-mousse-fini-mat-spf50)) and SVR's Sun Secure Blur ([Parashop](https://www.parashop.com/protections-solaires/31102-sun-secure-blur-creme-mousse-flouteur-optique-spf50-teinte-50ml-svr.html)), may be whipped creams in tubes or jars rather than aerosols. That is inferred from their names. If true, they show a mousse-like texture is possible without the can.

For a D2C brand, a true aerosol adds four costs:

- flammability labelling;
- dangerous-goods limits on parcel shipping;
- minimums for cans, valves and actuators that typically run into the thousands (an inference);
- packaging and raw materials bought to suppliers' minimums. Swiss filler ASM's general terms make the customer take these on ([ASM](https://www.aerosol-service.com/en/bedingungenimpressum/alb/)).

Anyone considering the US should also know that the FDA has sent warning letters to five companies selling mousse sunscreens. No final FDA order authorises a foam dosage form ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/fda-warns-about-mousse-sunscreens-2025a1000lxp)).

### Lancaster's "water" is a shaken lotion, not an aerosol

Lancaster's Sun Protective Water is not what its name implies. Harrods describes it as "a water-like spray that can be applied even to wet, fresh-from-the-pool skin" and quotes the label: "**Bi-phase formula, shake well to blend thoroughly**" ([Harrods](https://harrods.com/en-se/p/lancaster-sun-beauty-sun-protective-water-spf-50-150ml-000000000007214547)). The SPF 30 ingredient list starts with water and dicaprylyl carbonate, a light emollient, which carry three UV filters: octocrylene, isoamyl p-methoxycinnamate and homosalate ([Lookfantastic](https://us.lookfantastic.com/lancaster-sun-protective-water-spf30-150ml/12383001.html)). The SPF 50 version drops octocrylene and homosalate and adds buriti oil ([Lookfantastic EU](https://eu.lookfantastic.com/p/lancaster-sun-beauty-sun-protective-water-spf50-150ml/15844264/)). Coty makes it in Monaco; US customs records show shipments from Coty Lancaster S.A.M. in October 2024 ([Panjiva](https://es.panjiva.com/Coty-Lancaster-S-A-M/89202003)). Judging by the SPF 30 ingredients, the reference product is neither an aerosol nor alcohol-based.

"Sun water" covers two technologies:

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Bi-phase lotions** | Lancaster; Caudalie's Vinosun, a "texture between water and oil" ([Skinlovers](https://www.skinlovers.com/en-eu/products/caudalie-vinosun-protect-sun-water-spf50-150ml)); Krème's Eau Solaire Fraîche, made in France ([Krème](https://kreme-paris.com/products/eau-solaire-spf-50)) |
| **Alcohol-based clear sprays** | Vichy's Acqua Solare, which contains denatured alcohol ([Easyfarma](https://easyfarma.it/vichy-capital-soleil-acqua-solare-protettiva-idratante-spf-50-200-ml/)); Bios Line's Nature's transparent SPF 50, which lists alcohol first ([Nature's](https://www.natures.it/en/prodotti/transparent-sun-spray-spf-50-isolari/)) |

Both types rely on organic UV filters dissolved in an oil or solvent phase. Mineral-only natural labs make opaque spray-creams, such as Propos'Nature's zinc and titanium SPF 50+ spray ([La Perle d'Argent](https://www.laperledargent.com/spray-creme-solaire-bio-spf-50-75-ml-propos-nature-c2x32760118)), so they are the wrong place to look. That is an inference.

### Four structural reasons keep SPF off zero-MOQ platforms

No platform states these reasons in writing, but the evidence points to them:

1. **US drug rules.** Most platforms are American, and in the US "all products that claim to provide Broad Spectrum SPF protection are regulated as sunscreen drug products" ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/understanding-over-counter-medicines/questions-and-answers-fda-announces-new-requirements-over-counter-otc-sunscreen-products-marketed-us)). That brings drug-facility, labelling and filter-list duties a print-on-demand model cannot carry.
2. **Per-formula testing.** Every SPF claim needs efficacy testing tied to one exact formula. As one compliance firm puts it, "suppliers would price themselves out of the market if they had to arrange lab testing for products that may, at some point, be ordered" ([ComplianceGate](https://www.compliancegate.com/dropshipping-product-safety-compliance-risks/)). The cost shows even at German micro-labs: Meine-Eigenmarke sells SPF 30 from 30 units but **requires 500 units for SPF 50** ([Meine-Eigenmarke](https://www.meine-eigenmarke.de/fuspflege/kosmetikprodukte/)).
3. **Liability.** Anyone placing a cosmetic on the market under their own name becomes the RP, responsible for CPNP notification, safety assessment and the Product Information File (PIF) ([AGES](https://www.ages.at/mensch/kosmetik/kosmetik-informationen-fuer-unternehmen); [BVL](https://www.bvl.bund.de/DE/Arbeitsbereiche/03_Verbraucherprodukte/03_AntragstellerUnternehmen/02_Kosmetik/01_Notifizierung/bgs_fuerAntragsteller_kosmetik_Notifizierung_node.html)). Enforcement is active:
   - The EU's 2025 JACOP market-surveillance project targeted sun products ([JACOP report](https://www.pap.pl/sites/default/files/2026-07/JACOP%202025%20-%20REPORT.pdf)).
   - Spain's AEMPS asked companies to withdraw products whose in vivo SPF did not support the label ([AEMPS](https://www.aemps.gob.es/informa/notasInformativas/cosmeticos-cuidado-personal/2021/Informe_AEMPS-an%C3%A1lisis_FPS_de_productos_solares.pdf?x39768=)).
   - In Australia, one zinc base formula that tested in the low to mid 20s against a 50+ claim was traced to **31 brands** ([RUSSH](https://www.russh.com/zinc-sunscreens-fail-testing-abc-investigation/)). A platform selling one tested base to hundreds of micro-brands would recreate that exposure.
4. **Logistics.** Aerosols are dangerous goods, and spray devices change the dose delivered.

Selfnamed avoids most of this because EU sunscreens are cosmetics, not drugs, and it holds the RP role and test data centrally. Testing is then paid once per formula, not once per brand (inference).

## The test report, not the factory, is what a white-label brand buys

In the EU, sunscreens are cosmetics, but SPF is a quantified claim that must be proven on the finished product.

### Recommendation 2006/647/EC sets the claim rules

**Commission Recommendation 2006/647/EC**, adopted 22 September 2006 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reco/2006/647/oj/eng)), is non-binding but is "the main document to consider when placing sunscreen products on the EU market" ([Biorius](https://biorius.com/regulatory/sunscreen-products)). Its rules:

- **Minimum efficacy:** SPF of at least 6, **UVA protection factor (UVA-PF) of at least one-third of the labelled SPF**, and a **critical wavelength of at least 370 nm** ([Recommendation text](https://zdravlje.gov.hr/UserDocsImages//2018%20Sanitarna//RECOMMENDATION%20EC%20on%20the%20efficacy%20of%20sunscreen%20products%20and%20the%20claims.pdf)).
- **Label categories:** "low", "medium", "high" and "very high".
- **Banned claims:** "sunblock" and "total protection" ([Obelis](https://www.obelis.net/news/stricter-labelling-principles-for-sunscreens-in-europe/)).
- **Stepped labelling:** a measured SPF between 30 and 50 is labelled 30 ([Beauty of Joseon](https://be.beautyofjoseon.com/blogs/news/daily-tinted-fluid-sunscreen-us-and-eu-versions-explained)), and **"SPF 50+" requires a measured SPF of at least 60** ([Valutazione Sicurezza Cosmetici](https://www.valutazionesicurezzacosmetici.it/prodotti-solari-cosmetici-normativa-spf/)).

UV filters must come from the authorised list. A secondary source reports that 4-MBC has been banned since 1 May 2025 under Regulation (EU) 2024/996, which should be checked on EUR-Lex ([Maven RS](https://www.mavenrs.com/blog/eu-sunscreen-regulations-compliance)).

### ISO methods: established and new

| Standard | What it measures | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ISO 24444:2019 | SPF, in vivo (human volunteers) | Amendment 1 issued 2022 ([technickenormy.cz](https://technickenormy.cz/en/bs-en-iso-24444-2019-a1-2022-cosmetics-sun-protection-test-methods-in-vivo-determination-of-the-sun-protection-factor-spf)); the core EU method ([Coslaw](https://coslaw.eu/sunscreen-focus-regulatory-requirements-in-the-european-union-and-united-states/)) |
| ISO 24443 | UVA-PF and critical wavelength, in vitro | Core EU method ([Coslaw](https://coslaw.eu/sunscreen-focus-regulatory-requirements-in-the-european-union-and-united-states/)) |
| ISO 24442 | UVA, in vivo | Older method ([CertifiedCosmetics](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/blog/cosmetics-laboratory-testing/sunscreen-efficacy-and-spf-testing/)) |
| ISO 16217 | Water resistance, in vivo | Paired with ISO 24444 in Cosmoderma's sun work ([Cosmoderma](https://www.cosmoderma.it/produzione-creme-solari-conto-terzi)) |
| ISO 18861 | Water and sweat resistance percentage, in vivo | ([CertifiedCosmetics](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/product/spf-testing/sweat-resistance-spf-laboratory-test-in-vivo-sun-protection-factor/)) |
| **ISO 23675:2024** (Double Plate Method) | SPF, fully in vitro | Published late 2024 ([Cosmetics & Toiletries](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/testing/sun-protection/news/22929537/2025-will-see-faster-more-ethical-and-accurate-spf-testing-iso-approves-two-new-methods)) |
| **ISO 23698:2024** (HDRS) | SPF, hybrid method | Published late 2024; needs specialised instruments, which limits availability ([Certified Cosmetics](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/blog/regulatory-news/alternative-spf-methods-in-2026-industry-adoption-and-regulatory-reality/)) |

**The shift to the new methods:**

- Cosmetics Europe welcomed the Double Plate Method in February 2025 ([Personal Care Magazine](https://personalcaremagazine.com/story/47291/cosmetics-europe-hails-isos-in-vitro-spf-test-approval)).
- **Its Recommendation No. 26, revised 23 June 2025, gives preference to ISO 23675 and ISO 23698 over ISO 24444** ([CosmeticOBS](https://cosmeticobs.com/en/articles/news-34/cosmetics-europe-publishes-recommendation-no26-on-the-use-of-alternative-methods-to-iso-244442019-6468)).
- Using them is voluntary, and products already tested to ISO 24444 need not be retested ([Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/blog/2025/08-04-eu-regulations-for-sunscreen-products/)).
- In 2026 both are implemented mainly in R&D ([Certified Cosmetics](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/blog/regulatory-news/alternative-spf-methods-in-2026-industry-adoption-and-regulatory-reality/)), and uptake outside the EU, including FDA timing, is unknown ([Cosmetics & Toiletries](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/testing/sun-protection/news/22929537/2025-will-see-faster-more-ethical-and-accurate-spf-testing-iso-approves-two-new-methods)).

**The scope limit matters for both target formats.** ISO 23675 applies to "emulsions or alcoholic one-phase formulations". It is not approved for powders or sticks, and it is "not validated for the determination of water resistance" ([J.S. Hamilton](https://hamilton.com.pl/en/spf-without-volunteers-the-cosmetics-market-is-taking-a-new-direction/)). For the two formats this likely means:

- **Bi-phase sun water:** probably outside that scope, so in vivo ISO 24444 testing remains likely.
- **Aerosol mousse:** it is unclear whether a propellant emulsion qualifies.
- **Any "water resistant" claim:** still needs in vivo testing.

Confirm these with a test lab. Results also vary between labs, and the spread is "particularly striking for sunscreens with an SPF50 or SPF50+" ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7540522/)). An SPF 50+ claim needs headroom.

### What a white-label brand can and cannot reuse

The brand on the label is the Responsible Person. It must notify CPNP, hold a safety assessment and PIF, and print its name and address ([AGES](https://www.ages.at/mensch/kosmetik/kosmetik-informationen-fuer-unternehmen)). The exceptions are a manufacturer that takes that role, as Valquer and Selfnamed say they do, or, for non-EU supply, the EU importer by default ([Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/blog/2025/08-04-eu-regulations-for-sunscreen-products/)).

The research found no primary source spelling out when a manufacturer's SPF report can be reused. The table below is standard EU practice, which the research flags for confirmation with a safety assessor or test lab, alongside the sourced cases that bear on it.

| Situation | Can the brand rely on the supplier's existing SPF data? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Identical formula (including fragrance and colour), same pack and dispenser, supplier grants written access, brand's safety assessor accepts | Usually yes; this is how white label works | Practice knowledge to confirm. Sourcing guides advise requesting SPF validation reports and a batch certificate of analysis (CoA) "that matches the formula that was tested" ([Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/price-comparison/spf-private-label)) |
| Formula tested to ISO 24444 before the in vitro methods arrived | Yes; no retest required | ([Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/blog/2025/08-04-eu-regulations-for-sunscreen-products/)) |
| Fragrance, colour or any ingredient changed | No; expect a retest or a documented justification | Practice knowledge, unverified in this research |
| Same bulk moved into an aerosol, or a tested emulsion turned into a mousse with propellant | No; it is a new product as dispensed and needs new SPF and UVA tests | Practice knowledge; the dispensed film and dose change |
| Same base sold to many brands | Allowed, but each brand's RP carries the risk if the base underperforms | One base, 31 brands, SPF in the 20s ([RUSSH](https://www.russh.com/zinc-sunscreens-fail-testing-abc-investigation/)) |
| One old report with no batch control | Weak protection under market surveillance | AEMPS withdrawals; one SPF 50 batch measured a mean of 47.1 ([AEMPS](https://www.aemps.gob.es/informa/notasInformativas/cosmeticos-cuidado-personal/2021/Informe_AEMPS-an%C3%A1lisis_FPS_de_productos_solares.pdf?x39768=)) |
| Platform product relabelled under your own name and address | You become the RP; the platform's certifications may not carry over | ([Selfnamed Help](https://help.selfnamed.com/en/articles/10031773-can-there-be-issues-with-specific-product-claims)) |
| Selling the same formula in the US | No; SPF products are OTC drugs, and brands run separate EU and US formulas | ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/understanding-over-counter-medicines/questions-and-answers-fda-announces-new-requirements-over-counter-otc-sunscreen-products-marketed-us); [Beauty of Joseon](https://be.beautyofjoseon.com/blogs/news/daily-tinted-fluid-sunscreen-us-and-eu-versions-explained)) |

The practical consequence: **a white-label SPF product is only "low fixed cost" if the supplier owns a test report on the exact formula in the exact dispenser, and grants you the right to use it.** No supplier in this research publishes such reports for its stock formulas, so this is the first question to ask, not the last.

### Correcting the "~€25k per SPF test" figure

The article notes "the costs vary from test institute to test institute". |

| "Just a few hundred" euros or dollars | [Cosmetics & Toiletries](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/testing/sun-protection/news/22929537/2025-will-see-faster-more-ethical-and-accurate-spf-testing-iso-approves-two-new-methods) | An expert's expected cost of ISO 23675 in vitro SPF, down from "several thousand" |

## Build the sun water first and treat the mousse as a capital project

The two formats need different suppliers and different budgets, so they should not share a launch plan.

### Route for the sun water

**Recommended route:** source a bi-phase or transparent SPF 50 pump spray from a sun lab that already holds one, rather than designing from scratch.

1. **Shortlist three suppliers.**
   - **Laboratori Sabatel**, first: about 200 units, and a catalogue transparent spray. Make it confirm a real SPF 50 transparent formula and send its reports.
   - **Valquer**, second: its SPF 50 "Agua Solar" spray, its offer to act as RP and register on CPNP, and its roughly 1,000-unit sweet spot.
   - **Zetaphar's "acqua solare spray"**, third.
2. **Sample textures cheaply in parallel.** Laboratori Italiani's 12-unit "sun tanning water", Cosmetica Conto Terzi's 25-piece SPF 50 spray and Meine-Eigenmarke's 30-unit SPF 30 spray let you compare textures before committing.
3. **If no stock base passes, go custom.** Use an organic-filter sun formulator: ALPOL, SofIA, Macosgroup (which bundles SPF tests, PIF and CPNP), Dermcos (transparent-sunscreen patent) or Bios Line (has made an alcohol-based SPF 50). Ashland's HydroSheer starting formulas are a reference.
4. **Budget for in vivo testing and avoid the aerosol.** A bi-phase product probably falls outside ISO 23675, so expect in vivo ISO 24444 at about $2,000 plus UVA and any water-resistance test. A pump-spray sun water also avoids the dangerous-goods costs that come with aerosols.

### Route for the mousse

**Recommended route:** license or adapt an existing tested SPF foam rather than develop one.

1. **Ask the holders of tested SPF foams, in this order:**
   1. Romar Global Care, about white-labelling or adapting the Agrado SPF 50+ mousse;
   2. Biofarma San Pietro Viminario, which explicitly lists sun mousse;
   3. Dermcos, credited maker of the Lavozon LSF 50+ foam;
   4. whoever fills Apotea's and Hjärtat's identical Swedish pharmacy solmousse. Find them by reading the "Tillverkad av/för" (made by/for) line on the cans.
   5. Evy itself, asked directly.
2. **If none will license, go custom.** Pair a sun formulator (ALPOL, SofIA, Cosmoya for in vitro SPF screening, or Macosgroup) with a filler that has a flammable-aerosol permit and small-run appetite: Aerochem, Proersa or Everton.
3. **Keep a non-aerosol fallback.** artefactum's non-aerosol foam is the only lead. A "crème mousse" texture in a tube or jar is a fallback that avoids the can altogether.
4. **Plan the economics as a capital project:**
   - aerosol component minimums in the thousands (an inference);
   - dangerous-goods shipping, a real problem for D2C parcels to the Nordics;
   - SPF and UVA testing on the foam as dispensed;
   - development timelines like Cosmoderma's roughly 12 months for a new sunscreen, or Laboratoire Ophylor's 12–18 months with 1,500–2,000-unit custom minimums ([Ophylor](https://www.laboratoire-ophylor.com/lancer-sa-marque-cosmetique-sur-mesure-ou-en-marque-blanche/)).

### A staged path and its published fixed costs

A staged path keeps fixed costs low:

1. **Stage 0:** Selfnamed's clear SPF 50 stick, at zero inventory, to test the brand.
2. **Stage 1:** the sun water as hero product, at 200–1,000 units.
3. **Stage 2:** the mousse, once volume justifies a can line.

### Questions to put to every supplier

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which stock SPF 50 or 50+ formulas do you hold today as (a) a foam or mousse, or (b) a bi-phase or transparent spray? Please send the full ingredient list (INCI) and a sample. | Confirms a ready format exists before any development fee. |
| 2 | For that exact formula, which reports exist: SPF by ISO 24444 (or ISO 23675/23698); UVA-PF and critical wavelength by ISO 24443; water resistance in vivo? Which lab, what date, what measured mean SPF? | "SPF 50+" needs a measured SPF of at least 60, and results vary between labs. |
| 3 | Was the test run on the product as dispensed, from the same can, valve, actuator or pump I would buy? | A different dispenser changes the dose delivered and can invalidate the test. |
| 4 | Will you grant a written letter of access to cite those reports in my PIF, and will your safety assessor sign the safety report (CPSR) for my label? | Without access, the brand pays for new testing. |
| 5 | Who is the Responsible Person on my label, you or my company? Who notifies CPNP and holds the PIF, and at what cost? | Valquer and Selfnamed take this role; most suppliers expect the brand to. |
| 6 | Which changes (fragrance, colour, pack, fill size, claim) would trigger retesting? | Defines how much customisation the stock dossier tolerates. |
| 7 | What is the minimum for bulk, for filled units, and for each component (can, valve, actuator, pump, label, box)? Is there a smaller first order? | Aerosol component minimums often exceed filling minimums. |
| 8 | What is the unit price at your MOQ, at 1,000 and at 5,000? What are the one-off fees for setup, printing, development and notification, and who pays for testing? | Few suppliers publish any price. |
| 9 | Is the formula exclusive to me, or sold to other brands, and how many? | Shared bases spread failure risk across brands, as in the Australian case. |
| 10 | Will each batch come with a CoA confirming it matches the tested formula, and do you re-verify SPF periodically? | SPF drift between batches is exposed by surveillance like JACOP. |
| 11 | What stability, compatibility and shelf-life data exist in the final pack? | Required for the safety report; aerosols need pack-compatibility data. |
| 12 | For aerosols or alcohol sprays: what is the flammability and dangerous-goods class, and can single units go by parcel to Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Finland? | Evy shipments have been confiscated at customs. |
| 13 | Which UV filters are used? Is the formula octocrylene-free and compliant with the current authorised-filter list? | Filters determine clarity, claims and future-proofing. |
| 14 | Is the formula within ISO 23675's scope (an emulsion or single-phase alcohol product), so cheaper in vitro SPF testing could be used? | Bi-phase products probably need in vivo testing. |
| 15 | What are the lead times for a first order and for reorders? | Benchmarks: Proersa 6–8 weeks; Cleansery 6–10 weeks stock vs 3–6 months custom. |

## Conclusion

The binding constraint for this brand is not MOQ but ownership of a tested dossier in the right dispenser. Micro-MOQ white label works for sunscreen only because a lab has already paid for an SPF report on one fixed formula in one fixed pack. Both target formats sit where such dossiers are rarest:

- **Sun water:** device-dependent and bi-phase, so probably still tested on human volunteers.
- **Mousse:** a flammable aerosol, whose tested bases are locked inside retailer own-label supply chains.

That reframes supplier selection as dossier selection, and it means **the format decision drives cost more than the choice of supplier does.** Format-wise, the sun water is a lab job that can launch in the hundreds of units, while the mousse is a filler job that likely starts in the thousands. The 2025 shift to in vitro SPF testing will cut test costs for ordinary emulsions, but probably not for a bi-phase water or any water-resistance claim, so it helps less here than headlines suggest.

The highest-value next steps are mundane and cheap:

- read the "Tillverkad av/för" line on an Apotea or Hjärtat solmousse can;
- ask Romar Global Care, Biofarma and Dermcos whether they will license an existing SPF foam to a non-competing D2C brand;
- make Sabatel prove that its transparent SPF 50 exists, with reports.

If the Swedish pharmacy mousse turns out to come from the same filler as Evy, the brand's most direct route to an Evy-class product is a Nordic supplier no directory lists.
