# Launch a therapeutic tablet, not an eco gimmick

Tube paste and gel still account for almost all toothpaste sold worldwide. Every alternative format is either a regional habit (tooth powder and Ayurvedic paste in India, TCM paste in China, liquid and foam dentifrice in Japan, miswak in the Middle East and Africa) or a Western niche worth well under 1% of category value (toothpaste tablets, at about **USD 67–120m** globally against a category of **USD 18.5–44bn**). In the West, value growth comes from science-led therapeutic claims, not from new formats or eco packaging. Haleon's oral health business grew **7.9% organically in 2025**, while Colgate's segment grew about 1% on falling volumes. Fluoride-free sales in the US have surged because of fluoride politics. The pioneer tablet brand in Germany, by contrast, peaked at about **EUR 3.5m** in sales once the zero-waste fashion cooled. The format should therefore carry a benefit that paste delivers poorly, and should not be the selling point on its own. **My recommendation is a water-free chewable tablet built around stannous fluoride, positioned on gum health plus sensitivity, with foam and mouthfeel matching paste, sold in a refillable tin.** It would launch first in German-speaking Europe through dm and Rossmann, then in the UK, then in the rest of Western Europe, and only then in the US and Canada. The order follows the regulation. In the EU and UK every toothpaste format is a cosmetic and needs only a notification, whereas the US fluoride monograph names paste, gel and powder but not tablets. A new format is therefore cheap to launch in Europe and slow and costly in the US. Three things should be validated before serious capital goes in: that the stannous tablet holds its potency (stability), that users rate its taste and foam as equal to paste (sensory parity), and which route the FDA will accept.

## Paste dominates worldwide; alternative formats survive as regional habits

Toothpaste is a mature category. Size estimates disagree by more than 2x depending on scope: **USD 18.51bn in 2024 at a 2.5% CAGR** according to SkyQuest ([SkyQuest via GII](https://gii.tw/report/sky1646265-toothpaste-market-size-share-growth-analysis-by.html)), against **USD 44.4bn in 2025 at a 2.7% CAGR** for a toothpaste-plus-toothpowder definition from WiseGuy ([WiseGuy Reports](https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/toothpaste-toothpowder-market)). Technavio expects **USD 3.85bn** of added value between 2024 and 2029, a 3.3% CAGR ([Technavio](https://technavio.com/report/toothpaste-market-industry-analysis)). Market share figures are more reliable than any of these sizes. Colgate alone held **40.9% of global toothpaste value** year to date in Q1 2025 ([Colgate 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21665/000002166525000020/cl-20250331.htm)). Growth is concentrated in emerging markets. China's toothpaste and toothbrush manufacturing grew **7.6% in 2025** to USD 6.8bn ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/china/industry/toothpaste-and-toothbrush-manufacturing/319/)). Japan grows at about **1.2% a year** ([IMARC](https://imarcgroup.com/japan-toothpaste-market)), and in India **55% of rural people still do not brush daily** ([Colgate India](https://www.colgateinvestors.co.in/pdf/analyst-meet-presentation-21022025.pdf)). No public source splits global value by physical format, so the figures for non-paste formats below are sized one by one.

None of the Asian formats has displaced tube paste even in its home market. Each persists as a premium or segment-specific option, and Japan's flat volumes push its innovation into formats and actives rather than into penetration. The "format" fight in India is mainly modern fluoride paste against Ayurvedic positioning. Colgate's Indian share fell from about **57% in FY15 to about 48% by 2020** as herbal brands gained ([HDFC Securities](https://www.hdfcsec.com/hsl.research.pdf/Colgate-Palmolive%20(India)%20Ltd%20-Initiating%20Coverage-HSLRR131221-.pdf)). That is the pattern worth remembering: in every market, formats and positionings take share by carrying a credible benefit or cultural meaning, not by being novel. This research found no sourced data on several Western micro-formats: tooth soaps, solid bars, strips and films, sachets, pre-pasted brushes, pump dispensers and refill systems.

## Western growth comes from clinical claims, not from new formats

Western Europe and North America grow about **3–4% a year by value**, mostly through price and mix. The US market is about **USD 5.5bn at about 3.1% CAGR** ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/united-states-toothpaste-market); [Market Data Forecast](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/united-states-toothpaste-market)). The UK is about **USD 909m at about 4.1%** ([Market Data Forecast UK](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/united-kingdom-toothpaste-market)), and German oral care hit a record of almost **EUR 2bn in 2024**, helped by price rises ([Statista/IKW](https://de.statista.com/statistik/daten/studie/166766/umfrage/umsatz-mit-zahn-und-mundpflegemitteln-seit-2007/)). The gap between the two category leaders explains where the value is. Colgate's oral, personal and home care sales grew only 1.0% in 2025, with **volume declines**. Its elmex franchise, however, is "driving record toothpaste market shares in Europe" on science-driven therapeutic positioning ([Colgate 2025 AR](https://investor.colgatepalmolive.com/static-files/4298e292-4aaf-4b10-8953-aad27c1065d7)). Haleon's oral health business grew **7.9% organically in 2025** and **7.3% in H1 2026**. parodontax grew double-digit, and Sensodyne Clinical White raised penetration by **130 bps, mainly among younger consumers** ([Haleon FY2025](https://www.haleon.com/content/dam/haleon/corporate/documents/investors/results/full-year-results-2025/fy25-statement.pdf.downloadasset.pdf); [Haleon H1 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001900304/000110465926088450/hln-20260630xex99d1.htm)). Sensitivity products are an estimated **44.8% of UK toothpaste value** ([Market Data Forecast UK](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/united-kingdom-toothpaste-market)). Taken together, the profitable centre of Western toothpaste is stacked therapeutic benefits (sensitivity with whitening, gum health with enamel) sold at premium prices to consumers who believe the science.

The second strong signal is specific to the US and is political. NielsenIQ data reported by Bloomberg showed **fluoride-free toothpaste sales growing about 16% a month on average through April 2025, against 2.4% for fluoridated paste** ([Gulf News/Bloomberg](https://gulfnews.com/world/americas/does-your-toothpaste-have-fluoride-rfk-jr-spurs-fluoride-free-movement-1.500167982)). **15% of US toothpaste users** said they considered fluoride-free options in 2025 ([Omya](https://www.omya.com/en/omya-specialty-materials/industries/personal-care-and-home-care/personal-care-and-home-care-articles/the-rise-of-fluoride-free-oral-care)). Utah and Florida banned water fluoridation in 2025 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/fluoride-ban-states-cavities-kids-florida-utah-rcna209750)). Grand View sizes the fluoride-free segment at about **USD 3.6bn globally in 2025**, with North America at 31.8% ([Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/fluoride-free-toothpaste-market-report)). Benefit-led nHAp brands are turning this into mass distribution. Boka reported retail sales **up 500% year to date** in 2025 and launched a probiotic-plus-nHAp paste at Walmart ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/boka-launches-probiotic-nano-hydroxyapatite-toothpaste-for-holistic-wellness-302548959.html)). The "skinification" of oral care has moved it into Sephora and Ulta ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/oral-care-brands-are-marketing-themselves-as-beauty-products/)), and Colgate launched an Ulta-exclusive Gen-Z sub-brand ([Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/colgate-goes-after-gen-z-with-new-sub-brand--175255)). Social virality wears off, though. Hismile's revenue reportedly fell to about USD 153m in 2025 after it cut marketing by USD 102m, even as profit tripled ([SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/opinion/20000-to-150-million-financial-teardown-hismile-viral-playbook/)).

Sustainability is the weakest demand driver, despite the most press coverage. Colgate estimates that **about 20bn tubes a year** go to landfill ([Colgate](https://www.colgatepalmolive.com/en-us/news/recyclable-toothpaste-tube-recycle-me-packaging-us)). The incumbents' answer was to make the tube recyclable rather than to change format. Colgate moved to single-material HDPE tubes ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90713412/after-149-years-colgates-toothpaste-tubes-are-finally-recyclable)), and Unilever did the same for Signal ([Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/press-and-media/press-releases/2021/unilever-to-introduce-recyclable-toothpaste-tubes/)). That removes much of the eco argument a new format could make. Tablets have hit the ceiling the evidence predicts. Denttabs grew from about EUR 200k to **about EUR 3.5m** once dm listed it, but demand has fallen as the zero-packaging trend lost momentum ([Lebensmittel Praxis](https://lebensmittelpraxis.de/drogerieartikel/40864-plastikfreie-mundhygiene-bleiben-zahnputztabletten-ein-nischenprodukt.html)). Bite, the US tablet leader, has estimated revenue of only **USD 4–8.8m** ([Growjo](https://growjo.com/company/Bite_Toothpaste_Bits)). The barriers cited are consistent: unfamiliar chewing, texture, taste, price and online-only distribution ([IJSRT](https://www.ijsrtjournal.com/article/a-comprehensive-review-on-toothpaste-tablets-formulation-benefits-and-future-prospects)). The most telling data point comes from Colgate's own trial. Tablets matched paste on plaque and gingival indices, but participants rated their **texture and foaminess worse** ([Loma Linda University](https://dentistry.llu.edu/sites/dentistry.llu.edu/files/2023-08/research-aug-2023.pdf)). Consumers will not give up sensory familiarity for sustainability alone. Segment-level evidence is thin. Gen Z responds to clinical-cosmetic launches, travel is the established tablet use case, and US parents are anxious about fluoride for children. The research found no quantified data on dry-mouth, elderly, men's or aligner-wearer demand.

## Regulation makes new formats cheap in Europe and costly in the US

Regulation is the decisive factor for format choice, and the four markets split sharply. In the EU, toothpaste of **any** format is a cosmetic under Regulation 1223/2009, whether or not it contains fluoride. Launch requires an EU Responsible Person, a safety report (CPSR) inside a product information file, and notification on the EU cosmetics portal (CPNP), with no pre-approval. The fluoride cap is 0.15% F (1,500 ppm). Both points are the standard reading of the law, but the consolidated Annex III text was not checked in this research. Nano-hydroxyapatite is permitted at up to **10% in toothpaste**, as rod-shaped, uncoated particles, with no uses that could expose users by inhalation. That rule has been fully enforceable since **1 Nov 2025** ([EUR-Lex 2024/858](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/858/oj); [Coslaw](https://coslaw.eu/watchout/hydroxypatite-nano/)). The EU safety committee (SCCS) found up to 29.5% safe in mid-2025, but no amendment raising the legal limit has been adopted ([ChemLinked](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/cosmetic-news/eu-sccs-concludes-hydroxyapatite-nano-is-safe-at-higher-levels-for-oral-use); [Cinoll](https://www.cinoll.com/blog/sccs-approves-higher-concentration-limits-for-nano-hydroxyapatite/)). The inhalation clause makes loose nHAp tooth powders legally risky. Compressed tablets carry far less risk. The upcoming dates are the EU packaging regulation (PPWR), applicable from **12 Aug 2026** with recyclability criteria from 2028 ([BRC](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/associate-insight/2026/european-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-ppwr/)), and the microplastics deadline for rinse-off products on **17 Oct 2027** ([Coptis](https://www.coptis.com/resources/reach-restriction-microplastics/)). Great Britain mirrors the EU regime but requires its own notification on the UK portal (SCPN) ([SCPN](https://submit.cosmetic-product-notifications.service.gov.uk/)). Nanomaterials there need **six months' advance notice**, and the UK has not been confirmed to have adopted the EU's nHAp restriction ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/call-for-data-nanomaterials-in-cosmetics); [EUverify](https://euverify.com/resource/uk-cosmetics-banned-restricted-ingredients/)).

In the US, fluoride toothpaste is an over-the-counter drug governed by **OTC Monograph M021** (the FDA's rulebook for anticavity products). Its fluoride ranges are written for "gel or paste" dentifrices, with a separate threshold for powders, and new formulations must pass biological testing: animal caries reduction plus a second test such as enamel fluoride uptake ([FDA M021](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/omuf/monographs/OTC%20Monograph%20M021-Anticaries%20Drug%20Products%20for%20OTC%20Human%20Use%2005.02.2023.pdf); [eCFR Part 355](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-355)). **Tablets and foams are not named dosage forms.** Bite states that no FDA test protocol exists for tablets ([Bite](https://bitetoothpastebits.com/blogs/blog/the-f-word-3-reasons-why-our-bits-are-fluoride-free)), yet some fluoride tablets are listed on DailyMed as monograph "paste, dentifrice" products, and the FDA does not review those listings ([DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=ad447c9a-6f0f-7fef-e053-2995a90a055f&type=display)). Enforcement is real. In July 2025 the FDA sent a warning letter calling non-compliant fluoride toothpastes "unapproved new drugs" ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/health-and-natural-beauty-usa-corp-700187-07282025)). The FDA's 2025 action on fluoride targeted **ingestible** pediatric fluoride, not toothpaste ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/fda-protect-children-unapproved-fluoride-drug-products.html)). Fluoride-free toothpaste is a cosmetic under MoCRA (the 2022 US cosmetics law): registration and listing, safety substantiation, and no anticavity claims ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/how-mocra-is-reshaping-fda-oversight-of-cosmetics-in-2026/)). The US advertising watchdog (NAD) has already pushed Boka to drop "remineralizes" claims ([Minvelle](https://www.minvelle.com/blogs/oral-health/boka-toothpaste-review-2026)). Canada authorises fluoride products under a new **Oral Health Products Monograph dated 25 Sept 2026**, through either a drug number (DIN) or a natural health product number (NPN). Fluoride-free cosmetic toothpastes need only a notification within 10 days of first sale ([Health Canada](https://webprod.hc-sc.gc.ca/nhpid-bdipsn/dbImages/mono_oral-health-products_english.pdf); [LLRN Care](https://llrncare.com/canada-cosmetic-labeling-requirements-for-oral-care-products/)).

| | EU | UK (GB) | US | Canada |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fluoride toothpaste | Cosmetic; notification only | Cosmetic; SCPN notification | OTC drug under M021 | DIN (most likely) or NPN under the 2026 monograph |
| Tablet or foam with fluoride | Same cosmetic route | Same cosmetic route | Not a named dosage form; untested, and a formal OTC monograph order request (OMOR) may be needed | Must check that the 2026 monograph lists the dosage form |
| Fluoride-free / nHAp | Cosmetic; nHAp ≤10%, specific particle shape | Cosmetic; 6-month nano notice | Cosmetic (MoCRA); no "anticavity" or "remineralizes" claims | Cosmetic notification; bilingual label |
| Indicative time to market* | 3–6 months | 3–6 months (+6 for nano) | 3–4 months fluoride-free; 6–12 months monograph; 12–24+ months via OMOR | Weeks for a cosmetic; about 3–6 months for a DIN |

*The timelines and costs in this row are unverified estimates from the regulatory notes. No authoritative source was found for CPSR, M021 testing, OMUFA or Health Canada fees.

## Format-led challengers stay small; benefit-led challengers get bought

The incumbents have mostly put their effort into recyclable tubes and acquisitions, not into new formats of their own. Colgate is the only major with a marketed tablet, the "CO. by Colgate" travel tabs at about **USD 14 a month** ([Dental Roundup](https://www.dentalroundup.com/best/best-toothpaste-tablets/)). It has, however, already proved the tablet-plus-refill economics at mass prices in another category: Softsoap foaming hand-soap tablets sell at **USD 5.99 for a starter kit and USD 4.99 for refills** with 71% less plastic ([Drug Store News](https://drugstorenews.com/colgate-palmolive-intros-softsoap-foaming-hand-soap-tablets)). The most important competitive signal is Haleon's patent **WO2024041876 / EP4577311** for a toothpaste tablet with an optional fluoride source, claimed to overcome "the previous sensorial problems" of existing tablets (GB priority August 2022, EP published July 2025) ([EPO](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP4577311NWA1/document.html)). The owner of Sensodyne and parodontax is working on the exact problem a new entrant would need to solve. No P&G tablet or foam products were found.

The challenger field falls into three groups with very different outcomes. Eco tablet brands are mostly bootstrapped and small, and many are fluoride-free. They include Bite (USD 6,000 start, founders kept 100% ownership, no Shark Tank deal partly over the lack of fluoride) ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/06/03/how-a-founder-turned-6000-into-a-multimillion-dollar-toothpaste-business.html); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/marcberman1/2020/04/01/move-over-crest-and-colgatebite-toothpaste-bits-featured-on-shark-tank-is-a-new-alternative/)), Denttabs, Lush and Georganics. Denttabs is the instructive exception: a **1,450 ppm fluoride** tablet sold at dm for **EUR 5.95 per 125 tablets** (about EUR 0.048 per brushing), and it also makes dm's private label ([Georganics](https://georganics.com/products/denttabs-toothpaste-tabs-mint); [dm](https://www.dm.de/marken/denttabs-48140); [VBKI](https://vbki.de/en/2023/02/08/tablette-statt-tube/)). Plain fluoride tablets in Germany are therefore already a low-price private-label product. The second group is benefit-led paste brands, such as nHAp and microbiome brands (Boka, RiseWell, Apagard, Evenmouth, Viome), which are winning mass retail. The third is beauty and lifestyle system brands built mostly on devices and whitening. quip raised about USD 162m by 2021 ([BeautyMatter](https://beautymatter.com/articles/quip-raises-100-million-series-b)). Spotlight reached Boots, Target and Ulta partly through an Invisalign-provider network ([RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2021/0707/1233544-12m-investment-in-spotlight-oral-care/); [BeautyMatter](https://beautymatter.com/articles/spotlight-oral-care-secures-new-funding)). The exits confirm that benefit wins over format. Colgate paid a reported **USD 351m for Hello** ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202304/kevin-j-ryan/how-avoiding-an-exit-strategy-led-hello-products-to-a-351-million-sale.html)) and Church & Dwight **USD 580m for TheraBreath** ([Happi](https://www.happi.com/contents/view_breaking-news/2021-11-29/church-dwight-to-acquire-therabreath-oral-care-for-580-million/)), both for brand and mass-retail sales velocity. No format-led startup has had a sizeable exit. Possible future buyers include Kimberly-Clark, which is acquiring Kenvue and Listerine for about **USD 48.7bn** ([Cosmetics Design Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2025/11/04/kimberly-clark-to-acquire-kenvue-in-487b-deal)), and Perrigo, whose declining oral care unit is under strategic review ([Perrigo Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001585364/000158536426000143/cy26q2ex991pressrelease.htm)). Perrigo could become a buyer, a seller or a manufacturing partner.

## A water-free stannous tablet beats eco tablets, foam and liquids

Each candidate format was scored against four tests drawn from the evidence: proven Western demand for its benefit, regulatory friction in Europe and the US, defensibility against private label and incumbents, and sensory risk.

| Candidate | Demand evidence | EU friction | US friction | Defensibility | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fluoride-free eco tablet | Weak; eco demand faded in DACH | Low | Low (cosmetic) | None; crowded | Avoid |
| Plain fluoride tablet | Moderate | Low | High (dosage form) | Low; dm private label at about EUR 0.05/brush | Avoid as the hero product |
| **Water-free stannous fluoride tablet (gum + sensitivity)** | **Strong: parodontax double-digit, UK sensitivity about 45%** | **Low** | High | **Medium-high: formulation know-how plus sensory parity** | **Lead** |
| nHAp paste or tablet, fluoride-free | Strong in the US | Low (≤10%) | Low, but NAD limits claims | Low; Boka, RiseWell and Bite nHAp already at Target | Possible US backup only |
| Low/no-foam gel | Niche (Fygg, Gallinée, Apagard Non-Foam) | Low | Low if fluoride-free | Low | Line extension |
| Liquid swish-then-brush | No Western evidence | Low | Not a monograph form | Unknown | Too speculative |
| Foam / mousse | No Western brands found | Low | Not a monograph form | Unknown | Only for care homes and aligner wearers |
| Kids' pre-dosed fluoride tablet | Parental anxiety (inferred) | Low | High, and politically exposed | Medium | Later extension, not the launch |

The case for the lead option has three parts. First, the benefit is where Western value growth already is: gum health and sensitivity, the territory of parodontax and Sensodyne that grows 7–9%. The format does not have to create its own demand. Second, a water-free format gives the tablet a technical reason to exist beyond eco. Stannous fluoride, probiotics and enzymes are known to lose stability in water-based formulas. This is the formulation logic in the research notes; it is plausible but has not been checked against a published stability study, and it is the first thing a lab should test. Third, the format can solve the "how much paste" dosing problem and the travel and aligner occasions, and it suits refills that meet the PPWR rules. A fixed dose of stannous fluoride per tablet also sidesteps the bad press of loose powders. The steelman against it is real. Haleon's patent shows the best-placed incumbent is attacking the same sensory gap with the brands consumers already trust. Even so, Haleon has shown no tablet product yet. An entrant that gets to market first with clinical sensory data, and ideally with a defensible patent on a stannous-specific formulation and its packaging, either keeps a niche or becomes the obvious acquisition target.

## Conclusion: the launch call, its risks and what to validate first

The deeper lesson of the research is that toothpaste format innovation has repeatedly failed when it sold packaging, and succeeded only when the format delivered something paste could not: Ayurvedic credibility in India, medicinal identity in China, ingredient leadership in Japan. In the West the same rule now favours a tablet that is better as a therapeutic product, not just greener. A second lesson is less obvious: regulation, not consumer demand, sets the order of entry. The US has the strongest consumer pull but the highest barrier to a new format, and Europe the reverse.

### Recommendation

| Dimension | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Format** | A water-free, compressed chewable tablet that matches paste on foam and mouthfeel (an explicit pass/fail threshold against a reference paste in a blinded sensory test). Sold in a refillable aluminium tin with mono-material refill pouches that meet the PPWR rules, using a desiccant or barrier design, because effervescent tablets need moisture-proof packaging ([US20120175273A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20120175273A1/en)). |
| **Benefit** | Stannous fluoride at about 1,100–1,450 ppm F. Positioned "gum health + sensitivity + cavity protection in one exact dose", with travel and "no tube, no mess" as secondary reasons to buy. A tablet free of sodium lauryl sulphate (SLS) and microplastics from launch, ahead of the October 2027 microplastics deadline. |
| **Target segment** | Adults aged 30–55 who already pay for Sensodyne, parodontax or elmex, plus aligner and orthodontic patients as a high-value occasion reached through dentists. Label for ages 12+ at launch to avoid the child-dose problem. |
| **Market entry order** | (1) Germany, Austria and Switzerland: largest European oral care market (about EUR 2bn), tablets already have shelf space at dm, fluoride is accepted, and elmex shows these consumers buy science. ### Key risks

The biggest risk is a **fast-follower incumbent**. A Sensodyne or parodontax tablet built on Haleon's patent would erase the entrant's head start on taste and foam within about 2–4 years, which is the time the entrant has to build loyal repeat buyers or sell. The second is **price compression at the drugstore**. dm already sells fluoride tablets at about EUR 0.05 per brushing under its own label and through Denttabs, and a retailer that can copy the format cheaply will do so unless the stannous benefit is protected and clearly claimed. Third, **stannous fluoride may not stay stable in a compressed tablet**. Stannous fluoride stains, tastes metallic and is hard to keep available over shelf life, so the core technical premise could fail in the lab. Fourth, **the US route stays unresolved**. If the FDA requires an OMOR, the cost runs to seven figures with a timeline of one to two years or more. That pushes US revenue out and leaves the most dynamic market to fluoride-free nHAp brands. Fifth, **claims enforcement**: gum-health wording that tips into medicinal claims in the EU, or NAD challenges in the US, could take away the benefit the format depends on. Sixth, **sensory rejection**: even Colgate's tablet scored worse on texture and foam, and a single bad first use ends a consumer trial.

### Gaps to validate before investing

These questions remain open and decide whether to invest. On the product side, the formulation and sensory questions must be settled before anything else: stannous fluoride stability and available fluoride over 24 months in the chosen packaging, and a blinded sensory test against parodontax and Sensodyne pastes. On the US side, a written FDA position is needed on whether a chewable tablet can be marketed under M021, together with verified OMUFA fees and M021.70 test costs. The research also did not establish whether *any* gum-health claim is available in the US without a new drug application, since the notes cover only the anticavity monograph. For Canada, the text of the 25 Sept 2026 Oral Health Products Monograph must be checked for permitted dosage forms, fluoride ranges and the DIN-versus-NPN question for stannous fluoride. Commercially, no consumer survey on format preference or willingness to pay for tablets was found for any Western market. There is also no scanner data for tablet sales in Germany, the UK or the US, and no failure data on tablet brands. dm and Rossmann listing terms and their private-label stance on therapeutic tablets are unknown. Finally, the EU fluoride Annex III wording, the status of the nHAp 29.5% amendment, and the UK statutory instrument SI 2026/23 all need checking by regulatory counsel. If stability and sensory parity both fail, the right fallback is not another eco tablet but a low-foam stannous gel in a recyclable tube, which keeps the benefit and drops the format risk.
