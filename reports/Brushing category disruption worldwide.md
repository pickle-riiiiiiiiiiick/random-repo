# Brushing holds its core while challengers take the edges

Worldwide, brushing-related oral care is a mature category worth roughly **USD 35–69bn**, with most vendors near USD 40bn. Its core has barely changed: fluoride paste in a tube, a manual brush, and one dominant supplier. Colgate holds **41.3% of global toothpaste value** and **32.4% of manual brushes**. Volumes are flat or falling, and the incumbents grow mainly through price and premium mix. The disruption is real but sits at the edges. It is happening in five places. Therapeutic premium paste is growing (Haleon Oral Health **+7.9% organic** in 2025). Power brushes are spreading as incumbents push app and sensor features down to cheaper models. Chinese content-commerce brands have pushed Crest and Colgate out of China's top five toothpaste brands and overtaken Philips online in electric brushes. Creator-driven "visible result" products (purple colour correctors, whitening strips, coloured mouthwash) top the TikTok Shop charts. And culturally native formulas lead their home markets: TCM in China, Ayurveda in India, bamboo salt in Korea, enzymes and high-fluoride pastes in Japan. Formats that ask people to change the ritual (tablets, tooth soap, mouthpiece "automatic" brushes, refill dispensers) have stayed niche or failed. The packaging innovation that did scale, the recyclable HDPE tube, is invisible to the consumer. Growth headroom in volume lies where brushing is not yet a twice-daily habit: MENA, rural South Asia and Africa. **WHO counts 3.5bn people with oral disease**, and only **41% of MENA schoolchildren brush twice a day** against 83% in the Americas. This report describes the landscape; it does not recommend a launch.

## Most figures here come from search extracts, and vendor sizes disagree by 2x

Readers should know how the evidence was gathered before weighing any number. The research teams **could not open most source pages**. Sources differ sharply in reliability. Company filings and results releases (Colgate, Haleon, P&G, Philips, Kenvue, Church & Dwight) are the most reliable layer. Syndicated market-research vendors (Grand View, Fortune Business Insights, Mordor, IMARC, GMI, Market Data Forecast and others) are low-to-medium quality. **Their market sizes conflict by a factor of two or more**, mostly because they define the category differently. ## A USD 35–69bn category run by one 41% leader, growing on price and mix

### Toothpaste dominates value while devices and adjuncts grow faster

Vendor estimates of global oral care in 2025 run from **USD 34.8bn** ([Fortune BI](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/oral-care-market-103533)) through **USD 39.9bn** ([Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/oral-care-market)) and **USD 40.3bn** ([market.us](https://market.us/report/oral-care-market/)) to **USD 68.9bn** ([Transparency Market Research](https://www.transparencymarketresearch.com/oral-care-products-market.html)). The vendors also disagree about how big toothpaste is within that total, putting its share anywhere from **27.3%** ([Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/oral-care-market)) to **49.8%** ([Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/oral-care-market)). So "the toothpaste market" ranges from **USD 18.5bn** ([SkyQuest via GII](https://gii.tw/report/sky1646265-toothpaste-market-size-share-growth-analysis-by.html)) to **USD 44.4bn** for a paste-plus-powder definition ([WiseGuy](https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/toothpaste-toothpowder-market)).

A cross-check from Colgate's own numbers favours the lower end. Colgate is a USD 20.4bn company with a 41.3% share. If oral care is a little over 40% of its sales (historically about 42%, but not re-verified here), its toothpaste sales are roughly USD 6–7bn at manufacturer prices, which implies a retail toothpaste market of roughly **USD 18–25bn** in the countries it tracks. That figure is an inference built on unverified assumptions.

Company results tell a different and more reliable growth story than the vendors. In 2025 the fastest growers were therapeutic paste and power brushes. **Haleon Oral Health** (Sensodyne, parodontax) grew **7.9% organically** in 2025 and 7.3% in H1 2026 ([Haleon FY2025](https://www.haleon.com/news/press-releases/financial/2026/2025-full-year-results); [Haleon H1 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001900304/000110465926088450/hln-20260630xex99d1.htm)). **Philips Personal Health**, which includes Sonicare, grew **8% on a comparable basis** ([Philips 2025](https://www.results.philips.com/ar25)). **P&G Oral Care** grew by a low single digit on a richer mix of premium paste and power brushes, with Oral-B power brushes up high single digits and **gaining 1.3 share points** ([P&G FY2025 AR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80424/000119312525191752/d879413dars.pdf)).

The mass leaders lagged. **Colgate's Oral, Personal & Home Care** segment grew just **1.0%** on falling volumes ([Colgate 2025 AR](https://investor.colgatepalmolive.com/static-files/4298e292-4aaf-4b10-8953-aad27c1065d7)). **Kenvue's Essential Health** division, which includes Listerine, fell **0.7% organically**, citing "competitive pressure in North American Oral Care" ([Kenvue 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1944048/000194404826000030/kvue-20251228.htm)). **Church & Dwight** reported that **Waterpik oral care declined** ([C&D 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/313927/000119312526048139/chd-20251231.htm)). The Waterpik and Kenvue results contradict the vendors' 6–7% growth forecasts for water flossers and mouthwash, at least in North America.

### Five multinationals and a ring of national champions

Toothpaste is close to a single-leader market. **Colgate-Palmolive's** global share was **41.3% in FY2025**, against 41.4% in FY2024 ([Colgate FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21665/000002166526000006/cl-20251231.htm)), and its US shares are **33.3% in toothpaste and 41.3% in manual brushes** ([Colgate Q4 2025 remarks](https://investor.colgatepalmolive.com/static-files/243eca3f-093a-45e8-966a-6929c7673653)). Kantar found Colgate in **60.5% of the world's households** in 2019, the only brand above half ([Kantar Worldpanel](https://kantarworldpanel.com/global/News/Colgate-bought-by-over-half-of-the-worlds-households)). Its 2025 toothpaste share rose in Europe, held flat in Asia Pacific and fell in North America, Latin America and Africa/Eurasia ([Colgate FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21665/000002166526000006/cl-20251231.htm)), so the leader is losing ground in its two largest regions. **P&G** (Crest, Oral-B, Blend-a-med) and **Haleon** (Sensodyne, parodontax) form the next tier, though neither discloses a global toothpaste share. **Kenvue** owns Listerine, the mouthwash leader, and is being bought by Kimberly-Clark for about **USD 48.7bn**, with closing expected in H2 2026 but not yet confirmed ([Cosmetics Design Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2025/11/04/kimberly-clark-to-acquire-kenvue-in-487b-deal)). **Church & Dwight** completes the multinational set with Waterpik, TheraBreath and Arm & Hammer.

Power brushes are a duopoly of Oral-B and Philips Sonicare; one low-quality estimate gives them **35% and 28%** of electric-brush value ([ElectroIQ](https://electroiq.com/stats/electric-toothbrush-statistics/)). Around this multinational core sits a ring of national champions that lead large markets on local terms. **Lion** holds about 29% of Japanese oral care value ([Euromonitor](https://www.euromonitor.com/oral-care-in-japan/report)) and **Yunnan Baiyao** about 25% of Chinese toothpaste ([ChemLinked](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/report/keeping-pace-with-the-changing-landscape-and-trends-in-chinas-toothpaste-market)). **LG H&H** and **Aekyung** lead Korea, **Dabur** and **Patanjali** own Indian herbal paste, and Unilever's **Pepsodent** dominates Indonesia.

### Spending is high in the West, but the price growth comes from premium products

Germany is the best-documented single market. Its oral care category reached **EUR 2.113bn in 2025, up 6.5%** ([IKW](https://www.ikw.org/fileadmin/IKW_Dateien/IKW_Bilder/Presse/Wirtschaftspressekonferenz_2025/04_Marktzahlen_PM_IKW_2025.pdf)), or about **EUR 25 per person**. China's toothpaste market is about **CNY 36.8bn** on one vendor's estimate ([ChemLinked](https://market.chemlinked.com/report/keeping-pace-with-the-changing-landscape-and-trends-in-chinas-toothpaste-market)), roughly **USD 3.6 per person**. Both per-person figures are our calculations from vendor or panel totals.

North America is the largest region for devices. It takes about **34–37%** of power-brush and water-flosser value on vendor estimates ([Fortune BI](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/electric-toothbrush-market-106355); [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/water-flosser-market)).

No panel source splits the category into mass, premium and therapeutic tiers. Two vendor proxies point the same way: sensitivity products are about **44.8% of UK toothpaste value** ([MDF UK](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/united-kingdom-toothpaste-market)), and "therapeutic" paste is about **43% of China's market** ([MRFR](https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/reports/china-toothpaste-market-46332)).

The arithmetic is simple. Western value grows 3–6% a year while volumes are flat, so price per tube rises at about the same rate. Premium therapeutic sub-brands carry that rise: Sensodyne Clinical, parodontax, Optic White Pro and elmex, which Colgate says is "driving record toothpaste market shares in Europe" ([Colgate 2025 AR](https://investor.colgatepalmolive.com/static-files/4298e292-4aaf-4b10-8953-aad27c1065d7)).

### Twice-daily brushing is a rich-country norm, not a global one

The WHO counts **3.5bn people (45%) with oral disease**, including about **2bn** with caries of permanent teeth, **514m** children with caries of primary teeth and about **1bn** with severe periodontal disease; three in four live in low- and middle-income countries ([ICIR/WHO](https://www.icirnigeria.org/who-nearly-3-5bn-people-suffer-from-oral-diseases/)).

Brushing habits split along income lines. A 72-country school study found that **90% of children brush daily but only 71% twice a day**, ranging from **82.9% in the Americas to 41.4% in MENA** ([Dental and Medical Problems 2024](https://dmp.umw.edu.pl/en/article/2024/61/4/495/)). Among adults, **77%** of dentate adults in England brush at least twice a day ([AOHS 2021](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/adult-oral-health-survey-2021/adult-oral-health-survey-2021-report-summary)) and **73.8%** do so in one Japanese prefectural survey ([Okinawa](https://www.pref.okinawa.jp/_res/projects/default_project/_page_/001/006/316/r3kenminnkennkoueiyoutyousashika.pdf)), against roughly **51%** in the US on a garbled YouGov extract ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/51427-how-often-do-americans-brush-a-look-at-oral-care-habits)). India is the largest gap: Kantar data presented by Colgate in August 2026 show **45% of rural Indians still do not brush daily** (down from 55%) and **76% of urban Indians do not brush twice a day** ([Colgate India investor day](https://www.colgateinvestors.co.in/pdf/cpil-investor-day-17aug26-transcript.pdf)).

Habits beyond the toothbrush are thinner. Only **32.7% of US adults floss daily** ([JADA 2024](https://jada.ada.org/article/S0002-8177(24)00188-0/abstract)), whereas Japanese prefectural data show 58.6% using floss or interdental brushes ([Akita](https://www.pref.akita.lg.jp/uploads/public/archive_0000000871_00/240305　【健康づくり推進課】お口の健康診断結果リーフレット.pdf)). About half of adults in England and Germany use a power brush: **59% of English brushers** ([AOHS 2021](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/adult-oral-health-survey-2021/adult-oral-health-survey-2021-report-summary)) and 48% in a manufacturer-funded German poll ([ZWP](https://www.zwp-online.info/zwpnews/dental-news/branchenmeldungen/oral-b-wirft-einen-blick-auf-die-pflegeroutine-der-deutschen)). Brush replacement lags the three-month guidance, which only **64% of US adults** follow ([Delta Dental 2023](https://www.deltadental.com/about-us/press-center/2023-press-releases/delta-dental-report-finds-adults-practice-good-oral-health-care-to-avoid-serious-dental-issues-and-major-expenses/)), and EU residents average just **1.2 dentist consultations a year**, from **0.3 in Romania** to **3.3 in the Netherlands** ([OECD 2024](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/health-at-a-glance-europe-2024_b3704e14-en/full-report/component-60.html)).

These figures show where the growth room is. In poorer markets it lies in how often people brush. In rich markets it lies in adjuncts (power brushes, interdental cleaning, whitening) and in getting people to replace brushes more often.

## Formulation scales inside the tube; formats that change the ritual stay niche

Most formats that change how people brush turn out to be F+P. A tablet must be anhydrous, a foam must work with a pump or aerosol, a liquid must be swishable, a concentrate must be low-dose, and a pre-pasted brush must stay dry or be water-activated.

### What scaled shares four traits, and what stayed niche asked consumers to change

The formulation innovations that scaled have four things in common. They fit the existing tube and ritual; they carry a claim regulators accept (anticaries, sensitivity, gingivitis, or Japan's quasi-drug claims); they come from incumbents that run clinical programmes; and they show a benefit quickly, with claims such as "two minutes", "instant" or "12 hours". Culturally native actives scale for a different reason. They carry meaning: Yunnan Baiyao's TCM gum-bleeding positioning holds about 25% of China, and Dabur's Ayurvedic paste grew **14.3%** year on year in Q2 FY26 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/capital-market-news/dabur-india-q2-pat-jumps-6-yoy-to-rs-453-cr-125103100549_1.html)).

The purple colour corrector is the clearest example of an idea moving from challenger to incumbent. Hismile's influencer-driven V34 serum was followed by Colgate's Max White Purple Reveal, a standard 1,450 ppm fluoride tube selling for **EUR 3.35** that Colgate itself compares with purple shampoo ([CosmeticOBS](https://cosmeticobs.com/en/articles/products-news-73/colgate-max-white-purple-reveal-the-new-toothpaste-with-purple-pigments-for-whiter-teeth-7316)).

Only two packaging innovations reached mass scale, and neither changes how the product is used. The first is the recyclable tube. Colgate moved about **75% of its toothpaste SKUs** to recyclable tubes by end-2024 ([Colgate proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21665/000130817925000223/cl_courtesy-pdf.pdf)), and at EPL, which makes over 9bn tubes a year, sustainable tubes rose from **5% of sales in FY22 to 43% in H1 FY27** ([The Machine Maker](https://themachinemaker.com/?p=113216)). Colgate and Unilever shared or licensed the design rather than defending it, which explains how quickly it spread. The second is the low-price sachet in emerging markets, which solves access rather than sustainability.

Formats that ask the user to chew, dissolve, swish or wet a bar pay a sensory and habit penalty. In Colgate's own tablet trial, plaque control matched paste, but participants rated **texture and foaminess worse** ([LLU](https://dentistry.llu.edu/sites/dentistry.llu.edu/files/2023-08/research-aug-2023.pdf)). German pioneer Denttabs grew to about **EUR 3.5m** in sales, then softened as the zero-waste trend faded ([Lebensmittel Praxis](https://lebensmittelpraxis.de/drogerieartikel/40864-plastikfreie-mundhygiene-bleiben-zahnputztabletten-ein-nischenprodukt.html)). Colgate Wisp took **USD 9.3m** in roughly its first three months of 2009 and then faded ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/?p=106262)); it survives as a steady travel and hotel product, ranked top of its Amazon category at about 20,000 units a month in July 2026 on a third-party estimate ([ASInsight](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/mini-cepillos-de-dientes-desechables)). The formats with durable demand are those that solve a context (no sink, travel, a low ticket price) rather than a conscience.

Foam is the one new-ritual format with measured fast growth right now. foamgle reports Amazon US sales rising about **65% per quarter on average** ([Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026070708272317538)). It publicised its US FDA OTC registration, which shows how far drug-claim status still matters.

Cross-category analogies follow the same "remove the water, then change the pack" logic. No Normal sells coffee concentrate in an aluminium tube, which won an ISPO award in 2024 ([Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2024/06/12/instant-coffee-paste-in-a-tube-is-here)). Colgate sells Softsoap hand-soap tablets at **USD 5.99 for a starter kit** ([Drug Store News](https://drugstorenews.com/colgate-palmolive-intros-softsoap-foaming-hand-soap-tablets)), yet has not carried the tablet-plus-refill model into toothpaste.

### Incumbent pipelines target actives and stability, not new formats

The incumbents' visible R&D aims to make known actives work better inside the existing tube: Colgate's patented "stannous booster" in Total Active Prevention (2025), Haleon's saliva-activated non-aqueous NovaMin base, Lion's PCA root-caries patent (JP 6474375) and enzyme paste ([Lion IP](https://www.lion.co.jp/en/company/governance/ip)), and P&G's long stannous-stabilisation patent family ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10328002)). The one clear incumbent signal on a new format is Haleon's **toothpaste-tablet patent EP4577311**, published July 2025. It is framed explicitly as a fix for the "sensorial problems" of existing tablets, and no matching product has been found ([EPO](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP4577311NWA1/document.html)).

These innovations share a technical root. Some actives are unstable in water, and non-aqueous bases, stannous stabilisation and anhydrous tablets are all answers to that same problem. This is the most direct technical link between formulation innovation and format innovation.

## Power brushes grow by pushing features down; automatic brushes failed

### Incumbents layer sensing and software on proven mechanisms

Power brushes are worth USD 3.5–5.0bn and growing at mid-to-high single digits. Use varies widely by country. LeadLeo, a Chinese consultancy whose estimates are undated and whose methodology is undisclosed, puts penetration at about **82% in South Korea, 76% in the US, 40–60% in Europe, 30% in Japan and 8% in China** ([Yidaiyilu/China Daily](https://eng.yidaiyilu.gov.cn/qwyw/rdxw/245719.htm)). A Mintel figure for the US is much lower: **41%** of adults used an electric brush in the past six months ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/health/toothbrushes/electric-toothbrush-or-manual-a3193343159/)). The likely cause is that the two sources measure different things.

Since 2020 the incumbents have followed one pattern: add sensors and an app at a premium price, then move those features down the range. The **Oral-B iO** debuted at CES 2020 after six years of R&D, with a magnetic drive, a smart pressure sensor and "AI Brushing Recognition" across 16 zones ([EFP/P&G](https://www.efp.org/publications-hub/pg-pearls-on-film-how-ai-uses-cameras-in-the-mouth/)); P&G then launched the cheaper iO2 "to encourage upgrades from manual" ([P&G FY2025 AR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80424/000119312525191752/d879413dars.pdf)), which reached German retail in February 2025. Philips sells the **Sonicare Prestige 9900 with SenseIQ** at **£299.99** ([Philips UK](https://www.philips.co.uk/c-e/pe/electric-toothbrushes/sonicare/prestige-9900)) while launching the next-generation 6000 and 6400 as Walmart exclusives ([roic.ai transcript](https://www.roic.ai/quote/PHG/transcripts/2025-year/3-quarter)); its Personal Health unit grew **14% in Q4 2025** at an 18% margin, though Philips repeatedly cited weakness in China ([Philips Q4 2025](https://www.philips.com/a-w/about/news/archive/corpcomms/news/press/2026/philips-fourth-quarter-results-2025)). Colgate's app-and-rewards **hum** launched at **USD 69.99** in 2020 and has since sold for as little as **USD 21.22** ([Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/17479713-colgate-hum-smart-electric-toothbrush-w-timer-bluetooth-and-travel-case-teal-21-22-free-shipping-w-prime-or-on-35)), discounting deep enough to suggest that smart features alone do not pull consumers. At the bottom of the ladder, Church & Dwight exited **Spinbrush** in 2025 while keeping Waterpik flossers ([C&D AR 2025](https://s203.q4cdn.com/233583214/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/Final-Annual-Report-740f0f.pdf)), a sign that the cheap battery-brush tier is being squeezed between USD 20–50 Chinese rechargeable sonic brushes and incumbents' entry-level rechargeables.

### Chinese challengers won at home and compete abroad on price-for-features

China is where incumbents have actually lost ground.

**Domestic brands took the lead online.** usmile led online electric-brush sales in Jan–Aug 2023 with **28.1%**, against **20.7% for Philips** and 9.7% for Oral-B ([Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/10249712.html)). Its share reportedly rose from 23% to 29% in 2024 as it passed Philips for first place ([36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3469391450641800)).

**Then the category contracted.** Online electric-brush sales fell **6.9% to RMB 5.19bn in 2024** (AVC via [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3469391450641800)). Philips reported a "double-digit decline in China" ([Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/markets/royal-philips-earnings-china-declining-sales/)). usmile also stalled. Its parent was fined **RMB 250,000** over Douyin livestream content that disparaged rival Laifen; reports date the fine to 2024 or September 2025. Its IPO plan was reported to have failed in November 2025 ([Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.cn/tech/2025-12-03/detail-infznzqi0246857.d.html?vt=4)).

**Abroad, Chinese brands play the mid-market on Amazon.** Their pitch is incumbent-premium features (apps, displays, hybrid motion) at USD/EUR 50–100, backed by Western credentials. **Laifen**, best known for hair dryers, launched the **Wave** in the US in 2024; it combines oscillation with sonic vibration, carries the **ADA Seal** and was named one of **TIME's Best Inventions of 2024**, and the Wave Special launched at USD 89.99 before being promoted at €59.99 in Germany ([TechNode/PRNAsia](https://technode.global/prnasia/laifen-expands-award-winning-electric-toothbrush-line-with-launch-of-wave-special); [netzwelt](https://www.netzwelt.de/schnaeppchen/255605-saubere-zaehne-ohne-muehe-elektrische-zahnbuerste-speichert-vorlieben.html)). **Oclean** reportedly earns about 90% of its revenue overseas, though that figure comes from an AI-generated profile ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/oclean)). In Germany, a 2026 market report says Xiaomi and Soocas are "beginning to challenge mid-market price points" against Oral-B, to which it credits an unverified 60%+ of the market ([SourceReady](https://www.sourceready.com/report/detail/germany-personal-care-devices-market-report-2026)). P&G's reported share gain suggests the challengers have so far taken share from other brands and the lower tiers, not from Oral-B in the West.

### DTC brush startups moved to retail, and mouthpieces failed on efficacy

A wave of direct-to-consumer (DTC) brush startups from 2015 to 2022 combined design-led sonic brushes with head-replacement subscriptions, and the survivors scaled through retail or professionals. **quip** raised USD 100m in 2021 and reported 10,000+ US stores ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210831005179/en/quip-Raises-100-Million-in-Funding-to-Redefine-Oral-Healthcare-Globally)); **Burst** built a network of 35,000+ dental-professional ambassadors ([Dentistry Today](https://dentistrytoday.com/?p=109909)); and **SURI**, which sells a repairable brush, reports sales of **£1.7m (2022), more than £10m (2023) and £23.4m (2024)**, all company-reported ([FEBE](https://www.febe.com/?p=34156); [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2134644)). Others failed in public: **Bruush** was suspended from Nasdaq in June 2024 and served a delisting notice in May 2025 ([Nasdaq](https://ir.nasdaq.com/node/109041)).

"Automatic" mouthpiece brushes produced the category's clearest failures. **Amabrush** (Austria) crowdfunded millions of euros for a "10-second toothbrush", went insolvent in June 2019 leaving about 21,000 crowdfunders with a total loss, and became the subject of a fraud investigation ([futurezone](https://futurezone.at/start-ups/das-ende-der-10-sekunden-zahnbuerste-amabrush-wird-geschlossen/400537048)). **Y-Brush** (France) entered judicial reorganisation in July 2025 and was bought by Biotech Dental that October ([Le Journal des Entreprises](https://www.lejournaldesentreprises.com/breve/la-y-brush-se-place-en-redressement-judiciaire-2124637); [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/y-brush-acquired-by-biotech-dental-6m-raised)). What activity remains is concentrated in children: Willo charges USD 199 plus USD 13 a month for its brushing robot ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/13/willo-launches-its-tooth-brushing-robot-for-kids/amp/)), and Playbrush sold 300,000+ gamified attachments before Sunstar bought it in 2021 ([UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/enterprise/case-studies/2024/jul/playbrush-ucl-graduates-revolutionise-toothbrushing-children)).

### The clinical evidence supports power over manual, but not the extras

The evidence for power brushes is modest but consistent. The 2014 Cochrane review found they reduce plaque by **11% at 1–3 months and 21% beyond 3 months**, and gingivitis by **6% and 11%**. It rated the evidence moderate quality and said the clinical importance was "unclear" ([Cochrane](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD002281.pub3)). An 11-year German cohort study linked power-brush use to about **one-fifth fewer teeth lost** ([Greifswald](https://www.medizin.uni-greifswald.de/fileadmin/user_extern_upload/Presse/2019/PI_39_2019_UMG.pdf)).

The evidence on mouthpieces points the other way. In an independent randomised trial, the Amabrush mouthpiece removed **11.4% of plaque against 31.4% for unguided manual brushing** ([Clin Oral Investig 2020](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00784-020-03359-5)). Positive results for U-shaped devices come from manufacturer-funded studies.

No independent evidence was found that app coaching or position tracking improves outcomes. Engagement with those features also looks weak. One survey of a self-selected review-site audience found **69% stop using smart features after three months** ([Electric Teeth](https://www.electricteeth.com/best-electric-toothbrush/)); this is a weak source.

### Water flossers and interdental brushes are smaller, flatter pools

**Water flossers** are a USD 1.0–1.15bn market. Waterpik leads but declined in 2025, and its owner Church & Dwight moved US flosser sourcing out of China because of tariffs ([C&D AR 2025](https://s203.q4cdn.com/233583214/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/Final-Annual-Report-740f0f.pdf)). Cordless Chinese irrigators (Oclean, Bitvae, Nicwell and others) are now named competitors in North America ([HTF MI](https://www.htfmarketintelligence.com/report/north-america-rechargeable-ultrasonic-oral-irrigator-market)).

**Interdental brushes** are a high-volume, low-value business: about **651m units at about USD 0.40 each**. Europe accounts for about 35%, and the top five makers (including TePe, GUM/Sunstar and Trisa) hold about 40% ([QYResearch via DRI](https://www.dri.co.jp/auto/report/qyr/251015-interdental-brush-global-market-share-and.html)). Hygienists' recommendations act as a moat for these makers.

**Sustainability claims about brushes are poorly sourced.** The widely repeated "1 billion toothbrushes discarded in the US each year" appears to be a back-of-envelope calculation (about 300m people × 3–4 brushes a year), not a measurement ([Sapienza IRIS](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/1480769)).

## Creators, dentists and retail velocity decide who scales

### DTC proves demand; retail delivers scale and exits

No oral-care subscription brand reached scale on DTC alone. **quip** shows the hybrid model most clearly. It sold USD 25 starter kits in about 1,800 Target stores while keeping refills on its own site ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/startups/why-subscription-toothbrush-brand-quip-is-expanding-into-walmart/)). When it moved into 3,100 Walmart stores in 2020, it priced refill heads lower online than in store. One industry executive summed up the model: customers discover the brand at Walmart, "but the relationship continues via the subscription channel." quip's later funding shrank from a **USD 100m Series B in 2021 to about USD 16m in 2024** ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/107722-81)).

The two large exits went to brands with retail velocity and a defensible benefit, not to subscription businesses. **Hello** sold to Colgate for a reported **USD 351m** in 2020 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202304/kevin-j-ryan/how-avoiding-an-exit-strategy-led-hello-products-to-a-351-million-sale.html)), and **TheraBreath** sold to Church & Dwight for **USD 580m** in 2021 ([C&D 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/313927/000156459022005528/chd-10k_20211231.htm)), after which its distribution doubled and its US mouthwash share rose from about 13% to a reported **25.3%** ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/church-dwight-q2-earnings-call-highlights)). That is the endpoint of the DTC story: an incumbent's distribution multiplying a proven benefit. **900.care** is the only refill subscription with a reported scale: more than **EUR 10m revenue and about 235,000 subscriptions** in 2023, company-reported ([Maddyness](https://www.maddyness.com/2024/03/26/900-care-leve-21-millions-deuros-pour-atteindre-100-millions-deuros-de-revenus-dici-trois-ans/)). No source discloses churn, customer-acquisition cost or lifetime value for any oral-care subscription.

### Social commerce rewards products with a visible before-and-after

Creator commerce favours products whose result can be filmed: whitening strips, purple colour correctors and coloured mouthwash. Fluoride paste and gum care, which have no before-and-after shot, do not appear in the TikTok Shop top lists. **DR.DENT Purple Whitening Strips** logged **488,565 orders on US TikTok Shop in Q1 2026**, the most of any top-10 product ([FastMoss](https://www.fastmoss.com/blog/top-selling-products-tiktok-shop-us-q1-2026/)), and ranked second by revenue in the UK for Jan–May 2026 at about £2.37m on a third-party estimate ([FastMoss UK](https://www.fastmoss.com/blog/best-selling-tiktok-shop-products-uk-2026/)). **Hismile**, bootstrapped on influencers, reports revenue "north of USD 150 million, profitably" ([SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/opinion/20000-to-150-million-financial-teardown-hismile-viral-playbook/)); teardowns link a later revenue dip to cuts in paid social, which suggests much of its volume was bought rather than organic. Its food-brand collaborations (KFC, Lucky Charms, Reese's, Violet Crumble) work as a content calendar ([License Global](https://www.licenseglobal.com/food-beverages/hismile-launches-lucky-charms-flavored-toothpaste)), and in June 2026 Australia's competition regulator fined it about **A$138,600** after staff posed as "random shoppers" in videos ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hismile)), a reminder that staged user content carries regulatory risk.

China runs the same playbook at larger scale: brands seed key opinion leaders (KOLs) through Juliang Xingtu, spread through thousands of smaller key opinion consumers (KOCs), then amplify with paid Qianchuan traffic. **Canban (参半)** started in mouthwash to avoid the crowded toothpaste market and reached **RMB 2.5bn of revenue in 2025 (+82.5%) at a 71.9% gross margin**, filing for a Hong Kong IPO in March 2026 ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/xiaokuo-files-for-hong-kong-ipo-as-it-expands-beyond-oral-care)). Its agency case study cites more than ¥2bn of 2024 "sales", which, set against the filing's +82.5% revenue growth, is probably retail value rather than revenue. **BOP** built a No.1 sub-category from more than 100m single-use mouthwash sticks ([CBNData](https://www.cbndata.com/information/198749)).

### Beauty shelves and dentist endorsement are the two premium routes

The "oral beauty" or "skinification" move put oral care on beauty shelves from about 2019, through Kendall Jenner's **Moon** at Ulta, **Marvis** at Sephora.com and Neiman Marcus, and **Spotlight** and **Cocolab** at Ulta and Boots. Colgate answered with **CO. by Colgate**, an iridescent Gen-Z sub-brand launched in March 2021 exclusively at Ulta ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/colgate-taps-into-the-oral-beauty-trend-with-new-sub-brand-for-gen-zers/)), choosing a separate brand and channel rather than its masterbrand. Ulta is now expanding oral care partly to capture shoppers displaced by **US drugstore closures** ([Glossy 2025](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/how-ulta-beauty-plans-to-win-wellness-in-2025/)). Beauty positioning has a weak flank. A Belgian consumer group found that TikTok-famous Marvis Classic Strong Mint contained far less available fluoride than its label stated ([Brussels Times](https://brusselstimes.com/belgium/2064290/belgians-advised-against-using-tiktok-famous-toothpaste/)).

The professional channel is where incumbent growth is strongest. Haleon says expert recommendation is the **No.1 trial motivator for 40% of new buyers** in the US ([Haleon CMD 2025](https://www.haleon.com/content/dam/haleon/corporate/documents/investors/cmd-2025/Consistently-growing-our-US-oral-health-business-breakout.pdf)). It defended its "No.1 dentist recommended" claim against a Colgate challenge in South Africa ([News24](https://www.news24.com/business/companies/sa-toothpaste-fight-sensodyne-1-colgate-0-20251122-0454)).

Challengers borrow dentist authority as provenance instead: Spotlight's **Made by Dentists** raised about EUR 3.5m from **about 450 Invisalign specialists** and entered 1,800 Target stores ([BeautyMatter](https://beautymatter.com/articles/spotlight-oral-care-secures-new-funding)), and **Cocolab** works with about 8,000 dentists ([Amazon seller story](https://sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com/the-sisters-turning-their-infectious-love-of-oral-hygiene-and-design-into-a-battle-against-gum-disease)). The **ADA Seal** requires fluoride for anticavity claims ([ADA](https://ada.org/Seal)). That closes the professional route to the fluoride-free segment.

### Culture, politics and price-pack architecture move share

Cultural and political positioning has produced some of the largest share shifts. In India, Patanjali doubled its share to 7.4% in the single year to March 2017 ([The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/business/patanjali-toothpaste-market-share-doubles)), Dabur Red became a ₹1,000-crore brand ([Dabur](https://www.dabur.com/digital-annual-report-2022-23/corporate/our-brands.html)), and during the 2025 India–US trade tensions Dabur ran "swadeshi" (Indian-made) advertising against Colgate ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/dabur-challenges-colgate-as-india-us-trade-tensions-hit-toothpaste-ads-125090500767_1.html)). In the US, fluoride politics pushed fluoride-free sales up "about 16% a month" against 2.4% for fluoride paste through April 2025, per NielsenIQ data reported by Bloomberg ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/rfk-jr-s-fluoride-criticism-spurs-alternative-toothpaste-sales)). That demand is marketed through podcasts and influencers, the mirror image of the Sensodyne dentist model. Kids' marketing runs on licensed characters tied to apps. Oral-B's Disney Magic Timer unlocks 23 Disney, Marvel and Star Wars characters when children scan a product ([Oral-B](https://oralb.com/en-us/disney-magic-timer/)).

In emerging markets, the opening price point is the main marketing weapon. Colgate's CEO blamed a mis-set **Rs 10 price-pack architecture** for weak India volumes in 2025 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/colgate-ceo-noel-wallace-not-pleased-with-india-sales-slump-eyes-fix-at-entry-price-point-78132.htm)).

## Habit, protection and price drive the basket; visible results drive trial

No representative multi-country ranking of purchase drivers is publicly available; Mintel, Kantar and NielsenIQ tables are paywalled. The available studies nonetheless point in a consistent direction.

**Protection is the baseline need in developed markets.** In the US, **64%** of consumers call cavity prevention an important factor when choosing toothpaste ([Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/us-oral-health-market-report-2024)). Whitening, sensitivity and "natural" claims then split the market into segments on top of that need.

**Habit and previous experience rank in the top two wherever they are measured.** Nigerian dental patients cite previous experience (**69.3%**) ahead of fluoride (62.4%) and dentist advice (55%) ([Makara J Health Res, cited studies](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol23/iss3/3/)). Habit coexists with promotion. Historically, **37–40%+ of US and UK toothpaste was sold on deal** ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/freshening-a-stagnant-sector/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339538350_The_US_Toothpaste_Market_A_Competitive_Profile)). The resulting pattern is "habitual but deal-responsive": shoppers rotate among a small repertoire of familiar brands rather than staying loyal to one.

**Price and availability lead in lower-income markets.** Cost tops the list in Ghana at **39.3%**, ahead of taste at 34% ([Kintampo study](https://www.academia.edu/98902283/THE_MAJOR_FACTORS_INFLUENCING_CONSUMER_BUYING_BEHAVIOUR_OF_TOOTHPASTE_IN_THE_KINTAMPO_NORTH_MUNICIPALITY)); Moroccan students cite brand (55%) and family choice (48%) ([Makara J Health Res](https://scholarhub.ui.ac.id/mjhr/vol26/iss2/8/)); and Nigerian households are rationing paste and returning to chewing sticks as the cost of living rises ([Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2024/10/high-cost-of-toothpaste-nigerians-return-to-chewing-stick/)). **Dentist influence depends heavily on who is asked**: it reaches 55% in a clinic sample but only 6% in a general-population sample, so its main effect is probably indirect, through on-pack claims.

**Symptom-based needs are large enough to support whole segments.** Dentine sensitivity averages **32%** across 39 studies ([PubMed 40462051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40462051/)), and a Haleon-funded European study puts it above half of adults ([Haleon](https://www.haleon.com/news/press-releases/brand/2023/new-study-reveals-now-1-in-2-adults-are-sufferung-the-pain-of-tooth-sensitivity)). Halitosis affects **31.8%** ([PubMed 28676903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28676903/)) and dry mouth **22%** ([Braz Dent J](https://www.scielo.br/j/bdj/a/zyjgvkJkWxkVXRs6wvrwGXg/)), while nearly **60%** of Chinese consumers report tooth discolouration ([Mintel China](https://store.mintel.com/report/china-oral-care-market-report)). Because these needs are tied to something the consumer feels, they plausibly explain why sensitivity and herbal segments show stickier loyalty than generic cavity protection.

**Fluoride doubts are a minority behaviour, but fluoride knowledge is weak.** About **5–15%** use non-fluoride paste, while about **20% do not know** whether their paste contains fluoride ([PMC11417910](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11417910/); [Maldupa 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/idh.12827)). That knowledge gap leaves room for "free-from" messaging.

**Younger consumers lean towards aesthetics.** Around 2020, **42% of UK under-35s had whitened their teeth** ([Dentistry.co.uk](https://www.dentistry.co.uk/2020/06/09/millennials-spend-national-average-treatment/)); **40% of US Gen Z** struggle to tell dental myths from facts online ([Oral Health Group](https://www.oralhealthgroup.com/clinical/dental-research/online-dental-misinformation-induces-fear-in-gen-z-and-millennials-u-s-endodontics-survey-finds-1003987184/)); and Sensodyne Clinical White's **130 bps penetration gain** came mainly from younger buyers ([Haleon FY2025](https://www.haleon.com/content/dam/haleon/corporate/documents/investors/results/full-year-results-2025/fy25-statement.pdf.downloadasset.pdf)), which shows incumbents can reach this group when they pair a clinical claim with a cosmetic one.

**Gender shapes how often people brush more than what they buy.** In India, **68% of men aged 35–44 brush only once a day**, against 52% of women ([Mintel](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/6-in-10-indians-use-toothpaste-once-daily-untapped-oral-care-innovations/)).

**Night brushing is the weaker habit.** Qualitative work finds that morning brushing is triggered by routine visual cues, while night brushing depends on felt urges ([Liverpool](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3117346/)). This matches brand-commissioned data showing a third of children skipping bedtime brushing ([DrBicuspid](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-hygiene/article/15375624/third-of-kids-in-britain-dont-brush-at-bedtime)).

**Discovery happens on the shelf in the West and online in China.** Hypermarkets and supermarkets carry an estimated **42%** of global oral care on a vendor estimate ([Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/global-oral-care-products-market-industry)), which keeps in-store promotion and on-pack claims central in the US and Europe. In China, e-commerce carries an estimated **40–60%** of oral care value ([Daxue](https://daxueconsulting.com/oral-care-market-in-china/)), and Douyin alone is about **44% of online toothpaste** ([ce.cn](http://www.ce.cn/cysc/zljd/gd/202411/06/t20241106_39194234.shtml)). **Smart features attract interest more than payment.** In one survey, **87%** of the public were willing to use a smart brush, but only **39%** would pay for professional feedback ([PubMed 41050618](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41050618/)).

**China** is the market where incumbents have lost most. Crest and Colgate were in the offline top five in 2021; in 2025 the retail-value top five were Yunnan Baiyao, Haolai/Darlie (Colgate's 50% joint venture), Canban, Saky and Lengsuanling ([21财经](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260331/herald/2f75d1e26e1961d20053c9f554d23932.html)). Online channels make up nearly 40% of toothpaste sales, and Douyin plus Tmall hold **65.9% of online sales** ([ce.cn](http://www.ce.cn/cysc/zljd/gd/202411/06/t20241106_39194234.shtml)). The disruption is not a new format but content-commerce premium paste sold alongside TCM functional incumbents, and the champions are starting to travel: Yunnan Baiyao has begun making toothpaste in Thailand, its first production outside mainland China ([FilingReader](https://filingreader.com/news-wire/shenzhen/2025-09-18/yunnan-baiyao-group-reports-strong-h1-2025-earnings-sets-new-record)).

**Japan** is a mature market led by Lion, which holds about 29% of oral care value in 2025, up from 25% in 2020 ([Euromonitor](https://www.euromonitor.com/oral-care-in-japan/report)). With an ageing, shrinking population, value growth comes from premium quasi-drug claims rather than volume: Lion's dextranase enzyme paste and its highest-ever TXA/IPMP gum-care concentration ([Towards Healthcare](https://www.towardshealthcare.com/companies/japan-oral-care-companies)), and 1,450 ppm fluoride pastes, including root-caries gels for elders, made possible by the 2017 labelling rules. Japan is also the only market where liquid dentifrice (Kao) and nHAp (Apagard) are long-standing mainstream options.

**Korea** stands out for ingredient-story pastes. LG H&H said bamboo salt took **more than 25%** of the market in an undated year ([JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/lifestyle/the-whole-tooth-behind-salty-brushing/11418531)). Premium sub-brands include Himalaya Pink Salt and Propolithera ([Happi](https://www.happi.com/top-company-profile/lg-household-healthcare/?cyear=2026)). Korea's **USD 11.43bn of cosmetics exports** in 2025 do not separate toothpaste ([Personal Care Magazine](https://www.personalcaremagazine.com/story/50378/korean-cosmetics-exports-hit-11-billion-record-high-in-2025)). foamgle is the visible oral-care export.

**India** shows how positioning, not format, moves share. Colgate fell from about **57% in FY15** to **42.6%** of a Rs 16,700 crore market ([HDFC Securities](https://www.hdfcsec.com/hsl.research.pdf/Colgate-Palmolive%20(India)%20Ltd%20-Initiating%20Coverage-HSLRR131221-.pdf); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/are-indians-turning-away-from-colgate-sales-of-the-iconic-toothpaste-brand-plunge-heres-why)) as Ayurvedic brands took share; Patanjali's dental line alone earned **Rs 332 crore in one quarter** ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/business/patanjali-foods-q1fy26-revenue-jumps-24-nears-rs-8900-cr-1503471569.html)). Premium paste rose from **14.8% to 18.6%** of toothpaste, yet traditional trade still carries **80%** of sales and e-commerce only 6% (of which quick commerce is about 40%), and rural per-capita consumption is about **half the Philippines' level** ([Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/colgate-investor-day-98467); [Colgate India investor day](https://www.colgateinvestors.co.in/pdf/cpil-investor-day-17aug26-transcript.pdf)). Every player now names frequency, especially night brushing, and premiumisation as the growth levers, since penetration is already near-universal.

**Southeast Asia** is multinational-led, but the leader differs by country. In Indonesia, **76%** of survey respondents use Pepsodent ([Katadata](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/consumer-durables-apparel/statistics/6f30c4029f59467/pepsodent-indonesias-most-popular-toothpaste-brand)), and mandatory halal certification for cosmetics, toothpaste included, takes effect on **17 October 2026** ([Halal Foundation](https://halalfoundation.org/indonesia-halal-certification-news/)). Colgate holds about 50% of the Philippines ([SeasiaStats](https://seasiastats.com/statistic/10-leading-toothpaste-brands-across-selected-asian-markets-dZo8p)). In Malaysia, Darlie relaunched its flagship as a 2-in-1 mouthwash toothpaste ([Campaign Brief Asia](https://campaignbriefasia.com/?p=169882)), and in Thailand Marvis credits its packaging for a decade of growth ([Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/business/2025/05/07/italian-premium-toothpaste-brand-marvis-celebrates-10-years-in-thailand/)).

**The Middle East** has no local brand with meaningful share. Differentiation comes from ingredients and identity layered onto multinational distribution: miswak pastes (Dabur Meswak, Colgate's Turkey-only Misvak, Eyüp Sabri Tuncer), halal claims, and siwak sticks whose demand surges during Ramadan ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-toothpaste-market)). Saudi toothpaste is only about **USD 194m** (vendor) ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251024019654/en/Saudi-Arabia-Toothpaste-Market-Report-2025-Regional-Insights-Competition-Forecast-Opportunities-2020-2030F---ResearchAndMarkets.com)). The region's headroom is behavioural: Egypt, Yemen and Morocco each have more than 10% of schoolchildren who rarely or never brush.

**Latin America** remains Colgate's stronghold. Its regional sales were **USD 4,776m**, up 3.9% organically, even as toothpaste share slipped ([Colgate 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21665/000002166526000006/cl-20251231.htm)). Brazil's notable 2025 move was nostalgic: a Kolynos-styled limited edition of Colgate's value brand Sorriso ([Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/marketing/sorriso-resgata-a-marca-kolynos-em-edicao-limitada)).

**Africa** is disrupted in reverse: affordability pushes consumers down-market. In Nigeria, shoppers moved to Oral-B and then back to Close-Up after a price rise ([Marketing Edge](https://marketingedge.com.ng/battle-of-toothpaste-brands-in-nigerian-market/)), and some returned to chewing sticks. In Kenya, Colgate held about 59% of usage in 2017 ([GeoPoll](https://www.geopoll.com/blog/colgage-dominates-kenya-oral-health-care-market/)).

**Europe** combines high power-brush use with record value growth driven by therapeutic premium paste. It is also the home of eco formats, all still small: Denttabs, Smyle (about EUR 3m revenue) ([Smyle](https://wesmyle.com/pages/sharefund)), 900.care and SURI's refill system. Regulation shapes which actives can travel: the EU's 10% cap on nHAp in toothpaste has been fully enforceable since November 2025 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/858/oj)).

**North America** is the most politically charged market. It combines fluoride-free momentum, TikTok Shop whitening, beauty-retail expansion and Chinese brushes on Amazon. It is also where Colgate, Kenvue and Waterpik all reported weakness in 2025.

Among consumables, **900.care** raised €31m ([Maddyness](https://www.maddyness.com/2024/03/26/900-care-leve-21-millions-deuros-pour-atteindre-100-millions-deuros-de-revenus-dici-trois-ans/)), **Spotlight** about €15.5m including €3.5m from ~450 Invisalign dentists, and **Canban** grew with ByteDance backing to a Hong Kong IPO filing in 2026; the tablet brands crowdfunded small sums (Smyle ~€1.4m, Georganics £0.38m), and **Bite** and **Hismile** were bootstrapped.

## Conclusion

The category's stability is the main finding. Incumbents dominate wherever their trust advantage matters: clinical actives, professional endorsement, regulated claims and mass distribution. Challengers win where those advantages count for little. The first such place is products that sell on a visible effect, where a filmed before-and-after replaces the dentist. Purple correctors, whitening strips and coloured mouthwash are the examples. The second is channels where incumbents are structurally weak: Douyin in China, TikTok Shop and Amazon in the West, and beauty retail. The third is meaning that multinationals cannot easily borrow: Ayurveda, TCM, "swadeshi" national pride, and in the US the politics of fluoride. Western sustainability formats, by contrast, never found a benefit that paste or a recyclable tube could not match.

Two less obvious points follow. First, the "disruptor" label is unstable. usmile overtook Philips and then stalled as China's electric-brush market shrank. Hismile's growth partly depended on paid traffic. quip's funding contracted. The durable winners so far are brands that turned social or cultural pull into distribution and a defensible benefit: TheraBreath, Yunnan Baiyao, Dabur and Canban. Second, the biggest unmet need is behavioural, not technological. Night brushing, interdental cleaning and brush replacement lag everywhere. Twice-daily brushing is still a minority habit in parts of MENA, India and Africa. Devices that promise to automate brushing have failed clinically. The most important open questions are therefore how to change habits and how cheaply oral care can be sold. A more advanced gadget does not answer either of them.
